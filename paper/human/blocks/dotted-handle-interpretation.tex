\subsection{Why a dotted handle gives the tensor-unit projector}
\label{subsec:dotted-handle-interpretation}

The dotted-handle operation belongs already to the untwisted premodular
evaluator and is not altered by the homological character decoration.  We
spell it out because the identity
\(\eta_{\mathbf1}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbf1}\) is what later allows the
same projector to absorb the new character coupons.

\begin{lemma}[A cross-section of a dotted handle]
\label{lem:dotted-handle-sections}
Let \(\mathcal H\) be a one-ended Kirby presentation and let \(\delta\) be a
dotted component of \(D_{\mathcal H}\).  Choose standard pair-of-balls
coordinates on the corresponding \(1\)-handle.  A cooriented cross-section
\(\Delta_\delta\), transverse to the framed attaching link, meets it in a
finite ordered list \(p_1,\ldots,p_r\).  Each occurrence remembers its framed
component \(k(p_j)\), its oriented strand, and its local intersection sign
\(\epsilon(p_j)\in\{+1,-1\}\).  After a simple-label state is fixed, the
occurrence inherits the label of that already oriented strand.  The signed
incidence numbers
\[
  I_\delta(k):=
  \sum_{j:\,k(p_j)=k}\epsilon(p_j)
\]
recover the cellular boundary map:
\begin{equation}
  \partial_2(e_k)=\sum_\delta I_\delta(k)e_\delta.
  \label{eq:dotted-cellular-boundary}
\end{equation}
Cross-sections for distinct dotted handles may be chosen with disjoint
supports.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the standard pair-of-balls models supplied by the handle decomposition and
perturb the sections to be transverse to the compact framed attaching link.
The usual signed-intersection computation of the cellular attaching map gives
\eqref{eq:dotted-cellular-boundary}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Dotted-handle projector]
\label{prop:dotted-handle-projector}
Let \(\cC\) be a premodular fusion category, let \(\mathcal H\) be a one-ended
Kirby presentation, fix a simple-label state \(\lambda\), and let \(\delta\)
be a dotted component of \(D_{\mathcal H}\).  Let \(B_\delta\) be the
boundary object determined by \(\lambda\) and the ordered cross-section of
\cref{lem:dotted-handle-sections}.  The local morphism inserted by the
evaluator at this cut is
\[
  P_{\mathbf1,B_\delta}.
\]
For several dotted handles, the operations tensor independently because their
handle neighborhoods are disjoint.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The cross-section of \cref{lem:dotted-handle-sections} is exactly the
cut-open tangle used in \cref{def:cut-open-dotted-tangle}; hence its ordered
boundary word realizes the object \(B_\delta\).  The claim is then the
dotted-circle rule in \cref{def:dotted-circle-translation}.  Disjoint handle
neighborhoods give tensor-independent insertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{convention}[Three independent pieces of information]
\label{conv:three-sign-sources}
Fix a twisted categorical input \((\cC,\eta)\), a one-ended Kirby presentation
\(\mathcal H\), a cocycle \(\omega\in Z^2(\mathcal H)\), and a simple-label state
\(\lambda\).
For a ribbon meeting a dotted-handle cross-section, keep the following data
separate.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),ref=\roman*]
  \item The orientation of the ribbon determines whether the oriented label is
  written as \(X\) or \(X^*\).
  \item The cross-section and attaching-circle orientations determine the
  signed incidence in \eqref{eq:dotted-cellular-boundary} and whether a
  boundary occurrence is incoming or outgoing.
  \item The handle cocycle supplies the integer \(\omega_k\) in the character
  coupon \((\eta^{\omega_k})_{\lambda_k}\).
\end{enumerate}
Neither the ribbon label nor the exponent is chosen by the dotted
cross-section.  The cross-section only displays the intrinsic operation and is
not additional input to the manifold invariant.
\end{convention}
