\subsection{Why a dotted handle gives the tensor-unit projector}
\label{subsec:dotted-handle-interpretation}

The only nonstandard part of \eqref{eq:twisted-handle-evaluator} is the rule
for a dotted circle.  We now relate it to the turned-over handle operation of
\cite[Definition~5.11]{Tham2021}.

\begin{lemma}[A cross-section of a dotted handle]
\label{lem:dotted-handle-sections}
For every dotted component \(\delta\), choose standard pair-of-balls
coordinates on the corresponding \(1\)-handle.  A cooriented cross-section
\(\Delta_\delta\), transverse to the framed attaching link, meets it in a
finite ordered list \(p_1,\ldots,p_r\).  Each occurrence remembers its framed
component \(k(p_j)\), its oriented ribbon label, and its local intersection
sign \(\epsilon(p_j)\in\{+1,-1\}\).  The signed incidence numbers
\[
  I_\delta(k):=
  \sum_{j:\,k(p_j)=k}\epsilon(p_j)
\]
recover the cellular boundary map:
\[
  \partial_2(e_k)=\sum_\delta I_\delta(k)e_\delta.
  \tag{4.6}\label{eq:dotted-cellular-boundary}
\]
Cross-sections for distinct dotted handles may be chosen with disjoint
supports.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the standard pair-of-balls models supplied by the handle decomposition and
perturb the sections to be transverse to the compact framed attaching link.
The usual signed-intersection computation of the cellular attaching map gives
\eqref{eq:dotted-cellular-boundary}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Turned-over interpretation]
\label{prop:dotted-section-index-three}
After turning the handle decomposition over, a primal dotted \(1\)-handle is a
dual index-\(3\) handle.  The ordered boundary word on
\(\Delta_\delta\) is the ribbon word transverse to the co-core of that dual
handle.  The index-\(3\) operation of
\cite[Definition~5.11, \(k=3\)]{Tham2021} is therefore precisely
\[
  P_{\mathbf1,B_\delta}.
\]
For several dotted handles, the operations tensor independently because their
handle neighborhoods are disjoint.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the pair-of-balls model \(D^1\times D^3\), a dotted-circle cross-section
records the arcs entering one attaching \(3\)-ball and leaving the other; see
\cite[Section~2.3.2, Figure~1]{BarenzBarrett2018}.  Turning the decomposition
over replaces this handle by a dual index-\(3\) handle.  The same ribbons may
be bunched and made transverse to its co-core, as in
\cite[proof of Proposition~9.4, p.~96]{Tham2021}.  Definition~5.11 kills every
resolved simple through-channel except the tensor unit and is the identity on
the unit channel.  Summing over multiplicities gives exactly the canonical
projector \(P_{\mathbf1,B_\delta}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{convention}[Three independent pieces of information]
\label{conv:three-sign-sources}
For a ribbon meeting a dotted-handle cross-section, keep the following data
separate.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),ref=\roman*]
  \item The orientation of the ribbon determines whether the oriented label is
  written as \(X\) or \(X^*\).
  \item The cross-section and attaching-circle orientations determine the
  signed incidence in \eqref{eq:dotted-cellular-boundary} and whether a
  boundary occurrence is incoming or outgoing.
  \item The handle cocycle supplies the integer \(a_k\) in the character
  coupon \((\eta^{a_k})_{\lambda_k}\).
\end{enumerate}
Neither the ribbon label nor the exponent is chosen by the dotted
cross-section.  The cross-section only displays the intrinsic operation and is
not additional input to the manifold invariant.
\end{convention}

\begin{convention}[Primal and turned-over indices]
\label{conv:primal-turned-over}
Our topological handle names are primal, whereas Tham's evaluator is applied
to the turned-over decomposition.  Thus
\[
  h^{\mathrm{Tham}}_1=h^{\mathrm{pr}}_3,
  \qquad
  h^{\mathrm{Tham}}_2=h^{\mathrm{pr}}_2,
  \qquad
  h^{\mathrm{Tham}}_3=h^{\mathrm{pr}}_1.
\]
This explains both the dotted index-\(3\) operation and the exponent
\(1-h_3(\mathcal H)\) in \eqref{eq:twisted-handle-evaluator}; compare
\cite[Section~4.2]{GompfStipsicz1999} and
\cite[Definition~5.11]{Tham2021}.
\end{convention}
