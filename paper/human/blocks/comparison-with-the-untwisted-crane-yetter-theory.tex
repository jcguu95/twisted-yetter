\subsection{Comparison with the untwisted Crane--Yetter theory}
\label{subsec:untwisted-comparison}

Here ``untwisted'' means that the character is the identity monoidal natural
automorphism.  It does not mean that the ribbon twist of $\cC$ is trivial.

\begin{theorem}[Untwisted comparison]
\label{thm:untwisted-comparison}
Let \(\cC\) be a premodular fusion category and let
$\operatorname{id}$ denote its identity character.  For every
closed connected oriented smooth four-manifold $X$ and every
$\alpha\in H_2(X;\mathbb Z)$,
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\operatorname{id}}(X,\alpha)
  =Z^{\mathrm{sk}}_{\mathrm{CY},\cC}(X)
  =Z_{\mathrm{CY},\cC}(X),
\]
where $Z^{\mathrm{sk}}_{\mathrm{CY},\cC}$ is Tham's closed skein
Crane--Yetter theory and $Z_{\mathrm{CY},\cC}$ is the original state sum.
In particular, the left-hand side is independent of $\alpha$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the identity character every coupon
$\eta_{\lambda_k}^{a_k}$ is an identity, for every marking and every simple
label.  Turn the primal decomposition over.  Under this operation the five
primal handle indices $0,1,2,3,4$ become Tham indices $4,3,2,1,0$,
respectively.  Definition~5.11 of \cite{Tham2021} therefore gives exactly
the following operators:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),ref=\roman*]
  \item every primal $0$-handle becomes an index-$4$ RT closure;
  \item every primal $1$-handle becomes the index-$3$ projection to
  the tensor-unit channel;
  \item every ordinary primal $2$-handle remains an index-$2$ operation and
  is expanded with the Kirby colour;
  \item every primal $3$-handle becomes an index-$1$ operation and
  contributes $\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)^{-1}$; and
  \item every primal $4$-handle becomes an index-$0$ operation and
  contributes $\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)$.
\end{enumerate}
Thus our untwisted composite is Tham's closed skein value for an arbitrary
handle decomposition.  On a one-ended presentation this composite expands as
\(\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)^{1-h_3}\) times the single-\(S^3\) handle sum in
\eqref{eq:twisted-handle-evaluator}.

There are two convention changes to record in that identification.  The
boundary orientation of the turned-over primal $0$-handle in a one-ended
presentation replaces the dotted--framed link $L$ by its mirror
$\overline L$, exactly as in
\cite[Example~9.1, Equation~(9.3)]{Tham2021}.  Tham's graphical convention,
however, is opposite to the convention of
\cite{BakalovKirillov2001}: reflecting a diagram through a horizontal plane
interchanges the two conventions.  Hence evaluation of
$\overline L$ in Tham's convention is evaluation of $L$ in the convention
used here; this is \cite[Remark~9.2]{Tham2021}.  No extra mirror or inverse
twist remains in the scalar.

It remains only to compare the three presentations of Crane--Yetter theory.
Theorem~5.25 of \cite{Tham2021} identifies the PLCW and skein boundary
categories, and Theorem~5.26 proves that this equivalence intertwines their
cobordism maps.  Applied to the closed cobordism $X$, it gives
\[
  Z^{\mathrm{sk}}_{\mathrm{CY},\cC}(X)
  =Z^{\mathrm{PLCW}}_{\mathrm{CY},\cC}(X).
\]
Finally, Theorem~3.61 of \cite{Tham2021} identifies the latter PLCW value
with the original triangulation state sum
$Z_{\mathrm{CY},\cC}(X)$.  Chaining these two equalities proves the theorem.
\end{proof}
