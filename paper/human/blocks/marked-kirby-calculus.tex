\subsection{Kirby calculus for closed oriented marked four-manifolds}
\label{subsec:marked-kirby-calculus}

Suppose that a closed oriented smooth four-manifold \(X\) is equipped with a
class \(\alpha\in H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\), and require every diffeomorphism to
carry this class to the class on its target.  Does the ordinary Kirby
calculus remain complete?  The answer is yes.

\begin{theorem}[Marked Kirby--Cerf completeness]
\label{prop:marked-kirby-cerf}
Let \(f\colon X\to Y\) be an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism of closed,
connected, oriented smooth four-manifolds, and let
\[
  \alpha\in H_2(X;\mathbb Z),
  \qquad
  \alpha'\in H_2(Y;\mathbb Z),
  \qquad
  f_*\alpha=\alpha'.
\]
For any special Kirby presentations \(\mathcal H\) of \(X\) and
\(\mathcal H'\) of \(Y\), the Kirby movie furnished by
\cref{prop:special-handle-movie-completeness} has a unique lift that
transports the marking \(\alpha\).  It ends at the pullback of the marking
\(\alpha'\), and its composite with the final identification by \(f\)
represents the prescribed marked diffeomorphism
\[
  f\colon(X,\alpha)\longrightarrow(Y,\alpha').
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The handle filtration of a special Kirby presentation \(\mathcal K\) has
cellular chain complex \(C_*(\mathcal K)\).  Put
\[
  H^2(\mathcal K)
  :=H^2\!\left(\operatorname{Hom}
    (C_*(\mathcal K),\mathbb Z)\right).
\]
Natural cellular-to-singular comparison gives an isomorphism
\[
  \kappa_{\mathcal K}\colon
  H^2(\mathcal K)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }H^2(X_{\mathcal K};\mathbb Z).
\]
Thus the marking of \(\mathcal H\) is the unique class
\[
  \xi_{\mathcal H}
  :=\kappa_{\mathcal H}^{-1}
    \bigl(\operatorname{PD}_X(\alpha)\bigr)
  \in H^2(\mathcal H).
  \tag{3.2}\label{eq:marked-presentation}
\]
This is the only place where the homology class enters the Kirby
presentation.

Pull \(\mathcal H'\) back along \(f\).  By ordinary Kirby--Cerf
completeness there is a finite movie on the fixed manifold \(X\),
\[
  \mathcal H=\mathcal H_0
  \xrightarrow{m_1}\mathcal H_1
  \longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow
  \mathcal H_N=f^*\mathcal H',
\]
whose realized diffeomorphism is isotopic to
\(\operatorname{id}_X\).  Each standard move
\(m_r\colon\mathcal H_{r-1}\to\mathcal H_r\) carries a relative isotopy
class of local diffeomorphisms
\(\phi_r\colon X_{\mathcal H_{r-1}}\to X_{\mathcal H_r}\).  It therefore
forces the transport
\[
  T_{m_r}:=
  \kappa_{\mathcal H_r}^{-1}
  \circ(\phi_r^{-1})^*
  \circ\kappa_{\mathcal H_{r-1}}.
  \tag{3.3}\label{eq:marking-transport}
\]
Starting with \(\xi_0=\xi_{\mathcal H}\), define recursively
\(\xi_r:=T_{m_r}\xi_{r-1}\).  This gives the unique possible marked lift of
the movie.

If \(\Phi_r=\phi_r\circ\cdots\circ\phi_1\), repeated substitution in
\eqref{eq:marking-transport} gives
\[
  \kappa_{\mathcal H_r}(\xi_r)
  =(\Phi_r^{-1})^*\operatorname{PD}_X(\alpha).
  \tag{3.4}\label{eq:movie-marking-naturality}
\]
At \(r=N\), the map \(\Phi_N\) is isotopic to
\(\operatorname{id}_X\).  Hence
\[
  \kappa_{f^*\mathcal H'}(\xi_N)
  =\operatorname{PD}_X(\alpha),
\]
so \(\xi_N\) is exactly the marking of the pulled-back presentation.

Finally, Poincar\'e duality is natural under orientation-preserving
diffeomorphisms:
\[
  f^*\operatorname{PD}_Y(\alpha')
  =f^*\operatorname{PD}_Y(f_*\alpha)
  =\operatorname{PD}_X(\alpha).
  \tag{3.5}\label{eq:pd-marking-naturality}
\]
Therefore the final identification by \(f\) carries \(\xi_N\) to the
marking of \(\mathcal H'\).  The underlying unmarked composite represents
\(f\) by \cref{prop:special-handle-movie-completeness}; hence the lifted
composite represents the prescribed marked diffeomorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Marked presentations and moves]
\label{def:marked-special-kirby-presentation}
\label{def:handle-homology-marking}
\label{def:marked-kirby-move}
The pair \((\mathcal H,\xi_{\mathcal H})\) in
\eqref{eq:marked-presentation} is the \emph{marked special Kirby
presentation} of \((X,\alpha)\) based on \(\mathcal H\).  A
\emph{handle cocycle} is a cocycle
\[
  a\in\operatorname{Hom}(C_2(\mathcal H),\mathbb Z)
\]
representing \(\xi_{\mathcal H}\).  If \(e_j\) denotes the oriented
\(2\)-handle generator corresponding to the \(j\)th framed component, write
\(a_j:=a(e_j)\).

A \emph{marked Kirby move}
\[
  m\colon(\mathcal H,\xi)\longrightarrow(\mathcal H',\xi')
\]
is an elementary Kirby move, or a \(3/3\) slide, for which
\[
  \xi'=T_m\xi.
  \tag{3.6}\label{eq:marked-move-condition}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Coordinate form of marking transport]
\label{prop:marking-coordinate-transport}
Let \(a\) be a handle cocycle for \((\mathcal H,\xi)\).  For a marked move
\(m\colon(\mathcal H,\xi)\to(\mathcal H',T_m\xi)\), the transported class
has a representative \(a'\) with the following coordinates.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),ref=\roman*]
  \item For framed isotopy, a \(1/1\) slide, a \(2/1\) slide, or a
  \(3/3\) slide, the \(2\)-handle generators are canonically identified and
  \(a'=a\).

  \item For a \(2/2\) slide, let \(e_x\) be the moving generator,
  \(e_y\) the stationary generator, and let
  \(\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}\) be fixed by the oriented handle-slide
  convention.  Then
  \[
    a_x'=a_x+\varepsilon a_y,
    \qquad
    a_y'=a_y,
    \tag{3.7}\label{eq:two-two-marking-transport}
  \]
  while all uninvolved coordinates are unchanged.

  \item A \(1\)--\(2\) birth admits \(a'=a\oplus0\); every representative
  of the transported class is cohomologous to one whose new \(2\)-handle
  coordinate is zero.

  \item For a \(2\)--\(3\) birth, the cocycle equation forces the new
  \(2\)-handle coordinate to be zero, so again \(a'=a\oplus0\).
\end{enumerate}
The death formulas are the inverses of the corresponding birth formulas.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The handle complex is the cellular chain complex of the handle filtration,
and cellular comparison is natural.  It is therefore enough to write the
standard cellular map for each local move
\cite[Sections~4.1--4.2]{DurstGeigesKegel2019}.

Isotopy, \(1/1\), \(2/1\), and \(3/3\) moves act as the identity on
\(C_2\).  For a \(2/2\) slide, the target-to-source basis change sends the
new moving generator to \(e_x+\varepsilon e_y\) and fixes all other
generators.  Precomposing \(a\) with this basis change gives
\[
  a_x'=a(e_x+\varepsilon e_y)=a_x+\varepsilon a_y,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:two-two-marking-transport}.

A \(1\)--\(2\) birth adds the acyclic complex
\[
  0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z
  \xrightarrow{\ \pm1\ }\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0
\]
in degrees \(2\) and \(1\).  The new degree-two cochain is therefore a
coboundary, so its coordinate may be chosen to be zero.  A \(2\)--\(3\)
birth adds the same acyclic complex in degrees \(3\) and \(2\); now the
degree-two cocycle equation itself forces the new coordinate to vanish.
Reversing these chain equivalences gives the death formulas.
\end{proof}
