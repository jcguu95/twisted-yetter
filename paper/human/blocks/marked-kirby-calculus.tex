\subsection{Marked Kirby calculus}
\label{subsec:marked-kirby-calculus}

We first specify the underlying Kirby moves.  Their lifts to marked
presentations will then be defined by the induced maps on handle cohomology.

\begin{definition}[Elementary Kirby move]
\label{def:kirby-move}
Let \(\mathcal H\) and \(\mathcal H'\) be special Kirby presentations.  An
elementary move \(m\colon\mathcal H\to\mathcal H'\) is one of the following
eight directed modifications:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),ref=\roman*]
  \item\label{move:framed-isotopy} framed isotopy;
  \item\label{move:one-over-one} a \(1/1\) slide;
  \item\label{move:two-over-one} a \(2/1\) slide;
  \item\label{move:two-over-two} a \(2/2\) slide;
  \item\label{move:one-two-birth} a \(1\)--\(2\) birth;
  \item\label{move:one-two-death} a \(1\)--\(2\) death;
  \item\label{move:two-three-birth} a \(2\)--\(3\) birth; or
  \item\label{move:two-three-death} a \(2\)--\(3\) death.
\end{enumerate}
For an \(r/s\) slide, \(r\) is the index of the moving handle and \(s\) the
index of the stationary handle.  Outside the supporting ball or
cancelling-pair neighborhood, the handle decomposition is unchanged.
\end{definition}

\begin{convention}[Standard local models]
\label{conv:referenced-kirby-dictionary}
The names in \cref{def:kirby-move} have their standard meanings in Kirby
calculus.  The \(1/1\) and \(2/1\) moves are the dotted-circle moves in
\cite[Figures~5.39 and~5.36]{GompfStipsicz1999}; a \(2/2\) move is an oriented
band sum with a framed push-off of the stationary component
\cite[Section~5.1]{GompfStipsicz1999}; and births insert the isolated standard
cancelling pairs, with deaths as their inverses.  Equivalently, apart from
framed isotopy, these are the five undirected rows of
\cite[Table~2]{BarenzBarrett2018}, with each cancellation separated into its
two directions.  This convention fixes only the topology of the source and
target.  It contains no categorical decoration and asserts no equality of
evaluations.
\end{convention}

\begin{lemma}[Transport of the marking]
\label{lem:handle-cohomology-naturality}
Let \(m\colon\mathcal H\to\mathcal H'\) be an elementary Kirby move
or a slide of one \(3\)-handle over another.  Let \(X_{\mathcal H}\) and
\(X_{\mathcal H'}\) denote the represented manifolds, and write
\(\phi_m\colon X_{\mathcal H}\to X_{\mathcal H'}\) for the
orientation-preserving diffeomorphism realized by the standard local model.
Then \(m\) determines a chain-homotopy equivalence
\[
  F_m\colon C_*(\mathcal H)\longrightarrow C_*(\mathcal H').
\]
If \(\kappa_{\mathcal H}\) and \(\kappa_{\mathcal H'}\) are the cellular
comparison isomorphisms, then
\[
  \kappa_{\mathcal H}\circ F_m^*
  =\phi_m^*\circ\kappa_{\mathcal H'}.
\]
Consequently the source-to-target transport of a marking is
\[
  T_m:=(F_m^*)^{-1}\colon
  H^2(\mathcal H)\longrightarrow H^2(\mathcal H').
  \tag{3.4}\label{eq:marking-transport}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a handle decomposition, the group \(C_k\) is the relative homology
generated by the oriented \(k\)-handles.  We construct \(F_m\) from the
standard local model of \cref{conv:referenced-kirby-dictionary}.

For framed isotopy, and for a \(2/1\) move, the attaching map of the moving
\(2\)-handle varies by isotopy in the boundary of the \(0\)- and
\(1\)-handlebody.  Isotopy extension gives \(\phi_m\), and cellular
continuation gives \(F_m\).  Continuation along the reversed isotopy is a
chain-homotopy inverse.

For a same-index \(k/k\) slide, with \(k=1,2\), or for a \(3/3\) slide, the
new moving handle generator represents the old moving generator plus or
minus the old stationary generator.  Let
\[
  J_m\colon C_*(\mathcal H')\longrightarrow C_*(\mathcal H)
\]
send that new generator to the corresponding signed sum and canonically
identify every other generator.  The cellular boundary matrices after the
slide are exactly the old matrices in this new basis, so
\(J_m\partial'=\partial J_m\).  Thus \(J_m\) is a chain isomorphism; set
\(F_m=J_m^{-1}\).  Its inverse is the chain map associated to the opposite
slide.

For a \(k\)--\((k+1)\) birth, where \(k=1\) or \(2\), the isolated
cancelling pair gives a direct-sum decomposition
\[
  C_*(\mathcal H')\cong C_*(\mathcal H)\oplus A_*,
  \qquad
  A_{k+1}=\mathbb Z u,
  \quad A_k=\mathbb Z v,
  \quad \partial u=\pm v.
\]
Take \(F_m\) to be inclusion of the first summand.  Projection onto that
summand is a homotopy inverse: on \(A_*\), the degree-one map
\(s(v)=\pm u\), \(s(u)=0\), satisfies
\(\partial s+s\partial=\operatorname{id}_{A_*}\).  A death uses the
projection and the same contraction in the reverse direction.

In each case, the map just constructed is induced by the map of filtered
handlebodies realizing \(\phi_m\): continuation for an isotopy, the identity
map written in the slid handle basis, or the elementary expansion/collapse
of a cancelling pair.  Naturality of the cellular-to-singular comparison
therefore gives
\(
  \kappa_{\mathcal H}\circ F_m^*
  =\phi_m^*\circ\kappa_{\mathcal H'}
\).
Since \(F_m\) is a chain-homotopy equivalence, \(F_m^*\) is an isomorphism on
cohomology, so its inverse defines \(T_m\) in the asserted direction.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Marked Kirby move]
\label{def:marked-kirby-move}
Let \((\mathcal H,\xi)\) and \((\mathcal H',\xi')\) be marked special
Kirby presentations.  Using the transport \(T_m\) supplied by
\cref{lem:handle-cohomology-naturality}, a \emph{marked Kirby move}
\[
  m\colon(\mathcal H,\xi)\longrightarrow(\mathcal H',\xi')
\]
is an elementary Kirby move \(m\colon\mathcal H\to\mathcal H'\) satisfying
\[
  \xi'=T_m\xi.
  \tag{3.5}\label{eq:marked-move-condition}
\]
A marked \(3\)-handle slide is defined by the same condition for an
underlying \(3\)-handle slide.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Coordinate form of marking transport]
\label{prop:marking-coordinate-transport}
Let
\[
  m\colon(\mathcal H,\xi)\longrightarrow(\mathcal H',\xi')
\]
be a marked Kirby move, and let \(a\in Z^2(\mathcal H)\) represent \(\xi\).
A cocycle \(a'\in Z^2(\mathcal H')\) representing
\(\xi'=T_m\xi\) may be chosen as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),ref=\roman*]
  \item For framed isotopy, a $1/1$ slide, or a $2/1$ slide, the framed
  $2$-handle generators are canonically identified and $a'=a$.

  \item For a positive or negative $2/2$ slide with moving generator $e_x$
  and stationary generator $e_y$, respectively,
  \[
    e_x'=e_x+e_y,\quad (a_x',a_y')=(a_x+a_y,a_y),
  \]
  or
  \[
    e_x'=e_x-e_y,\quad (a_x',a_y')=(a_x-a_y,a_y).
    \tag{3.6}\label{eq:two-two-marking-transport}
  \]
  Every uninvolved coordinate is unchanged.

  \item A $1$--$2$ birth adjoins an acyclic summand in degrees $1,2$,
  and the transported class is represented by $a'=a\oplus0$.  On the side
  containing the pair, every representative is cohomologous to one whose new
  $2$-handle coordinate is zero.

  \item A $2$--$3$ birth adjoins an acyclic summand in degrees $2,3$.
  The cocycle equation forces the new $2$-handle coordinate to be zero, so
  again $a'=a\oplus0$.
\end{enumerate}
The coordinate rules for deaths are the inverses of the corresponding birth
rules.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the first three moves the cellular continuation map in degree $2$ is,
respectively, the identity, the identity after changing the $C_1$-basis,
and the identity under isotopy of the attaching map.  This gives (i).
For a $2/2$ slide, the cochain $a':=a\circ J_m$ satisfies
$F_m^*a'=a$, so it represents $T_m([a])$.  Evaluating on
$J_m(e_x')=e_x\pm e_y$ gives
$a_x'=a_x\pm a_y$, while $e_y$ and every other generator are fixed.

For a $1$--$2$ birth the added cochain summand is
$\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\,\pm1\,}\mathbb Z$ in degrees $1,2$.
Extension by zero represents the transported class, and subtracting a
coboundary makes the new degree-$2$ coordinate zero.  For a $2$--$3$
birth the same two-term complex lies in degrees $2,3$; hence the degree-$3$
cocycle equation itself forces the new coordinate to vanish.  Reversing the
chain equivalences gives the death statements.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Completeness of special handle movies]
\label{prop:special-handle-movie-completeness}
Let \(\mathcal H_0\) and \(\mathcal H_1\) be special handle decompositions of
the same closed, connected, oriented smooth four-manifold \(X\).  They are
related, while keeping their unique \(0\)- and \(4\)-handles fixed, by a
finite sequence of
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),ref=\roman*]
  \item isotopies of attaching maps within regular levels;
  \item slides between handles of the same index \(1\), \(2\), or \(3\); and
  \item creations or cancellations of \(1\)--\(2\) and \(2\)--\(3\)
  cancelling pairs.
\end{enumerate}
The composite identification of the underlying manifold is isotopic to the
identity of \(X\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose small balls in the interiors of the unique \(0\)- and \(4\)-handles.
After an ambient isotopy, the balls and their boundary collars may be taken to
be the same for the two decompositions.  Removing their interiors turns both
decompositions into relative handle decompositions of the same cobordism
\[
  W\colon S^3\longrightarrow S^3
\]
with handles only of indices \(1\), \(2\), and \(3\).  Their regular levels
are connected: indices \(1\) and \(2\) preserve connectedness, and an
index-\(3\) attachment that disconnected a level could not be repaired by
any later handle before the connected outgoing \(S^3\).

The connected Cerf theorem
\cite[Theorem~3.4]{GayWehrheimWoodward2012} supplies a finite sequence of
Cerf moves between these two relative decompositions.  Its connected-level
condition rules out births of \(0\)--\(1\) and \(3\)--\(4\) pairs.  Keeping
track of attaching maps and a generic pseudo-gradient refines the critical
point switches to isotopies within levels and same-index handle slides; this
is the handle-theoretic form of Cerf completeness recorded in
\cite[Theorem~4.2.12]{GompfStipsicz1999}.  Thus only the moves listed above
occur.  The sequence is obtained from a path of Morse functions on the fixed
cobordism \(W\); the accompanying identifications are generated by ambient
isotopies beginning at the identity and by the standard local
creation/cancellation models.  Hence their composite is isotopic to
\(\operatorname{id}_W\) relative to the boundary.  Extending by the two fixed
balls gives an identification isotopic to \(\operatorname{id}_X\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Dotted-diagram form of a special handle movie]
\label{lem:special-handle-movie-kirby-dictionary}
After choosing dotted--framed diagrams, every movie furnished by
\cref{prop:special-handle-movie-completeness} is represented by a finite
sequence of the eight moves in \cref{def:kirby-move}, together with slides
among \(3\)-handles.  Conversely, each step in that diagrammatic sequence
realizes the corresponding step of the handle movie.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
An index-\(1\) slide is the \(1/1\) dotted-circle move
\cite[Figure~5.39]{GompfStipsicz1999}; an index-\(2\) slide is the framed
band-sum move of \cite[Section~5.1]{GompfStipsicz1999}; and index-\(3\)
slides are retained separately.  An isotopy of a \(2\)-handle attaching
circle in the \(1\)-handlebody is drawn as framed isotopy together with the
\(2/1\) dotted-circle move of
\cite[Figure~5.36]{GompfStipsicz1999}.  Finally, the two allowed adjacent-index
creations and cancellations give the \(1\)--\(2\) and \(2\)--\(3\)
birth/death moves.  These are precisely the local models fixed in
\cref{conv:referenced-kirby-dictionary}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Naturality of a marking along a handle movie]
\label{lem:marking-naturality-along-movie}
Let
\[
  M=(m_1,\ldots,m_N)\colon \mathcal H_0\longrightarrow\mathcal H_N
\]
be a finite movie of the moves above, let
\(\Phi_M\colon X_0\to X_N\) be the orientation-preserving diffeomorphism
realized by the movie, and write
\[
  \kappa_i\colon H^2(\mathcal H_i)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }
  H^2(X_i;\mathbb Z)
\]
for cellular comparison.  If
\(T_M=T_{m_N}\circ\cdots\circ T_{m_1}\), then
\[
  \kappa_N\circ T_M
  =(\Phi_M^{-1})^*\circ\kappa_0.
  \tag{3.7}\label{eq:movie-marking-naturality}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a single move, \eqref{eq:movie-marking-naturality} is exactly the
cellular-to-singular naturality in
\cref{lem:handle-cohomology-naturality}; the inverse appears because
cohomological pullback is contravariant whereas \(T_m\) is directed from
source to target.  The general identity follows by induction on \(N\), using
functoriality of pullback at the induction step.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Marked Kirby--Cerf completeness]
\label{prop:marked-kirby-cerf}
Let \((X,\alpha)\) and \((Y,\alpha')\) be marked four-manifolds,
let \((\mathcal H,\xi_{\mathcal H})\) and
\((\mathcal H',\xi_{\mathcal H'})\) be marked special Kirby presentations
of \((X,\alpha)\) and \((Y,\alpha')\), respectively, and let
\(f\colon X\to Y\) be an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism.  If
\(f_*\alpha=\alpha'\), then the two marked presentations are connected by a
finite sequence of marked lifts of the eight moves in
\cref{def:kirby-move}, together with marked \(3\)-handle slides.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Pull \(\mathcal H'\) back along \(f\).  This gives a special handle
decomposition \(f^*\mathcal H'\) of \(X\), whose final identification with
\(\mathcal H'\) is \(f\).  Apply
\cref{prop:special-handle-movie-completeness} to \(\mathcal H\) and
\(f^*\mathcal H'\), and then apply
\cref{lem:special-handle-movie-kirby-dictionary}.  The resulting diagrammatic
movie connects \(\mathcal H\) to \(\mathcal H'\), uses only the asserted
moves, and realizes \(f\) up to isotopy.

Let \(T_M\) be its composite marking transport.  By
\cref{lem:marking-naturality-along-movie} and naturality of Poincar\'e duality,
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \kappa_{\mathcal H'}(T_M\xi_{\mathcal H})
  &=(f^{-1})^*\kappa_{\mathcal H}(\xi_{\mathcal H}) \\
  &=(f^{-1})^*\operatorname{PD}_X(\alpha) \\
  &=\operatorname{PD}_Y(f_*\alpha) \\
  &=\operatorname{PD}_Y(\alpha') \\
  &=\kappa_{\mathcal H'}(\xi_{\mathcal H'}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(\kappa_{\mathcal H'}\) is an isomorphism,
\(T_M\xi_{\mathcal H}=\xi_{\mathcal H'}\).  Mark every intermediate
presentation by the image of \(\xi_{\mathcal H}\) under the corresponding
partial composite of the maps \(T_m\).  Each step is then a marked Kirby
move (or a marked \(3\)-handle slide), and the final marking is
\(\xi_{\mathcal H'}\), as required.
\end{proof}
