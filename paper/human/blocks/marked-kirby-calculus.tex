\subsection{Marked Kirby calculus}
\label{subsec:marked-kirby-calculus}

The ordinary Kirby moves must now be read together with their induced maps on
the handle complex.  This is the only additional meaning of the adjective
``marked.''

\begin{definition}[Elementary marked Kirby move]
\label{def:kirby-move}
An elementary move \(m\colon\mathcal H\to\mathcal H'\) is one of the following
eight directed modifications:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),ref=\roman*]
  \item\label{move:framed-isotopy} framed isotopy;
  \item\label{move:one-over-one} a \(1/1\) slide;
  \item\label{move:two-over-one} a \(2/1\) slide;
  \item\label{move:two-over-two} a \(2/2\) slide;
  \item\label{move:one-two-birth} a \(1\)--\(2\) birth;
  \item\label{move:one-two-death} a \(1\)--\(2\) death;
  \item\label{move:two-three-birth} a \(2\)--\(3\) birth; or
  \item\label{move:two-three-death} a \(2\)--\(3\) death.
\end{enumerate}
For an \(r/s\) slide, \(r\) is the index of the moving handle and \(s\) the
index of the stationary handle.  Outside the supporting ball or
cancelling-pair neighborhood, the handle decomposition is unchanged.
\end{definition}

\begin{convention}[Standard local models]
\label{conv:referenced-kirby-dictionary}
The names in \cref{def:kirby-move} have their standard meanings in Kirby
calculus.  The \(1/1\) and \(2/1\) moves are the dotted-circle moves in
\cite[Figures~5.39 and~5.36]{GompfStipsicz1999}; a \(2/2\) move is an oriented
band sum with a framed push-off of the stationary component
\cite[Section~5.1]{GompfStipsicz1999}; and births insert the isolated standard
cancelling pairs, with deaths as their inverses.  Equivalently, apart from
framed isotopy, these are the five undirected rows of
\cite[Table~2]{BarenzBarrett2018}, with each cancellation separated into its
two directions.  This convention fixes only the topology of the source and
target.  It contains no categorical decoration and asserts no equality of
evaluations.
\end{convention}

\begin{lemma}[Transport of the marking]
\label{lem:handle-cohomology-naturality}
Every elementary move determines a chain-homotopy equivalence
\[
  F_m\colon C_*(\mathcal H)\longrightarrow C_*(\mathcal H').
\]
Under cellular comparison, \(F_m^*\) agrees with pullback on singular
cohomology.  Consequently the source-to-target transport of a marking is
\[
  T_m:=(F_m^*)^{-1}\colon
  H^2(\mathcal H)\longrightarrow H^2(\mathcal H').
  \tag{3.4}\label{eq:marking-transport}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A same-index slide is an elementary basis change in the corresponding chain
group.  A \(2/1\) move is cellular continuation under an isotopy of a
\(2\)-handle attaching map in the \(1\)-handlebody.  A birth adjoins an
acyclic two-term summand \(\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\,\pm1\,}\mathbb Z\), and a
death removes it.  These maps are precisely the cellular continuation maps of
the two relative CW structures, so cellular-to-singular comparison gives the
claimed naturality.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Three-handle slides]
\label{lem:three-handle-slides}
A slide of one \(3\)-handle over another changes only the chosen basis of
\(C_3(\mathcal H)\).  It preserves both \(h_3(\mathcal H)\) and
\(\operatorname{im}(\partial_3)\), and hence acts trivially on the data used by
the evaluator.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(A\in\operatorname{GL}(C_3(\mathcal H))\) is the elementary basis change,
the new boundary map is \(\partial_3A\), which has the same image as
\(\partial_3\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Marked Kirby--Cerf completeness]
\label{prop:marked-kirby-cerf}
Let \(\mathcal H\) and \(\mathcal H'\) present \(X\) and \(Y\), and let
\(f\colon X\to Y\) be an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism.  If
\(f_*\alpha=\alpha'\), then the two marked presentations are connected by a
finite sequence of the eight moves in \cref{def:kirby-move}, together with
\(3\)-handle slides.  The composite of the maps \(T_m\) carries
\(\xi_{\mathcal H}\) to \(\xi_{\mathcal H'}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Pull the target decomposition back along \(f\).  After fixing neighborhoods of
the unique minimum and maximum, the relative connected Cerf theorem
\cite[Theorem~3.6]{GayWehrheimWoodward2012} gives a finite movie with only
critical-value crossings and births or deaths of adjacent-index pairs.  The
fixed extrema exclude \(0\)--\(1\) and \(3\)--\(4\) pairs.  The relative Kirby
theorem \cite[Theorem~5.5.3 and Section~5.4]{GompfStipsicz1999} expresses the
movie by the eight moves above and slides among \(3\)-handles.  At each step,
\cref{lem:handle-cohomology-naturality} identifies the transported handle
class with the same singular-cohomology class.  Induction along the movie,
followed by Poincar\'e-duality naturality
\[
  f^*\operatorname{PD}_Y(f_*\alpha)=\operatorname{PD}_X(\alpha),
\]
gives the endpoint statement.
\end{proof}
