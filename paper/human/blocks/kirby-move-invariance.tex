\subsection{Marked Kirby--Cerf invariance and manifold descent}
\label{subsec:kirby-invariance}

The identity-character evaluator is already invariant under Kirby--Cerf
moves by \cref{rem:identity-character-specialization}.  We prove that the
presentation-level scalar remains unchanged after the transported character
coupons are inserted.  The proof compares the same local ribbon identities;
the only new inputs are the cocycle-coordinate transport of
\cref{prop:marking-coordinate-transport} and the naturality, monoidality, and
duality identities for \(\eta\).

\begin{lemma}[Elementary-move invariance for transported cocycles]
\label{lem:character-compatibility-elementary-moves}
Let
\[
  m\colon(\mathcal H,\beta)
  \longrightarrow(\mathcal H',|m|_*\beta)
\]
be a marked elementary Kirby--Cerf move between one-ended Kirby data.  Let
\(\omega\) represent \(\xi_{\mathcal H,\beta}\), and choose a representative
\(\omega'\) of \(\xi_{\mathcal H',|m|_*\beta}\) whose coordinates satisfy
\cref{prop:marking-coordinate-transport}.  Then
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,\omega)
  =
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H',\omega').
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The eight directed move types available in a one-ended movie divide into
three kinds of character check.  We list every type explicitly.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Moves that do not change the relevant \(2\)-handle
coordinates.}
These are the following three move types.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Level isotopy.}
  Here a level isotopy means a framing-preserving ambient isotopy of the
  attaching embeddings in the boundary of the lower-handle submanifold,
  performed between two adjacent critical values.  No handle slide, birth,
  or death occurs.  This includes isotopies which, in a dotted-circle picture,
  are drawn by passing a \(2\)-handle strand through a dotted circle.
  It carries each labelled \(2\)-handle component, together with its coupon
  \((\eta_{\lambda_k})^{\omega_k}\), to the corresponding target component.
  Thus the source and target are framed-isotopic decorated ribbon graphs.
  Their Reshetikhin--Turaev evaluations are equal, while the label weights and
  normalization factor are unchanged.

  \item \textbf{A \(1/1\) slide.}
  Before the slide, the local morphisms passing through the two dotted circles
  are reduced to identity morphisms by the evaluator.  After the slide, the
  corresponding local morphisms are again reduced to identity morphisms.
  Thus the slide only changes a piece that has already been reduced to the
  identity into a piece that is again reduced to the identity, so the
  evaluation is unchanged.

  \item \textbf{A \(3/3\) slide.}
  The slide changes the boundary map \(\partial_3\) by precomposition with an
  elementary automorphism of \(C_3\); in particular,
  \(\operatorname{im}(\partial_3)\subset C_2\) is unchanged.  It changes
  neither the group \(C_2\) with its handle basis nor the dotted--framed
  ribbon graph.  Hence every label \(\lambda_k\), every cocycle coordinate
  \(\omega_k\), and every coupon
  \((\eta_{\lambda_k})^{\omega_k}\) is literally the same on the two sides.
  The number of \(3\)-handles is also unchanged, so the normalization factors
  agree and the two evaluator summands are equal.
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{\(2/2\) slides.}
Let \(x,y\) be the moving and stationary simple labels and let \(a,b\) be
their source cocycle coordinates.  The coordinate transport from
\cref{prop:marking-coordinate-transport} is
\[
  (a,b)\longmapsto(a+b,b)
  \quad\text{or}\quad
  (a,b)\longmapsto(a-b,b),
\]
according to the two orientations.  The two oriented local models are checked
by expanding the same regular-colour sliding identity.

Write
\[
  \Omega=\sum_{i\in\operatorname{Irr}(\cC)}d_i\,i
\]
for the regular colouring.  The untwisted proof is the sliding lemma
\cite[Lemma~2.30]{Tham2021}: after the identity on the attaching band is
resolved through all simple fusion channels and all multiplicity basis
vectors, the source and target \(\Omega\)-coloured graphs become the same
finite sum.  In particular, this is an identity of the \emph{complete}
regular-colour sums.  It does not identify a source graph with fixed labels
\((x,y)\) with a target graph carrying the same fixed labels.

More explicitly, for the positive local orientation choose composition-dual
bases
\[
  u_{z,\mu}\colon z\longrightarrow x\otimes y^*,
  \qquad
  p_{z,\mu}\colon x\otimes y^*\longrightarrow z,
\]
and for the negative orientation choose the analogous bases with
\(x\otimes y\) in place of \(x\otimes y^*\).  Semisimplicity gives
\begin{align*}
  \operatorname{id}_{x\otimes y^*}
  &=\sum_{z,\mu}u_{z,\mu}\circ p_{z,\mu}
    &&\text{in the positive model},\\
  \operatorname{id}_{x\otimes y}
  &=\sum_{z,\mu}u_{z,\mu}\circ p_{z,\mu}
    &&\text{in the negative model}.
\end{align*}
Inserting the appropriate resolution at attachment and contracting the same
dual bases at detachment is precisely the fusion-channel expansion used in
the proof of the untwisted sliding lemma.
Thus both sides have the same indices \((x,y,z,\mu)\), the same quantum
dimension coefficients, the same exterior graph, and the same normalization.
Only the character multiplier still has to be compared.

For each summand of this \emph{common expansion}, the comparison is as
follows.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The positive \(2/2\) slide.}
  Its channel condition is \(z\subset x\otimes y^*\).  The source
  multiplier is \(\eta_x^a\eta_y^b\), whereas after attachment and cocycle
  transport the same channel carries
  \[
    \eta_x^{a+b}\eta_{z^*}^{b}
    =\eta_x^a\eta_y^b,
  \]
  by \cref{lem:two-over-two-character-factors}.

  \item \textbf{The negative \(2/2\) slide.}
  Its channels satisfy \(z\subset x\otimes y\).  The corresponding target
  multiplier is
\[
  \eta_x^{a-b}\eta_z^b
  =\eta_x^a\eta_y^b.
\]
  This is the second identity of
  \cref{lem:two-over-two-character-factors}.
\end{itemize}
The equalities hold for every multiplicity index \(\mu\).  Naturality moves
the coupons through the inclusion, projection, braiding, and exterior
morphisms occurring in that summand.  Consequently the source and target
decorate the common untwisted fusion expansion by exactly the same scalar on
each term.  Summing over \(x,y,z,\mu\) now gives equality of the two complete
decorated regular-colour sums.  This proves the \(2/2\) slide identity; it does
not assert a false termwise equality before the fusion resolutions are
inserted.

\item \textbf{Adjacent-index births and deaths.}
These are the following four directed move types.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A \(1\)--\(2\) birth.}
  The new \(2\)-handle attaching circle goes geometrically once over the new
  dotted \(1\)-handle, and no other strand meets this cancelling local model.
  Hence, for a simple label \(\lambda\) on the new \(2\)-handle, the
  through-object at the dotted disk is \(\lambda\) or \(\lambda^*\).  The
  target cocycle may be represented by \(\omega'=\omega\oplus0\), so its new
  character coupon is the identity.  Since the tensor unit is simple,
  \[
    \operatorname{tr}
      \bigl(P_{\mathbf1,\lambda}\bigr)
    =\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{\cC}
      (\mathbf1,\lambda)
    =\delta_{\lambda,\mathbf1}.
  \]
  The same equation holds with \(\lambda^*\) in place of \(\lambda\), so the
  local orientation does not affect the result.
  The entire new local contribution to the label sum is therefore
  \[
    \sum_{\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}(\cC)}
      d_\lambda\,
      \operatorname{tr}
        \bigl(P_{\mathbf1,\lambda}\bigr)
    =d_{\mathbf1}=1.
  \]
  Thus adding the cancelling pair does not change the evaluator.

  \item \textbf{A \(1\)--\(2\) death.}
  Cohomological descent permits a representative whose disappearing
  \(2\)-handle coordinate is zero.  The same geometric-intersection-one
  calculation shows that the isolated pair contributes the scalar \(1\), so
  removing it does not change the evaluator.

  \item \textbf{A \(2\)--\(3\) birth.}
  The cocycle equation forces the new \(2\)-handle coordinate to be zero, so
  the new coupon is \(\eta^0=\operatorname{id}\).

  \item \textbf{A \(2\)--\(3\) death.}
  This is the reverse calculation: the cocycle equation forces the
  disappearing \(2\)-handle coordinate, and hence its character exponent,
  to be zero.
\end{itemize}
The calculation for the \(1\)--\(2\) pair uses essentially that each label
state assigns a simple object to every \(2\)-handle component.  For a general
object such as \(\mathbf1\oplus\mathbf1\), the trace of the tensor-unit
projector would be \(2\); such an object is not a single label state in the
evaluator.
In all four cases the untwisted cancellation and normalization identities
therefore apply without alteration.
\end{itemize}

For the identity character, the cited handle-decomposition invariance proves
the required local ribbon identity together with its label weights and
normalization factor.  The checks above show, move by move, that inserting the
transported character coupons preserves the corresponding untwisted local
identity.  For a \(2/2\) slide this comparison is termwise only after both
sides have been expanded into the common complete fusion-channel sum; it is
not termwise in the original simple labels.  Summing the resulting local
identities gives the asserted equality of presentation-level scalars.  In a
death move, the replacement by a zero-coordinate source representative does
not change the scalar by \cref{prop:representative-independence}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Marked Kirby--Cerf descent]
\label{thm:marked-kirby-invariance-descent}
Let \((\mathcal H,\beta)\) and \((\mathcal K,\gamma)\) be marked one-ended
Kirby data, let
\[
  \rho\colon|(\mathcal H,\beta)|\longrightarrow(X,\alpha),
  \qquad
  \sigma\colon|(\mathcal K,\gamma)|\longrightarrow(Y,\delta)
\]
be marked Kirby presentations, and suppose that
\[
  f\colon(X,\alpha)\longrightarrow(Y,\delta)
\]
is a marked diffeomorphism.  Then
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,\beta)
  =\CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal K,\gamma).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For an elementary marked move, choose source and target cocycles as in
\cref{prop:marking-coordinate-transport}.  Their presentation-level
evaluations agree by \cref{lem:character-compatibility-elementary-moves}, and
\cref{prop:representative-independence} identifies these values with the
corresponding cohomology-class evaluations.  Induction on the length of a
finite marked movie therefore proves invariance along the movie.

Marked Kirby--Cerf completeness, \cref{prop:marked-kirby-cerf}, supplies a
finite marked movie from \((\mathcal H,\beta)\) to
\((\mathcal K,\gamma)\) realizing \(f\).  Invariance along that movie proves
the asserted equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Twisted Crane--Yetter invariant]
\label{def:twisted-crane-yetter-invariant}
Fix a twisted categorical input \((\cC,\eta)\).  Given a marked
orientation-preserving diffeomorphism class represented by an
\(H_2\)-marked \(4\)-manifold \((X,\alpha)\), choose a marked one-ended Kirby
datum \((\mathcal H,\beta)\) and a marked Kirby presentation
\[
  \rho\colon|(\mathcal H,\beta)|\longrightarrow(X,\alpha).
\]
The \emph{homologically twisted Crane--Yetter invariant} is
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(X,\alpha)
  :=\CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,\beta).
\]
\Cref{thm:marked-kirby-invariance-descent} shows that this value is
independent of the representative \((X,\alpha)\), the marked Kirby datum, and
the marked Kirby presentation.  Thus \(\CY_{\cC,\eta}\) is an invariant of
marked orientation-preserving diffeomorphism classes of closed, connected,
oriented smooth \(4\)-manifolds.
\end{definition}
