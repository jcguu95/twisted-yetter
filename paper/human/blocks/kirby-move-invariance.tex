\subsection{Kirby--Cerf-move invariance}
\label{subsec:kirby-invariance}

Throughout this subsection, fix a twisted categorical input \((\cC,\eta)\).

The proof can be read from the following list.  Each item is local, and the
same exterior ribbon graph is restored after the calculation.
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=2.2em]
  \item[Changing the cocycle representative.]
  A coboundary inserts character coupons around a dotted handle; the
  tensor-unit projector absorbs them.

  \item[Framed isotopy.]
  Ribbon graphical coherence preserves the RT evaluation.

  \item[\(1/1\) slides and \(2/1\) passages.]
  Cut at the stationary dotted handle.  Only the tensor-unit channel survives,
  and that channel is transparent to the slide.

  \item[\(2/2\) slides.]
  Resolve the band attachment into simple fusion channels, transport the
  character coupon by naturality, and apply the inverse resolution at
  detachment.

  \item[\(3/3\) slides.]
  Only the basis of \(C_3\) changes; the number of \(3\)-handles and
  \(\operatorname{im}(\partial_3)\) remain fixed.

  \item[\(1\)--\(2\) and \(2\)--\(3\) cancellations.]
  Put the pair in standard position.  The new character exponent is zero, and
  the remaining local scalar is cancelled by the change in normalization.
\end{description}

The rest of this subsection proves precisely these six assertions.

The character coupons disappear on the tensor-unit channel for a formal
reason that will be used repeatedly.

\begin{lemma}[Absorption by the tensor-unit projector]
\label{lem:unit-projector-absorbs-character}
For every object $W\in\cC$ and every $n\in\mathbb Z$,
\[
  P_{\mathbf1,W}\eta_W^n=P_{\mathbf1,W}
  =\eta_W^nP_{\mathbf1,W}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $P_{\mathbf1,W}=\sum_\mu u_\mu\widehat u_\mu$ as in
\cref{def:tensor-unit-projector}.  Monoidality gives
$\eta_{\mathbf1}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbf1}$.  Naturality, first for
$u_\mu\colon\mathbf1\to W$ and then for
$\widehat u_\mu\colon W\to\mathbf1$, gives
\[
  \eta_W^n u_\mu=u_\mu,
  \qquad
  \widehat u_\mu\eta_W^n=\widehat u_\mu.
\]
Summing these identities proves both equalities.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Naturality of the tensor-unit projector]
\label{lem:unit-projector-naturality}
For every morphism $f\colon U\to V$ in $\cC$,
\[
  fP_{\mathbf1,U}=P_{\mathbf1,V}f.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Finite semisimplicity decomposes every object as a direct sum of its simple
isotypic summands.  By Schur's lemma, a morphism has no matrix entries
between the tensor-unit isotypic summand and a nonisomorphic simple
isotypic summand.  It therefore preserves the tensor-unit summand, which is
exactly the displayed identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite transport of the tensor-unit projector]
\label{cor:finite-unit-projector-transport}
A tensor-unit projector may be moved through any finite composite of
crossings, cups, caps, twists, and morphism coupons, with the source and
target projectors changed according to the boundary objects.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{lem:unit-projector-naturality} successively to the elementary
morphisms in the ribbon expression.  Graphically, this is the tunnelling
property of the completeness idempotent in
\cite[Lemmas~2.27--2.28]{Tham2021}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Local form of a dotted-handle operation]
\label{lem:dotted-collar-factorization}
Let \(\mathcal H\) be a one-ended Kirby presentation, let
\(a\in Z^2(\mathcal H)\), fix an ordinary-label state $\lambda$, and let
$\delta$ be an oriented dotted component of \(D_{\mathcal H}\) equipped with
a dotted-handle section.  Its positive face determines
the signed word $w_\delta$ and the boundary-state object
$B_\delta=|w_\delta|$ of \cref{def:dotted-circle-translation}.  After all
other labels and dotted operations are fixed, regluing the common exterior
defines a linear functional
\[
  \mathcal E_\delta\colon
  \operatorname{End}_{\cC}(B_\delta)\longrightarrow\mathbb C.
\]
The dotted operation in that term is
\[
  \mathcal E_\delta(P_{\mathbf1,B_\delta}).
  \tag{4.7}\label{eq:dotted-section}
\]
For every puncture $p$ on the $k$th ordinary component, the following four
features remain distinct.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),ref=\roman*]
  \item The orientation of component $k$ fixes the ribbon label
  $\lambda_k$ before the disk is chosen; this is what distinguishes
  $\lambda_k$ from $\lambda_k^*$.

  \item The oriented arc and the disk coorientation determine the intersection
  sign $\epsilon(p)$.  This is both its contribution to $\partial_2$ and the
  variance of the corresponding boundary point in the signed cut word.

  \item The marking representative supplies $a_k$ and hence the actual coupon
  $(\eta^{a_k})_{\lambda_k}$.  The disk supplies neither this exponent nor its
  sign.

  \item Neither the oriented label nor \(a_k\) depends on the dotted-handle
  section.  A negative boundary occurrence uses the rigid dual of the
  already fixed oriented label; it does not recolour the component.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Read the punctures on the positive collar face.  The explicit realization
$B_\delta=|w_\delta|$ uses the rigid dual for a negative boundary occurrence
but does not change the label of the underlying oriented component.  The
opposite face is the reversed rigid dual, exactly as in
\cref{def:dotted-circle-translation}.  Regluing everything
outside the section defines the linear context $\mathcal E_\delta$.

Inside the product neighborhood, bunch the transverse ribbons and insert the
dimension-weighted simple-channel resolution of
\cite[Lemma~2.27]{Tham2021}.  Definition~5.11, $k=3$, sends a resulting simple
channel $i$ to zero unless $i\cong\mathbf1$, in which case it removes that
channel.  Summing the surviving multiplicity terms is precisely the canonical
projector $P_{\mathbf1,B_\delta}$, so regluing gives
\eqref{eq:dotted-section}.  Notice that the projector is inserted on
the through-channel before closure; no currying of the whole exterior tangle
is involved.  The second item is the standard signed-intersection formula for
the coefficient of a $1$-handle in the cellular boundary of a $2$-handle; the
first and third items were fixed before the disk was chosen, and the fourth
follows from them.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Independence of auxiliary graphical choices]
\label{prop:graphical-auxiliary-independence}
For a one-ended Kirby presentation $\mathcal H$ and a handle cocycle
\(a\in Z^2(\mathcal H)\),
the evaluator is independent of the dual bases used to expand the
tensor-unit projectors and of the dotted-handle sections used to draw them.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The sum $\sum_\mu u_\mu\widehat u_\mu$ is the canonical projector onto the
full tensor-unit isotypic summand, so a change of composition-dual bases does
not change its morphism.  The evaluator is defined by composing the
intrinsic handle maps.  By \cref{prop:dotted-section-index-three}, a
dotted-handle section only draws those fixed maps.  Two drawings of the same
intrinsic composite have the same
morphism by ribbon graphical coherence \cite[Theorem~6.1]{Shum1994}; no
comparison theorem for arbitrary spanning-disk systems is required.  For
several dotted handles, their actual handle neighborhoods are disjoint, so
their maps tensor independently.  Exchanging their written order is the
interchange law for tensor products of morphisms.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Independence of auxiliary orientations]
\label{prop:orientation-choice-independence}
Let \(\mathcal H\) be a one-ended Kirby presentation and let
\(a\in Z^2(\mathcal H)\).  The evaluator is unchanged when any auxiliary
oriented handle generator and
its tied Kirby component orientation are reversed as in
\cref{conv:orientation-reversal}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For an ordinary component, reindex its simple-label sum by
$i\leftrightarrow i^*$.  Ribbon orientation reversal is precisely the
standard dual-label convention, $d_{i^*}=d_i$, and
\[
  (\eta^{-a})_{i^*}
  =\eta_{i^*}^{-a}\operatorname{id}_{i^*}
  =\eta_i^a\operatorname{id}_{i^*}.
\]
Thus every reindexed summand gives the same RT morphism under the canonical
orientation--duality identification and has the same coefficient.  For a
dotted component, reversing the $1$-handle generator also reverses the disk
coorientation.  The signed boundary word is replaced by its rigid dual, with
reversed order; pivotal duality carries the canonical projector onto the
tensor-unit summand to the canonical projector on the dual boundary object,
and the exterior closure is carried to its pivotal transpose.  Spherical
graphical evaluation is unchanged.  The incidence
coefficient and the dual $1$-cochain generator both reverse, so the
representative calculation below is covariant.  A $C_3$ orientation change
does not change $\operatorname{im}(\partial_3)$ and hence does not change
    $H^2(\mathcal H)$ or its marking.
\end{proof}

For an ordered signed boundary word
$w=((X_1,\sigma_1),\ldots,(X_r,\sigma_r))$, with
$\sigma_j\in\{+1,-1\}$, set
\[
  \chi_\eta(w):=\prod_{j=1}^r\eta_{X_j}^{\sigma_j}
  \in\mathbb C^\times.
\]
Here $X_j$ is the label of the already oriented ribbon.  The sign records the
variance of its boundary occurrence; categorically a negative occurrence is
interpreted by rigidity, and
$\eta_{X_j^*}=\eta_{X_j}^{-1}$.  It does not recolour the component.

\begin{lemma}[Dotted-incidence character identity]
\label{lem:dotted-incidence-character}
Let \(\mathcal H\) be a one-ended Kirby presentation, let \(\lambda\) be a
simple-label state, and let $e_\delta^*$ be the cellular $1$-cochain dual to
a dotted primal $1$-handle \(\delta\).  Let $w_\delta$ be the signed boundary
word of \cref{lem:dotted-collar-factorization}.  For every $m\in\mathbb Z$,
\[
  \prod_k\eta_{\lambda_k}^{m(\delta^1e_\delta^*)_k}
  =
  \chi_\eta(w_\delta)^m.
  \tag{4.8}\label{eq:dotted-incidence-character}
\]
Moreover the scalar on either side acts trivially on the closed term
$\mathcal E_\delta(P_{\mathbf1,B_\delta})$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient $(\delta^1e_\delta^*)_k$ is the coefficient of the dotted
$1$-handle in the cellular boundary of the $k$th $2$-handle.  In the collar
normal form, \eqref{eq:dotted-cellular-boundary} gives the cochain identity
\[
  \delta^1e_\delta^*=\sum_k I_\delta(k)e_k^*,
  \qquad
  I_\delta(k)=\sum_{p:\,k(p)=k}\epsilon(p).
\]
The puncture $p$ contributes
$\eta_{\lambda_{k(p)}}^{m\epsilon(p)}$.  Multiplying these contributions and
grouping equal component labels proves
\eqref{eq:dotted-incidence-character}.  This bookkeeping uses the disk only
to record the signed boundary occurrence; the ordinary component was already
coloured by $\lambda_{k(p)}$, and the disk supplies neither that colour nor
the exponent $a_{k(p)}$.

By rigidity and \cref{lem:character-identities}, the component of $\eta^m$
on the boundary-state object $B_\delta=|w_\delta|$ is exactly the scalar
$\chi_\eta(w_\delta)^m$: a contravariant occurrence is interpreted on the
dual object and hence inverts its character value.  Therefore the scalar
introduced by the coboundary is $(\eta^m)_{B_\delta}$.  It is absorbed on
either side of $P_{\mathbf1,B_\delta}$ by
\cref{lem:unit-projector-absorbs-character}.  Applying the exterior context
$\mathcal E_\delta$ proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Representative independence]
\label{prop:representative-independence}
Let \(\mathcal H\) be a one-ended Kirby presentation.  If two integral
cellular $2$-cocycles $a,a'\in Z^2(\mathcal H)$ represent the same class
in $H^2(\mathcal H)$, then
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,a)=\CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,a').
\]
Consequently the value depends only on the marked class
$\xi_{\mathcal H}\in H^2(\mathcal H)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $a$ and $a'$ represent the same cellular cohomology class, there is a
$1$-cochain $b\in C^1(\mathcal H)$ such that
\[
  a'-a=\delta^1b.
\]
The group $C^1(\mathcal H)$ is freely generated by the primal $1$-handles, so it is
enough to prove the assertion when $b=m e^*_{\delta}$ for one dotted
component $\delta$ and one integer $m$, and then induct over the finite
support of $b$.

Fix an ordinary-label state $\lambda$.  Replacing $a$ by
$a+m\delta^1e_\delta^*$ multiplies its character weight by
\[
  \prod_k\eta_{\lambda_k}^{m(\delta^1e_\delta^*)_k}.
\]
By \cref{lem:dotted-incidence-character}, this is the component of $\eta^m$
on the through-channel $B_\delta$ carrying the dotted projector, and
hence is absorbed by that projector before the exterior is closed.  The
Kirby-colour weights and all other local morphisms are
unchanged.  Therefore the two fixed-label summands are equal.  Summing over
$\lambda$, and then applying the argument successively to the finitely many
generators in the support of $b$, proves the claim.
\end{proof}

We therefore write
$\CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,\xi_{\mathcal H})$ for the value of any cellular
$2$-cocycle representing $\xi_{\mathcal H}$.

\begin{lemma}[Character transport along a ribbon]
\label{lem:finite-coupon-transport}
Let $n\in\mathbb Z$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),ref=\roman*]
  \item For every morphism $f\colon U\to V$ in \(\cC\),
  \[
    (\eta^n)_Vf=f(\eta^n)_U.
  \]

  \item The same naturality identity applies to braidings and twists.

  \item For every object \(X\in\cC\), at a dual bend the transported
  component is its mate:
  \[
    \operatorname{ev}_X
      (\operatorname{id}_{X^*}\otimes(\eta^n)_X)
    =
    \operatorname{ev}_X
      ((\eta^{-n})_{X^*}\otimes\operatorname{id}_X),
  \]
  with the analogous coevaluation identity.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently a character decoration may be transported along any compact
ribbon through the finitely many crossings, extrema, twists, and coupons of
a generic diagram.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first statement is naturality of the monoidal natural automorphism
$\eta^n$; braiding and twist are particular morphisms.  Naturality for
$\operatorname{ev}_X$ gives
\[
 \operatorname{ev}_X
 ((\eta^n)_{X^*}\otimes(\eta^n)_X)=\operatorname{ev}_X.
\]
Precompose with $(\eta^{-n})_{X^*}\otimes\operatorname{id}_X$ to obtain the
displayed bend identity; the coevaluation formula is dual.  Applying these
identities successively along the finite route proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of coupon naturality]
\label{rem:coupon-naturality-scope}
The first identity transports the automorphism of the whole boundary object
through a multivalent coupon; it does not route one factor through an
arbitrary leg.  At a dual bend, expressing the same oriented route on the
dual strand replaces the local exponent \(n\) by \(-n\).
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Index-$3$ handle-slide identity]
\label{lem:index-three-handle-slide}
Let \(m\colon\mathcal H\to\mathcal H'\) be a $1/1$ slide between one-ended
Kirby presentations in the local form of
\cref{prop:local-movie-normal-forms}.  Turn the handle decompositions over,
and denote the two involved dual index-$3$ handles before the slide by
$H_m^\vee,H_s^\vee$ and after the slide by
$H_m^{\vee\prime},H_s^{\vee\prime}$.  After identifying the incoming and
outgoing level boundaries outside the supporting ball, let
\(\mathsf H^{(3)}_{\mathrm{src}}\) and
\(\mathsf H^{(3)}_{\mathrm{tgt}}\) be the composites of the two intrinsic
index-$3$ handle maps on the source and target sides.  Then, for every
decorated incoming skein $\Gamma$,
\[
  \mathsf H^{(3)}_{\mathrm{src}}([\Gamma])
  =
  \mathsf H^{(3)}_{\mathrm{tgt}}([\Gamma]).
  \tag{4.9}\label{eq:one-one-index-three-interface}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{remark}[Multiplicity indices in the $1/1$ identity]
\label{rem:one-one-multiplicity-data}
At dotted-handle sections, each side is expanded using the full
composition-dual resolution of the tensor-unit isotypic summand for each of
the two handles.  Equation~\eqref{eq:one-one-index-three-interface} retains
every multiplicity index separately: it does \emph{not} replace two
projectors by a single projector on the tensor product of their
through-objects.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:dotted-section-index-three}, the two projectors are the
graphical expressions of the actual dual index-$3$ handle maps.  Expand the
incoming skein simultaneously across the two co-cores using complete simple
resolutions.  Write its summands as
\[
  [\Gamma]
  =\sum_{i,j,\mu,\nu}[\Gamma_{i,j;\mu,\nu}],
\]
where $i,j$ are the two transverse simple channels and $\mu,\nu$ are their
full multiplicity indices; the trace-dual resolution coefficients are
absorbed into the notation $[\Gamma_{i,j;\mu,\nu}]$.  By
\cite[Definition~5.11, $k=3$]{Tham2021}, the
two source handle maps act termwise as
\[
  \mathsf H^{(3)}_{\mathrm{src}}
    ([\Gamma_{i,j;\mu,\nu}])
  =\delta_{i,\mathbf1}\delta_{j,\mathbf1}
    [\Gamma_{i,j;\mu,\nu}].
  \tag{4.10}\label{eq:one-one-simple-channel-action}
\]

The standard $1/1$ source and target are the same-index slide models of
\cref{def:handle-slide}, realized as in
\cref{prop:elementary-move-realization}.  Moving from the source pair of
co-cores to the target pair changes the two complete resolutions by the
tunnelling isomorphism of
\cite[Lemma~2.28, Equation~(2.17)]{Tham2021}.  This isomorphism is obtained
from the same composition-dual bases, is invertible on every multiplicity
space, and carries the $(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ summand identically to the
$(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ summand.  Applying the target index-$3$ maps therefore
again gives the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:one-one-simple-channel-action}, with the same surviving
$\mu,\nu$.  Summing the resolved terms proves
\eqref{eq:one-one-index-three-interface}.  This is the algebraic expansion
of the final $1$-handle-slide relation in the proof of
\cite[Proposition~5.13, pp.~53--54]{Tham2021}.

The input is an arbitrary decorated skein, so all character coupons and
uninvolved morphisms remain present.  The supporting-ball model and
\cref{item:fixed-exterior} identify the relation's endpoints with the actual
Kirby source and target, while the common exterior is applied only after the
local morphism equality has been established.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Projector naturality for a $2/1$ passage]
\label{lem:two-one-projector-naturality}
Let \(m\colon\mathcal H\to\mathcal H'\) be a $2/1$ passage between one-ended
Kirby presentations.  Put \(m\) into the clean local form of
\cref{prop:local-movie-normal-forms}, denote its stationary dotted component
by \(\delta\), and cut that handle.  Write $B=|w_\delta|$ for its
through-object, let \(x\in\operatorname{Irr}(\cC)\) be the moving oriented
ribbon label, and let $a\in\mathbb Z$ be the moving character exponent.
There is a common exterior closure functional on
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\cC}(B\otimes x,x\otimes B)$ under which the positive
source and target local tangles are respectively the two sides of
\[
  c_{B,x}\bigl(P_{\mathbf1,B}\otimes(\eta^a)_x\bigr)
  =
  \bigl((\eta^a)_x\otimes P_{\mathbf1,B}\bigr)c_{B,x}.
  \tag{4.11}\label{eq:positive-two-one-interface}
\]
For the opposite crossing orientation they are the two sides of
\[
  c_{x,B}^{-1}\bigl(P_{\mathbf1,B}\otimes(\eta^a)_x\bigr)
  =
  \bigl((\eta^a)_x\otimes P_{\mathbf1,B}\bigr)c_{x,B}^{-1}.
  \tag{4.12}\label{eq:negative-two-one-interface}
\]
The clean attaching collar is the left factorization, the band transport is
the displayed braiding followed by transport along the
stationary component, and the clean detaching collar is the right
factorization.  Positive orientation gives $c_{B,x}$, negative orientation
gives $c_{x,B}^{-1}$, the blackboard push-off fixes the band framing, and a
bend transports the coupon by its rigid mate.  The source and target
regluing maps agree outside the supporting ball.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In the pair-of-balls model for the stationary $1$-handle, a $2/1$ passage
pulls the moving attaching arc through one ball and returns it through the
other.  Cutting at the positive face of
\cref{prop:dotted-section-index-three} identifies the source local
tangle with the left composite of \eqref{eq:positive-two-one-interface} and
the referenced target local tangle with the right composite.  Reversing the
local crossing gives
\eqref{eq:negative-two-one-interface}.  The blackboard push-off of the band
fixes the ribbon framing, and a change of local orientation is expressed by
the rigid mate at the corresponding cup or cap.  Thus the equations name the
actual source and target tangles, not merely abstract morphisms with the same
type.

Both equalities are the naturality square for the braiding, applied to
$P_{\mathbf1,B}\colon B\to B$ and
$(\eta^a)_x\colon x\to x$.  Equivalently, expand $P_{\mathbf1,B}$ as the full
composition-dual resolution and specialize the tunnelling identity
\cite[Lemma~2.28, Equation~(2.17), p.~8]{Tham2021} to the morphism
$f=(\eta^a)_x$; every unit multiplicity index remains in the sum.  Every
event between the two clean collars is handled by
\cref{lem:finite-coupon-transport}; cups and caps use the displayed mate
identity there.  The exterior is fixed by \cref{item:fixed-exterior}.
Applying that common exterior context proves the local evaluation equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Dotted-handle slides]
\label{lem:dotted-handle-slides}
Let \(m\colon\mathcal H\to\mathcal H'\) be either a $1/1$ slide or a $2/1$
passage, and let \(\xi\in H^2(\mathcal H)\).  Then
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,\xi)
  =\CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H',T_m\xi).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put the slide into the clean local form of
\cref{prop:local-movie-normal-forms} and keep the exterior fixed.

For a $1/1$ slide, \cref{lem:index-three-handle-slide} identifies the
source and target composites of the two dual index-$3$ maps.  Because the
identity is an equality on every decorated input skein, it retains all
character coupons already present on ordinary strands.  It also retains the
two full unit-projector resolutions separately, including all multiplicity
indices.  No ordinary $2$-handle generator changes.

For a $2/1$ passage, \cref{lem:two-one-projector-naturality} identifies the
source and target local tangles with the two sides of projector and character
naturality, for either crossing orientation.  During the finite route, the
    moving character decoration is transported only by the naturality rules of
\cref{lem:finite-coupon-transport}; any bend uses the dual mate and any
multivalent coupon uses whole-word naturality.  Since the route returns to
the same oriented moving component, the final coupon is again
$(\eta^{a})_x$.  Applying the common exterior context therefore gives the
target marked local morphism with no appeal to modular killing.

For both moves the ordinary $2$-handle generators are canonically identified
before and after the move.  Choose the target cocycle to be the image of the
source cocycle under the induced cochain map; corresponding ordinary
components then carry the same exponents and the same character coupons.
The preceding graphical equality is therefore already an equality of marked
summands.  Any other cocycle representing the transported target class gives
the same answer by \cref{prop:representative-independence}.  Summing over all
ordinary labels proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Trace-dual band-transport identity]
\label{lem:weighted-kirby-colour-slide}
Let \(x\in\operatorname{Irr}(\cC)\).  Fix an $x$-coloured moving ribbon, an
oriented band, and a common exterior tangle \(E\), with no character coupon
inserted in the local band calculation; write
\(\operatorname{Cl}_E\) for closure by that exterior.  Expand the stationary
Kirby colour
$\Omega_{\cC}=\sum_y d_y y$.  For a simple stationary label $y$, choose the
trace-dual resolution
\[
  \operatorname{id}_{x\otimes y^\varepsilon}
  =\sum_{z,\mu}d_z\,\iota_{z,\mu}\pi_{z,\mu},
  \qquad \varepsilon\in\{+1,-1\}.
\]
Let $A^\varepsilon_{y,z,\mu}$ be the local morphism obtained by inserting
$\iota_{z,\mu}$ and $\pi_{z,\mu}$ in the clean attaching collar.  Transport
the insertion along the finite band route.  Pivotal mating gives a bijection
from the attaching fusion basis to the detaching fusion basis; write
$\mu\mapsto\mu^\sharp$ for this bijection and call the resulting local
morphism $D^\varepsilon_{y,z,\mu^\sharp}$.  Then, after the canonical
identification of the attaching and detaching boundary words,
\[
  \operatorname{Cl}_E(A^\varepsilon_{y,z,\mu})
  =\operatorname{Cl}_E(D^\varepsilon_{y,z,\mu^\sharp})
\]
for every $(y,z,\mu)$, and consequently
\[
  \sum_{y,z,\mu}d_y d_z\,
    \operatorname{Cl}_E(A^\varepsilon_{y,z,\mu})
  =
  \sum_{y,z,\mu}d_y d_z\,
    \operatorname{Cl}_E(D^\varepsilon_{y,z,\mu^\sharp}).
\]
Both assertions hold for either band orientation.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the positive orientation use channels $z\subset x\otimes y^*$; for the
negative orientation use $z\subset x\otimes y$.  Braiding naturality moves
the inclusion and projection through each crossing of the finite route.
Rigidity moves them through cups and caps by taking mates, twist naturality
handles framing twists, and braiding naturality handles the remaining
crossings.  In the evaluator the stationary Kirby-colour ribbon carries no
independent morphism coupon along this route; the character coupon is
transported separately in \cref{lem:finite-coupon-transport}.  Induction on
the event list therefore identifies
$A^\varepsilon_{y,z,\mu}$ with
$D^\varepsilon_{y,z,\mu^\sharp}$ before closure.  Applying the common linear
functional $\operatorname{Cl}_E$ proves the displayed equality for each
matched pair of basis elements.

The mate operation is a bijection between the attaching and detaching fusion
bases.  Sphericality preserves their trace-dual pairing, so the coefficient
$d_z$ is unchanged.  Multiplication by the stationary Kirby-colour weight
$d_y$ and summation give the displayed weighted equality.  Applying the
inverse completeness relation at detachment recombines the sum into the
target Kirby colour.  This is the insertion and band-transport calculation of
\cite[Lemmas~3.2--3.4]{BarenzBarrett2018}.  The factors $d_y d_z$ and the
pivotal mate are displayed in Equations~(3.1.3)--(3.1.8), while preservation
of the trace-dual pairing is proved in Equations~(3.1.9)--(3.1.11), with the
pivotal functor specialized to the identity.  Thus no extra functorial
structure from the generalized setting is used here.  Reversing the
stationary orientation, replacing every label by its dual, and using
$\Omega_{\cC}^*=\Omega_{\cC}$ gives the negative orientation; spherical
duality preserves $d_y d_z$ and every multiplicity index.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Character-aware fusion attachment]
\label{lem:character-aware-fusion-attachment}
Let $x,y\in\operatorname{Irr}(\cC)$ and let $a,b\in\mathbb Z$.  Choose, for
each \(\varepsilon\in\{+1,-1\}\), every simple summand $z$ of
\(x\otimes y^\varepsilon\), where \(y^{-1}:=y^*\) and
\(y^{+1}:=y\), and every multiplicity index $\mu$, trace-dual maps
\[
  \iota_{z,\mu}\colon z\longrightarrow x\otimes y^{\varepsilon},
  \qquad
  \pi_{z,\mu}\colon x\otimes y^{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow z,
  \qquad
  \pi_{z,\mu}\iota_{z,\nu}
    =\frac{\delta_{\mu\nu}}{d_z}\operatorname{id}_z,
  \qquad
  \sum_{z,\mu}d_z\,\iota_{z,\mu}\pi_{z,\mu}
    =\operatorname{id}_{x\otimes y^{\varepsilon}}.
\]
The source, channel, and target labels are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|c|c|c|c}
band & attaching channel & target stationary label & target coordinates
  & target character \\
\hline
positive & $z\subset x\otimes y^*$ & $z^*$ & $(a+b,b)$
  & $\eta_x^{a+b}\eta_{z^*}^{b}$ \\
negative & $z\subset x\otimes y$ & $z$ & $(a-b,b)$
  & $\eta_x^{a-b}\eta_z^{b}$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For the positive band, the pivotal mate
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}(z,x\otimes y^*)
  \cong\operatorname{Hom}(y,z^*\otimes x)
\]
is the detaching basis.  Suppressing associators, it sends
$\iota\colon z\to x\otimes y^*$ to
\[
  \iota^\sharp
  :=(\operatorname{id}_{z^*}\otimes\operatorname{id}_x
       \otimes\operatorname{ev}_y)
    (\operatorname{id}_{z^*}\otimes\iota\otimes\operatorname{id}_y)
    (\operatorname{coev}_z\otimes\operatorname{id}_y)
  \colon y\longrightarrow z^*\otimes x.
\]
Under this mate isomorphism, the ordered detaching labels are \((z^*,x)\).
The first is the stationary target strand in the positive convention and the
second is the moving strand.  Both rows therefore name the actual oriented
target label.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The negative row is obtained by conjugating the positive slide by reversal of
the auxiliary orientation of the stationary handle, both before and after the
slide.  In the reversed coordinates
\[
  \bar e_y=-e_y,\qquad \bar b=-b,\qquad \bar y=y^*,
\]
the geometric negative slide is the positive slide
$e_x'=e_x+\bar e_y$.  Its attaching channels are
$z\subset x\otimes\bar y^*=x\otimes y$, and the positive calculation gives
the target label $z^*$ with respect to the reversed target orientation.
Reversing that target orientation back changes $z^*$ to $z$ and
$\bar b$ to $b$.  The multiplicity index is transported by the same pivotal
mate.

The displayed normalization is the spherical trace-dual resolution
\cite[Lemma~2.37, Equation~(2.2.5)]{BarenzBarrett2018}; the factor $d_z$
occurs exactly once.  Naturality and monoidality of $\eta$ give
\[
  (\eta_x^n\otimes\eta_{y^{\varepsilon}}^n)\iota_{z,\mu}
  =\iota_{z,\mu}\eta_z^n
\]
for every $n\in\mathbb Z$.  Since
$\eta_{y^*}=\eta_y^{-1}$, the positive channels satisfy
$\eta_z=\eta_x\eta_y^{-1}$ and therefore
\[
  \eta_x^{a+b}\eta_{z^*}^{b}
  =\eta_x^{a+b}\eta_z^{-b}
  =\eta_x^{a+b}(\eta_x\eta_y^{-1})^{-b}
  =\eta_x^a\eta_y^b.
\]
The negative channels satisfy $\eta_z=\eta_x\eta_y$, so
\[
  \eta_x^{a-b}\eta_z^b
  =\eta_x^{a-b}(\eta_x\eta_y)^b
  =\eta_x^a\eta_y^b.
\]
For the negative row, apply the already proved positive identity after replacing
$(e_y,b,y)$ by $(\bar e_y,\bar b,\bar y)=(-e_y,-b,y^*)$.  Its target character
factor is
\[
  \eta_x^{a+\bar b}\eta_{z^*}^{\bar b}
  =\eta_x^{a-b}\eta_{z^*}^{-b}
  =\eta_x^{a-b}\eta_z^b.
\]
Now reverse the auxiliary target orientation back.  By
\cref{prop:orientation-choice-independence}, this changes neither the RT term
nor its coefficient, and it converts the target label and coordinate to the
negative row of the table.  Spherical duality preserves the trace-dual pairing,
$d_{y^*}=d_y$, and $d_{z^*}=d_z$, so every multiplicity term and the single
$d_z$ factor are carried unchanged.  Multiplying each term of the weighted
identity resolution by the corresponding character equality proves both rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Character-aware $2/2$ slide]
\label{lem:character-aware-two-two}
Let \(m\colon\mathcal H\to\mathcal H'\) be either orientation of a $2/2$
slide, and let \(\xi\in H^2(\mathcal H)\).  Then
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,\xi)
  =\CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H',T_m\xi).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix all labels outside the two participating components.  Write $a$ and $b$
for the moving and stationary character exponents, and expand their Kirby
colours into simple labels $x$ and $y$.  For
$\varepsilon=-1$ in the positive convention and $\varepsilon=+1$ in the
negative convention, use the local terms
$A^\varepsilon_{y,z,\mu}$ and $D^\varepsilon_{y,z,\mu^\sharp}$ of
\cref{lem:weighted-kirby-colour-slide}.  With the fixed exterior labels
suppressed, the fully expanded source and target contributions have the
common categorical coefficient $d_xd_yd_z$ and the form
\[
\begin{aligned}
  Z_{\mathrm{src}}^\varepsilon
    &=\sum_{x,y,z,\mu}d_xd_yd_z\,
      s_{\mathrm{src}}(x,y;a,b)\,
      \operatorname{Cl}_E(A^\varepsilon_{y,z,\mu}),\\
  Z_{\mathrm{tgt}}^\varepsilon
    &=\sum_{x,y,z,\mu}d_xd_yd_z\,
      s_{\mathrm{tgt}}^\varepsilon(x,y,z;a,b)\,
      \operatorname{Cl}_E(D^\varepsilon_{y,z,\mu^\sharp}),
\end{aligned}
\]
where
\[
  s_{\mathrm{src}}(x,y;a,b)=\eta_x^a\eta_y^b
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
  s_{\mathrm{tgt}}^{-1}(x,y,z;a,b)
    &=\eta_x^{a+b}\eta_{z^*}^{b},\\
  s_{\mathrm{tgt}}^{+1}(x,y,z;a,b)
    &=\eta_x^{a-b}\eta_z^{b}.
\end{aligned}
\]
We now compare these sums in three separate steps.

In the source expansion, $y$ is the external stationary label and $z$ is the
inserted fusion channel.  After band transport these roles are exchanged:
$z^*$ in the positive convention (respectively $z$ in the negative
convention) is the external stationary label of the target, while $y$ is the
channel summed over by the inverse resolution.  Hence the source and target
expansions both carry the same product $d_xd_yd_z$; the notation does not
identify their external Kirby-colour labels term by term.

\emph{Attach.}
Insert the complete trace-dual resolution at the clean attaching collar.
This produces every simple channel $z$ and every multiplicity index $\mu$
exactly once, with the single coefficient $d_z$.  The two scalar identities
proved in \cref{lem:character-aware-fusion-attachment} give, term by term,
\[
  s_{\mathrm{src}}=s_{\mathrm{tgt}}^{-1}
  \quad\text{or}\quad
  s_{\mathrm{src}}=s_{\mathrm{tgt}}^{+1},
\]
according to the band orientation.  These are precisely the transported
coordinates of \cref{conv:two-two-coordinates}.

\emph{Finite transport.}
The route is finite by \cref{prop:local-movie-normal-forms}.  The termwise
identity in \cref{lem:weighted-kirby-colour-slide} transports the fusion
insertion, while \cref{lem:finite-coupon-transport} transports its character
coupon.  At a dual bend the coupon is replaced by its mate and its displayed
local exponent changes sign; at a multivalent coupon naturality is applied to
the whole boundary word.  Consequently
\[
  s_{\mathrm{src}}\operatorname{Cl}_E
    (A^\varepsilon_{y,z,\mu})
  =s_{\mathrm{tgt}}^\varepsilon\operatorname{Cl}_E
    (D^\varepsilon_{y,z,\mu^\sharp})
\]
for every $x,y,z,\mu$.

\emph{Detach.}
At the clean detaching collar, compose the transported inclusion with its
trace-dual projection.  Orthogonality removes unequal channels and basis
indices, and
\[
  \sum_{z,\mu}d_z\iota_{z,\mu}\pi_{z,\mu}
  =\operatorname{id}_{x\otimes y^\varepsilon}
\]
recombines the surviving terms into the target Kirby-colour expansion.  The
$d_z$ in this equation is the factor already displayed in the common
coefficient $d_xd_yd_z$; no second factor is inserted.  The map
$\mu\mapsto\mu^\sharp$ is a bijection, so it merely reindexes the target
fusion basis.  Summing the matched identities over $x,y,z,\mu$ and then over
the fixed exterior labels gives
$Z_{\mathrm{src}}^\varepsilon=Z_{\mathrm{tgt}}^\varepsilon$ for both
orientations.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Unnormalized graph sum]
\label{def:unnormalized-graph-sum}
For a one-ended Kirby presentation \(\mathcal H\) and
\(a\in Z^2(\mathcal H)\), define
\[
  \mathcal R_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,a)
  :=\sum_{\lambda}
      \left(\prod_k d_{\lambda_k}\right)
      \operatorname{RT}_{\cC}
        \bigl(S^3,\Gamma(\mathcal H,a;\lambda)\bigr).
  \tag{4.13}\label{eq:raw-graph-sum}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Normalization identity]
\label{lem:normalization-identity}
For every one-ended Kirby presentation \(\mathcal H\) and every
\(a\in Z^2(\mathcal H)\),
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,a)
  =\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)^{1-h_3(\mathcal H)}
   \mathcal R_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,a).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is \cref{def:twisted-handle-evaluator,def:unnormalized-graph-sum}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cochain normal forms for cancelling pairs]
\label{lem:cancellation-cochain-normal-forms}
Let \(\mathcal H\to\mathcal H^+\) be a birth in the isolated standard local
form of \cref{prop:local-movie-normal-forms}.  For a $1$--$2$ birth,
extension by zero on the new primal
$2$-handle sends a source $2$-cocycle to a target $2$-cocycle representing
the transported class.  Conversely, every target $2$-cocycle is cohomologous
to one whose new $2$-handle coordinate is zero.  The same statement applies
to an isolated $1$--$2$ death after the slide reduction in
\cref{item:cancellation-normal-form}.  For a standard $2$--$3$ birth or
death, every cocycle on the side containing the pair has zero coordinate on
the added $2$-handle.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
After the other incidence entries have been cleared by the $2/2$ slides in
\cref{item:cancellation-normal-form}, the handle cochain complex of the
isolated cancelling $1$--$2$ pair contains a direct
summand
\[
  \mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\,\pm1\,}\mathbb Z
\]
in degrees $1$ and $2$.  Extension by zero is the inclusion of the
complementary cochain complex.  Subtracting the coboundary of the appropriate
multiple of the new degree-$1$ basis cochain makes the new degree-$2$
coordinate zero, without changing its cohomology class.

For a cancelling $2$--$3$ pair the corresponding direct summand lies in
degrees $2$ and $3$, again with differential $\pm1$.  The cocycle equation
in degree $3$ therefore forces the new degree-$2$ coordinate itself to be
zero.  These are precisely the two isolated local cancellation complexes.
We do not claim that an arbitrary, non-isolated presentation already splits
before the slide reduction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Local cancellation sums]
\label{lem:local-cancellation-sums}
For the simple-label set of $\cC$,
\[
  \sum_i d_i\,\operatorname{tr}_i(P_{\mathbf1,i})=1,
  \qquad
  \sum_i d_i\,\operatorname{tr}_i(\operatorname{id}_i)
  =\operatorname{Dim}(\cC).                  \tag{4.14}\label{eq:local-cancellation-sums}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $i$ is simple, then $P_{\mathbf1,i}$ is the identity for
$i\cong\mathbf1$ and zero otherwise.  Since $d_{\mathbf1}=1$, the first sum
is $1$.  Spherical trace gives
$\operatorname{tr}_i(\operatorname{id}_i)=d_i$, so the second sum is
$\sum_i d_i^2=\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[$1$--$2$ cancellation identity]
\label{lem:one-two-cancellation-evaluation}
Let \(\mathcal H\) be a one-ended Kirby presentation, and let
\(\mathcal H^+\) be obtained from it by inserting an isolated standard
cancelling \(1\)--\(2\) pair.  For \(a\in Z^2(\mathcal H)\), let
\(a^+=a\oplus0\) be the
transported cocycle of
\cref{prop:marking-coordinate-transport}.  Then
\[
  \mathcal R_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H^+,a^+)
  =\mathcal R_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,a),
  \qquad
  h_3(\mathcal H^+)=h_3(\mathcal H),
  \tag{4.15}\label{eq:one-two-cancellation-evaluation}
\]
and hence
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H^+,a^+)
  =\CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,a).
\]
The reverse equality holds for an isolated \(1\)--\(2\) death.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix every exterior label.  The new \(2\)-handle coordinate is zero, so its
character coupon is
\((\eta^0)_i=\operatorname{id}_i\).  The new dotted circle and its cancelling
meridian contribute exactly
\[
  \sum_i d_i\,\operatorname{tr}_i(P_{\mathbf1,i})=1
\]
by \cref{lem:local-cancellation-sums}.  Thus every fixed exterior summand is
unchanged, proving the first equality in
\eqref{eq:one-two-cancellation-evaluation}.  This birth changes neither the
number of \(3\)-handles nor the normalization.  Substitution into
\eqref{eq:raw-graph-sum} proves the normalized equality.  Reading the same
calculation backwards proves the death statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[$2$--$3$ cancellation identity]
\label{lem:two-three-cancellation-evaluation}
Let \(\mathcal H\) be a one-ended Kirby presentation, and let
\(\mathcal H^+\) be obtained from it by inserting an isolated standard
cancelling \(2\)--\(3\) pair.  For \(a\in Z^2(\mathcal H)\), let
\(a^+=a\oplus0\).  Then
\[
  \mathcal R_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H^+,a^+)
  =\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)\,
   \mathcal R_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,a),
  \qquad
  h_3(\mathcal H^+)=h_3(\mathcal H)+1,
  \tag{4.16}\label{eq:two-three-cancellation-evaluation}
\]
and hence
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H^+,a^+)
  =\CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,a).
\]
The reverse equality holds for an isolated \(2\)--\(3\) death.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The cocycle equation forces the new \(2\)-handle coordinate to vanish, so its
character coupon is the identity.  For every fixed exterior labeling, the
new \(0\)-framed component contributes
\[
  \sum_i d_i\,\operatorname{tr}_i(\operatorname{id}_i)
  =\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)
\]
by \cref{lem:local-cancellation-sums}.  This gives the raw-sum equality in
\eqref{eq:two-three-cancellation-evaluation}.  Meanwhile the new \(3\)-handle
changes the normalization by
\(\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)^{-1}\).  The two factors cancel.  Reversing the
calculation proves the death case.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Three-handle slides]
\label{lem:three-handle-slides}
Fix a twisted categorical input \((\cC,\eta)\).  Let \(\mathcal H'\) be
obtained from a one-ended Kirby presentation \(\mathcal H\) by sliding one
\(3\)-handle over another, and let \(a\in Z^2(\mathcal H)\).  The slide
changes only the chosen basis of \(C_3(\mathcal H)\).  It preserves both
\(h_3(\mathcal H)\) and
\(\operatorname{im}(\partial_3)\).  The same coordinate tuple \(a\)
represents the transported marking, and
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,a)
  =\CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H',a).
  \tag{4.17}\label{eq:three-handle-slide-evaluation}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(A\in\operatorname{GL}(C_3(\mathcal H))\) is the elementary basis change,
the new boundary map is \(\partial_3A\), which has the same image as
\(\partial_3\).  Thus the cocycle condition and its quotient by
\(\operatorname{im}(\partial_2^*)\) are unchanged.  The dotted--framed Kirby
diagram, every character exponent, and every RT summand are unchanged, as is
\(h_3\).  Equivalently, this is exactly the dependence isolated in
\cref{prop:evaluator-three-handle-dependence}.  The two sides of
\eqref{eq:three-handle-slide-evaluation} are therefore the same defining
sum.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Pullback relabelling]
\label{lem:pullback-presentation-evaluation}
Let \(f\colon X\to Y\) be an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism, let
\((\mathcal H',\xi_{\mathcal H'})\) be a marked one-ended Kirby presentation
of \((Y,\alpha')\), and let
\((f^*\mathcal H',\widetilde\xi_{\mathcal H'})\) be its pullback to \(X\).
Then
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}
    (f^*\mathcal H',\widetilde\xi_{\mathcal H'})
  =
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H',\xi_{\mathcal H'}).
  \tag{4.18}\label{eq:pullback-presentation-evaluation}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a handle cocycle representing \(\xi_{\mathcal H'}\).  Transporting
the handles along \(f^{-1}\) gives a cocycle on \(f^*\mathcal H'\) with the
same integer coordinate on every corresponding oriented \(2\)-handle; by
cellular naturality it represents
\(\widetilde\xi_{\mathcal H'}\).  The two presentations have the same
dotted--framed diagram, handle counts, framings, component orientations, and
ordinary label states.  For corresponding label states, the categorical
ribbon expression, every character coupon, and every Kirby-colour
coefficient are therefore identical.  The defining sums agree term by term.
Representative independence, \cref{prop:representative-independence}, removes
the initial choice of cocycle.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Extreme-handle cancellation]
\label{prop:extreme-handle-cancellation}
The general handle-operator evaluation of
\cref{def:general-handle-operator-evaluation} is unchanged by a
\(0\)--\(1\) or \(3\)--\(4\) birth or death.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
These moves do not change \(C_2\), hence they introduce no character coupon.
The two adjacent elementary handle operators are the inverse cancellation
maps of \cite[Definition~5.11 and Proposition~5.13]{Tham2021}.  Their
composite is the identity on the common exterior state space.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[One-ended elementary Kirby--Cerf-move invariance]
\label{thm:elementary-kirby-invariance}
Let
\[
  m\colon(\mathcal H,\xi)\longrightarrow(\mathcal H',\xi')
\]
be a marked Kirby--Cerf move between one-ended Kirby data, chosen from the
nine types that preserve the one-ended condition.  Then
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,\xi)
  =\CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H',\xi').
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose a cocycle $a$ representing $\xi$, and in each case choose the
target representative $a'$ of $\xi'=T_m\xi$ prescribed by
\cref{prop:marking-coordinate-transport}.  By
\cref{prop:representative-independence}, proving the equality for these
representatives proves it for the transported cohomology class.  We use the
local forms of \cref{prop:local-movie-normal-forms}; the exterior graph and
all labels not mentioned below remain fixed.

\emph{Level isotopy.}
Here $a'=a$, the label states are canonically identified, and
\(h_3(\mathcal H')=h_3(\mathcal H)\).  If the move is a level isotopy of a
\(3\)-handle attaching sphere, then the dotted--framed diagram,
\(\operatorname{im}(\partial_3)\), and the defining sum are literally
unchanged.  Otherwise, for every label state $\lambda$, ribbon graphical
coherence \cite[Theorem~6.1]{Shum1994} gives
\[
  \operatorname{RT}_{\cC}
    \bigl(S^3,\Gamma(\mathcal H,a;\lambda)\bigr)
  =
  \operatorname{RT}_{\cC}
    \bigl(S^3,\Gamma(\mathcal H',a';\lambda)\bigr).
\]
The Kirby-colour coefficients agree termwise, so summing proves the desired
equality.

\emph{$1/1$ slides and $2/1$ passages.}
For both moves $a'=a$ on the canonically identified $2$-handle
generators, and $h_3$ is unchanged.  For a $1/1$ slide the required local
equality is exactly \eqref{eq:one-one-index-three-interface}.  For a $2/1$
passage it is \eqref{eq:positive-two-one-interface} or
\eqref{eq:negative-two-one-interface}, according to the crossing orientation.
After applying the common exterior closure and summing over ordinary labels,
\cref{lem:dotted-handle-slides} gives the normalized equality.

\emph{$2/2$ slide.}
Use the positive or negative coordinate rule
\eqref{eq:two-two-marking-transport}.  The attach calculation identifies the
source character factor with the transported target factor; finite transport
preserves each term; and the trace-dual detachment recombines the target
Kirby colour.  Thus
\[
  Z_{\mathrm{src}}^\varepsilon
  =Z_{\mathrm{tgt}}^\varepsilon
\]
by \cref{lem:character-aware-two-two}.  A $2/2$ slide does not change
$h_3$, so this raw equality is the required evaluator equality.

\emph{$3/3$ slide.}
The equality is \eqref{eq:three-handle-slide-evaluation}: the move changes
only the basis of \(C_3\), preserving \(h_3\),
\(\operatorname{im}(\partial_3)\), every cocycle coordinate on \(C_2\), and
the entire dotted--framed ribbon graph.

\emph{$1$--$2$ birth and death.}
For birth, and for death after the explicit $2/2$ slide reduction of
\cref{item:cancellation-normal-form}, the pair is isolated from the exterior.
The marking rule gives $a'=a\oplus0$, and
\eqref{eq:one-two-cancellation-evaluation} proves the isolated equality.
For death, the preceding $2/2$ slides preserve the marked evaluator by
\cref{lem:character-aware-two-two}; representative independence supplies the
zero-coordinate representative, and
\cref{lem:one-two-cancellation-evaluation} is read backwards.

\emph{$2$--$3$ birth and death.}
The cocycle equation gives $a'=a\oplus0$.  The two explicit equalities in
\eqref{eq:two-three-cancellation-evaluation} show that the raw sum acquires
one factor of $\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)$ while the normalization acquires its
inverse.  Their product is $1$.  The death case is the reverse equality.

These are precisely the nine directed moves occurring in a one-ended
connected Cerf movie.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Well-definedness on a marked 4-manifold]
\label{prop:marked-manifold-evaluation-well-defined}
Let \((\mathcal H,\xi)\) and \((\mathcal K,\zeta)\) be marked one-ended
Kirby data
for the same \(H_2\)-marked 4-manifold \((X,\alpha)\).  Then
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,\xi)
  =\CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal K,\zeta).
\]
Thus the presentation-level evaluation depends only on \((X,\alpha)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply marked Kirby--Cerf completeness,
\cref{prop:marked-kirby-cerf}, to the identity marked diffeomorphism of
\((X,\alpha)\).  It gives a finite marked Kirby--Cerf movie from
\((\mathcal H,\xi)\) to \((\mathcal K,\zeta)\), up to the tautological
endpoint identification.  Apply
\cref{thm:elementary-kirby-invariance} at every elementary step, then induct
on the length of the movie.  The endpoint identification changes no term of the
defining sum, by \cref{lem:pullback-presentation-evaluation} with
\(f=\operatorname{id}_X\).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Manifold-level evaluator]
\label{def:manifold-level-evaluator}
For a marked 4-manifold $(X,\alpha)$, choose a marked one-ended Kirby
presentation $(\mathcal H,\xi_{\mathcal H})$ as in
\cref{prop:kirby-presentation-existence,def:marked-kirby-presentation}
and set
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(X,\alpha)
  :=\CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,\xi_{\mathcal H}).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Marked diffeomorphism invariance]
\label{prop:diffeomorphism-invariance}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be closed connected oriented smooth 4-manifolds, let
$f\colon X\to Y$ be an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism, and let
$\alpha\in H_2(X;\mathbb Z)$.  Then
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(X,\alpha)
  =\CY_{\cC,\eta}(Y,f_*\alpha).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose marked one-ended Kirby presentations of the two marked manifolds and
pull the target presentation back along $f$.  It is a marked Kirby datum for
$(X,\alpha)$ by naturality of Poincar\'e duality.  Its value equals the value
of the chosen source datum by
\cref{prop:marked-manifold-evaluation-well-defined}, and it equals the target
value by \cref{lem:pullback-presentation-evaluation}.  This proves the
displayed equality.
\end{proof}
