\subsection{Marked Kirby--Cerf invariance and manifold descent}
\label{subsec:kirby-invariance}

The identity-character evaluator is already invariant under Kirby--Cerf
moves by \cref{rem:identity-character-specialization}.  We prove that the
presentation-level scalar remains unchanged after the transported character
coupons are inserted.  The proof compares the same local ribbon identities;
the only new inputs are the cocycle-coordinate transport of
\cref{prop:marking-coordinate-transport} and the naturality, monoidality, and
duality identities for \(\eta\).

\begin{lemma}[Elementary-move invariance for transported cocycles]
\label{lem:character-compatibility-elementary-moves}
Let
\[
  m\colon(\mathcal H,\beta)
  \longrightarrow(\mathcal H',|m|_*\beta)
\]
be a marked elementary Kirby--Cerf move between one-ended Kirby data.  Let
\(\omega\) represent \(\xi_{\mathcal H,\beta}\), and choose a representative
\(\omega'\) of \(\xi_{\mathcal H',|m|_*\beta}\) whose coordinates satisfy
\cref{prop:marking-coordinate-transport}.  Then
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,\omega)
  =
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H',\omega').
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The nine directed move types available in a one-ended movie divide into
three kinds of character check.  We list every type explicitly.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Moves that do not change the relevant \(2\)-handle
coordinates.}
These are the following four move types.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Level isotopy.}
  Here a level isotopy means a framed ambient isotopy of the attaching data
  inside a regular boundary level, with no handle slide, birth, or death.
  It carries each labelled \(2\)-handle component, together with its coupon
  \((\eta_{\lambda_k})^{\omega_k}\), to the corresponding target component.
  Thus the source and target are framed-isotopic decorated ribbon graphs.
  Their Reshetikhin--Turaev evaluations are equal, while the label weights and
  normalization factor are unchanged.

  \item \textbf{A \(1/1\) slide.}
  Cut open the two dotted handles involved in the slide.  Denote the source
  and target local composites of tensor-unit projectors by
  \(Q_{\mathrm{src}}\) and \(Q_{\mathrm{tgt}}\), and denote by
  \(E_{\lambda,\omega}[-]\) the common exterior ribbon tangle, including all
  labels and character coupons.  The standard local \(1/1\) projector
  identity is an equality of morphisms with the same boundary,
  \[
    Q_{\mathrm{src}}=Q_{\mathrm{tgt}}.
  \]
  To see this equality, expand both tensor-unit projectors in complete simple
  resolutions.  Every term vanishes unless both through-channels are the
  tensor unit.  On the surviving \(\mathbf1\)-channels, the handle slide is
  reduced to the unitors and associators, and monoidal coherence identifies
  the source and target composites.  Thus the equality holds before any
  exterior tangle is attached.
  Applying the same exterior context to both sides gives
  \[
    \operatorname{ev}_{\cC}
      \bigl(E_{\lambda,\omega}[Q_{\mathrm{src}}]\bigr)
    =
    \operatorname{ev}_{\cC}
      \bigl(E_{\lambda,\omega}[Q_{\mathrm{tgt}}]\bigr).
  \]
  The \(2\)-handle labels and cocycle coordinates are canonically identified,
  so this is equality of the decorated source and target summands, not merely
  of their undecorated versions.

  \item \textbf{A \(2/1\) dotted-handle passage.}
  Recall from \cref{def:handle-slide} that this is not a handle slide: it is
  the ambient isotopy of a \(2\)-handle attaching circle which, in dotted
  notation, is drawn by passing one strand through a dotted component.  Cut
  open that dotted handle, let \(B\) be its through-object, let
  \(P_{\mathbf1,B}\colon B\to B\) be the tensor-unit projector, and let the
  moving strand have label \(X\) and exponent \(a\).  For either local
  crossing orientation, write
  \(b\colon B\otimes X\to X\otimes B\) for the corresponding braiding or
  inverse braiding.  The source and target local morphisms are the two sides
  of
  \[
    b\circ\bigl(P_{\mathbf1,B}\otimes(\eta_X)^a\bigr)
    =
    \bigl((\eta_X)^a\otimes P_{\mathbf1,B}\bigr)\circ b.
  \]
  This is the naturality equation for the braiding.  Closing both sides by the
  common exterior tangle proves equality of the decorated local evaluations;
  the label weights and normalization factor are unchanged.

  \item \textbf{A \(3/3\) slide.}
  The slide changes the boundary map \(\partial_3\) by precomposition with an
  elementary automorphism of \(C_3\); in particular,
  \(\operatorname{im}(\partial_3)\subset C_2\) is unchanged.  It changes
  neither the group \(C_2\) with its handle basis nor the dotted--framed
  ribbon graph.  Hence every label \(\lambda_k\), every cocycle coordinate
  \(\omega_k\), and every coupon
  \((\eta_{\lambda_k})^{\omega_k}\) is literally the same on the two sides.
  The number of \(3\)-handles is also unchanged, so the normalization factors
  agree and the two evaluator summands are equal.
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{\(2/2\) slides.}
Let \(x,y\) be the moving and stationary simple labels and let \(a,b\) be
their source cocycle coordinates.  The coordinate transport from
\cref{prop:marking-coordinate-transport} is
\[
  (a,b)\longmapsto(a+b,b)
  \quad\text{or}\quad
  (a,b)\longmapsto(a-b,b),
\]
according to the two orientations.  The two oriented local models are checked
separately:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The positive \(2/2\) slide.}
  Its channel condition is \(z\subset x\otimes y^*\).  Monoidality and
  duality give
  \[
    \eta_x^{a+b}\eta_{z^*}^{b}
    =\eta_x^{a+b}(\eta_x\eta_y^{-1})^{-b}
    =\eta_x^a\eta_y^b,
  \]
  so its character factor is preserved.

  \item \textbf{The negative \(2/2\) slide.}
  Its channels satisfy \(z\subset x\otimes y\), and
\[
  \eta_x^{a-b}\eta_z^b
  =\eta_x^{a-b}(\eta_x\eta_y)^b
  =\eta_x^a\eta_y^b.
\]
  Thus its character factor is preserved as well.
\end{itemize}
Thus every fusion-channel term has exactly the same character factor before
and after attachment.  Naturality transports that factor along the band, and
the trace-dual detachment used in the untwisted proof preserves it.

\item \textbf{Adjacent-index births and deaths.}
These are the following four directed move types.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A \(1\)--\(2\) birth.}
  The transported cohomology class has a representative
  \(\omega'=\omega\oplus0\), so the new coupon is
  \(\eta^0=\operatorname{id}\).

  \item \textbf{A \(1\)--\(2\) death.}
  Cohomological descent permits a representative whose disappearing
  \(2\)-handle coordinate is zero.  Its coupon is therefore the identity
  before the pair is removed.

  \item \textbf{A \(2\)--\(3\) birth.}
  The cocycle equation forces the new \(2\)-handle coordinate to be zero, so
  the new coupon is \(\eta^0=\operatorname{id}\).

  \item \textbf{A \(2\)--\(3\) death.}
  This is the reverse calculation: the cocycle equation forces the
  disappearing \(2\)-handle coordinate, and hence its character exponent,
  to be zero.
\end{itemize}
In all four cases the untwisted cancellation and normalization identities
therefore apply without alteration.
\end{itemize}

For the identity character, the cited handle-decomposition invariance proves
the required local ribbon identity together with its label weights and
normalization factor.  The checks above show, move by move, that inserting the
transported character coupons preserves that identity on every labelled
summand.  Summing over the simple labels therefore gives the asserted equality
of presentation-level scalars.  In a death move, the replacement by a
zero-coordinate source representative does not change the scalar by
\cref{prop:representative-independence}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Elementary marked Kirby--Cerf invariance]
\label{thm:elementary-kirby-invariance}
Let
\[
  m\colon(\mathcal H,\beta)\longrightarrow(\mathcal H',\beta')
\]
be a marked elementary Kirby--Cerf move between one-ended Kirby data.  Then
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,\beta)
  =\CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H',\beta').
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose a source cocycle \(\omega\), and choose a target cocycle \(\omega'\)
as in \cref{prop:marking-coordinate-transport}.  By
\cref{lem:character-compatibility-elementary-moves}, their presentation-level
evaluations are equal.  The definition on cohomology classes and
\cref{prop:representative-independence} identify these two values with the
displayed marked evaluations.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite marked-movie invariance]
\label{cor:finite-marked-movie-invariance}
If a finite marked Kirby--Cerf movie connects
\((\mathcal H,\beta)\) to \((\mathcal K,\gamma)\), then
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,\beta)
  =\CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal K,\gamma).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{thm:elementary-kirby-invariance} at each step and induct on the
length of the movie.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Descent to marked 4-manifolds}
\label{subsubsec:marked-manifold-descent}

\begin{proposition}[Well-definedness on a marked \(4\)-manifold]
\label{prop:marked-manifold-evaluation-well-defined}
Let
\[
  \rho\colon|(\mathcal H,\beta)|\longrightarrow(X,\alpha),
  \qquad
  \sigma\colon|(\mathcal K,\gamma)|\longrightarrow(X,\alpha)
\]
be marked one-ended Kirby presentations of the same marked \(4\)-manifold.
Then
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,\beta)
  =\CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal K,\gamma).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Marked Kirby--Cerf completeness, \cref{prop:marked-kirby-cerf}, supplies a
finite marked movie between the two presentations.  Apply
\cref{cor:finite-marked-movie-invariance}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Manifold-level evaluator]
\label{def:manifold-level-evaluator}
For a marked \(4\)-manifold \((X,\alpha)\), choose a marked one-ended Kirby
presentation
\[
  \rho\colon|(\mathcal H,\beta)|\longrightarrow(X,\alpha)
\]
and define
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(X,\alpha)
  :=\CY_{\cC,\eta}(\mathcal H,\beta).
\]
This is independent of the chosen presentation by the preceding proposition.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Marked diffeomorphism invariance]
\label{prop:diffeomorphism-invariance}
Let
\[
  f\colon(X,\alpha)\longrightarrow(Y,\beta)
\]
be a marked diffeomorphism.  Then
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(X,\alpha)
  =\CY_{\cC,\eta}(Y,\beta).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose marked one-ended Kirby presentations of the source and target.
Marked Kirby--Cerf completeness gives a finite marked movie realizing
\(f\), and \cref{cor:finite-marked-movie-invariance} identifies their
values.
\end{proof}
