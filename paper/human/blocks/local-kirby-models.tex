\subsection{Local models used in the proof}
\label{subsec:local-kirby-models}

The following conventions turn a topological Kirby move into a local ribbon
calculation while keeping the exterior tangle fixed.

\begin{convention}[Auxiliary orientation reversal]
\label{conv:orientation-reversal}
Reversing the chosen orientation of a framed \(2\)-handle simultaneously sends
\[
  e_k\longmapsto-e_k,
  \qquad a_k\longmapsto-a_k,
  \qquad i\longmapsto i^*.
\]
Reversing a dotted \(1\)-handle orientation reverses its cross-section
coorientation and replaces its boundary word by the reversed rigid dual.  Both
operations change only auxiliary oriented bases, not the underlying Kirby
presentation.
\end{convention}

\begin{convention}[Coordinates for a \(2/2\) slide]
\label{conv:two-two-coordinates}
Let \(e_x\) and \(e_y\) be the moving and stationary \(2\)-handle generators,
and let \(a,b\) be the corresponding cocycle coordinates.  For the positive
slide we use
\[
  e_x'=e_x+e_y,
  \qquad (a',b')=(a+b,b),
\]
with fusion channels \(z\subset x\otimes y^*\).  For the negative slide we
use
\[
  e_x'=e_x-e_y,
  \qquad (a',b')=(a-b,b),
\]
with channels \(z\subset x\otimes y\).  These are the handle-basis change and
its dual cochain coordinates.
\end{convention}

\begin{proposition}[Local movie normal forms]
\label{prop:local-movie-normal-forms}
The standard Kirby moves may be arranged as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),ref=\roman*]
  \item\label{item:slide-normal-form}
  After framed isotopy, every slide is supported in a \(3\)-ball and is a
  clean attachment, transport along the stationary component through finitely
  many local ribbon features, and a clean detachment.  For a \(1/1\) slide,
  the target dotted sublink is again an unlink and the source and target admit
  disjoint cross-sections agreeing outside the supporting ball.

  \item\label{item:cancellation-normal-form}
  A birth inserts an isolated standard cancelling pair.  Before a death, the
  remaining handles may be slid away from the pair so that the death removes
  an isolated standard pair.

  \item\label{item:fixed-exterior}
  Outside the supporting neighborhood, the framed tangle and all uninvolved
  components remain fixed.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
These are the standard band-slide and cancelling-pair models of Kirby
calculus; compare \cite[Sections~5.1 and~5.4]{GompfStipsicz1999} and
\cite[Table~2]{BarenzBarrett2018}.  Compact collars at the two ends of a slide
band can be made clean by a small isotopy.  Along the compact stationary
circle, generic position leaves only finitely many crossings, extrema, and
framing twists to pass.  For a cancelling \(1\)--\(2\) pair, slide every other
\(2\)-handle off the cancelling \(1\)-handle; the \(2\)--\(3\) case is the
upside-down analogue.  These reductions take place inside the stated
neighborhoods and leave the exterior fixed.
\end{proof}
