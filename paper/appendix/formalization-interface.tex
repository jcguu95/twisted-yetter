\section{Lean interface and trust boundary}
\label{app:lean-interface}

This appendix is intended to make the Lean formalization auditable through a
human mathematical interface.  It records exact Lean declarations rather than
merely matching names.  The present subsection covers only the public inputs
of the manifold-level invariant.  Later subsections must separately flatten
the category laws, topology axioms, derived proof obligations, public outputs,
and final axiom closures.

The Lean source snapshot inspected here is commit \texttt{dfa0c61}.  A
source-only red-team scan
of
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/Kirby/Final/Semantics/Independent}
reported $19$ explicit axioms and $33$ possible proof fields.  These counts are
conservative syntactic findings, not the axiom closure of the final theorem and
not a judgment that every field is improper.  No kernel audit is claimed in
this subsection.  Its current terminal status is
\texttt{INCONCLUSIVE\_HUMAN\_REVIEW\_REQUIRED}.

\subsection{Public final-theorem inputs}
\label{app:lean-public-inputs}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{I\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]

\item \textbf{Coefficient field.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The Lean construction is parametrized by an algebraically closed field $K$ of
characteristic zero.  The mathematical results in this paper specialize to
$K=\mathbb C$.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
Every public declaration carries the ambient parameters
\begin{verbatim}
{K : Type uK} [Field K] [IsAlgClosed K] [CharZero K]
\end{verbatim}

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These are typeclass assumptions, not project-specific axioms.  The Lean
statement is more general than the paper statement.  Thus this is a faithful
specialization at $K=\mathbb C$, but not a literal statement match.

\item \textbf{Twisted premodular category.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The algebraic input is a premodular fusion category $\cC$ over $K$, together
with a monoidal natural automorphism
$\eta\colon\operatorname{Id}_{\cC}\Rightarrow
\operatorname{Id}_{\cC}$.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
\begin{verbatim}
structure TwistedPremodularCategory
    (K : Type uK) [Field K] [IsAlgClosed K] [CharZero K] where
  category : CategoryTheory.BundledPremodularCategory.{v, uK, uC, uI} K
  character : TensorIdentityCharacter (C := category.C)
\end{verbatim}
The declaration occurs in
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/CategoryTheory/Premodular/Bundled.lean},
lines 446--449.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is a two-field data structure.  Its immediate human meaning agrees with
the paper.  This does not yet certify the claim: the inherited laws inside
\texttt{BundledPremodularCategory} and \texttt{TensorIdentityCharacter} must be
flattened and reviewed in the category-law subsection.

\item \textbf{Four-manifold vocabulary.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The intended input $X$ is a closed, connected, oriented smooth
four-manifold, and it has an underlying topological space.

\emph{Lean declarations.}
\begin{verbatim}
axiom ClosedConnectedOrientedSmoothFourManifold : Type

axiom underlyingTopologicalSpace :
  ClosedConnectedOrientedSmoothFourManifold → TopCat
\end{verbatim}
They occur in \texttt{EntranceAxioms.lean}, lines 332--336, under
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/Kirby/Final/Semantics/Independent}.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These are explicit foundational vocabulary axioms; Lean does not construct
smooth four-manifolds from more primitive objects.  Connectedness is now part
of the primitive type's stated meaning and name, rather than being inferred
indirectly from a later presentation-existence axiom.  This exactly matches
the manifold scope of the paper.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{Oriented diffeomorphism.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For four-manifolds $X$ and $Y$, an input $f$ is an orientation-preserving
diffeomorphism $X\to Y$.  Forgetting smooth structure gives a homeomorphism of
their underlying topological spaces.

\emph{Lean declarations.}
\begin{verbatim}
axiom OrientedDiffeomorphism :
  ClosedConnectedOrientedSmoothFourManifold →
  ClosedConnectedOrientedSmoothFourManifold →
  Type

axiom underlyingHomeomorphism
    {X Y : ClosedConnectedOrientedSmoothFourManifold} :
    OrientedDiffeomorphism X Y →
    (underlyingTopologicalSpace X ≅ underlyingTopologicalSpace Y)
\end{verbatim}
The source is \nolinkurl{EntranceAxioms.lean}, lines 338--347.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
Both are explicit foundational axioms.  Their intended meaning matches the
paper at the level currently stated, but groupoid operations and orientation
compatibility are not visible in these declarations.  Whether downstream
proofs require more than the displayed forgetting map remains part of the
topology-boundary audit.

\item \textbf{Integral second homology and its transport.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For a four-manifold $X$,
\[
  \operatorname{ManifoldH}_2(X)=H_2(X;\mathbb Z).
\]
An oriented diffeomorphism $f\colon X\to Y$ induces the usual
additive-group isomorphism
\[
  f_*\colon H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow H_2(Y;\mathbb Z).
\]

\emph{Lean declarations.}
\begin{verbatim}
noncomputable def ManifoldH₂
    (X : ClosedConnectedOrientedSmoothFourManifold) : AddCommGrpCat :=
  integralSingularH₂Functor.obj (underlyingTopologicalSpace X)

noncomputable def inducedH₂
    {X Y : ClosedConnectedOrientedSmoothFourManifold}
    (f : OrientedDiffeomorphism X Y) :
    ManifoldH₂ X ≃+ ManifoldH₂ Y :=
  CategoryTheory.Iso.addCommGroupIsoToAddEquiv
    (integralSingularH₂Functor.mapIso (underlyingHomeomorphism f))
\end{verbatim}
The source is \nolinkurl{EntranceAxioms.lean}, lines 350--366.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These are derived noncomputable definitions, not new axioms.  They use
Mathlib's integral singular-homology functor and the explicitly supplied
underlying homeomorphism.  This matches the paper's $H_2(X;\mathbb Z)$ input.

\item \textbf{The marked public input.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The manifold-side argument of the invariant is exactly a pair
$(X,\alpha)$ with
$\alpha\in H_2(X;\mathbb Z)$; no Kirby diagram, evaluator, compiler, or proof
certificate is supplied by the caller.

\emph{Lean declaration as used by the public definition.}
\begin{verbatim}
(Xα : Σ X : ClosedConnectedOrientedSmoothFourManifold, ManifoldH₂ X)
\end{verbatim}
This is the dependent sum used in
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/Kirby/Final/TwistedYetter.lean}, lines 173--177.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is ordinary dependent-pair data, not an axiom or proof field.  It matches
the paper's marked input.

\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Transitive algebraic input}
\label{app:lean-category-input}

This subsection expands the category-theoretic content hidden behind I2.  The
purpose is not to reproduce Mathlib's definitions of category, abelian
category, monoidal category, braiding, or rigidity.  It is to expose every
project-defined layer between those standard interfaces and the public pair
$(\cC,\eta)$, so that no evaluator-correctness statement can hide inside the
word ``premodular.''

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{A\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]

\item \textbf{The first-class bundle.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The object $\cC$ packages one category together with compatible instances
making it abelian, $K$-linear, monoidal, rigid, braided, monoidally additive,
monoidally $K$-linear, and premodular.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The structure \texttt{BundledPremodularCategory} has precisely the fields
\begin{verbatim}
C, category, abelian, linear, monoidal,
rigid, braided, monoidalPreadditive,
monoidalLinear, premodular
\end{verbatim}
Its complete declaration is in
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/CategoryTheory/Premodular/Bundled.lean}, lines
19--37.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The first nine fields install standard Mathlib structures and their coherence
laws.  The last field is expanded in A2.  None mentions Kirby diagrams,
graphical evaluation, homology markings, or invariance.  Status:
\texttt{MATCH}, subject to A2--A6.

\item \textbf{Premodularity.}

\emph{Human statement.}
A premodular category over $K$ is a braided ribbon fusion category over an
algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
After listing the ambient Mathlib instances, the complete project-specific
class body is
\begin{verbatim}
class PremodularCategory ...
    extends FusionCategory K C, RibbonCategory C
\end{verbatim}
in \nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/CategoryTheory/Premodular/Basic.lean}, lines
25--31.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is a domain-defining class, not a theorem assumption about the proposed
invariant.  It agrees with the paper's use of a premodular fusion category and
with the convention of \cite[Definition~8.13.1]{EGNO2015}.  Its two parents are
expanded next.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{Fusion structure.}

\emph{Human statement.}
A fusion category is a finite semisimple $K$-linear rigid monoidal category
whose tensor unit is simple.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The class \texttt{FusionCategory} extends
\texttt{FiniteSemisimpleCategory K C} and has the single additional field
\begin{verbatim}
tensorUnit_simple : Simple (tensor unit)
\end{verbatim}
in \nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/CategoryTheory/Fusion/Basic.lean}, lines
25--32.  Here ``tensor unit'' is the source expression \texttt{(one\_ C)}
written without its Unicode glyph.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
Simplicity of the tensor unit is part of the definition of a fusion category,
not a project-specific proof shortcut.  The class assumes the standard
Mathlib linear, rigid, and monoidal interfaces rather than redefining their
coherence equations.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{Finite semisimplicity and its witnesses.}

\emph{Human statement.}
Every morphism space is finite-dimensional over $K$; there are finitely many
isomorphism classes of simple objects; and every object is a finite direct sum
of simple objects.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The class \texttt{FiniteSemisimpleCategory} has exactly three fields:
\begin{verbatim}
hom_finite
finite_simple_skeleton
finite_simple_decomposition
\end{verbatim}
in
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/CategoryTheory/FiniteSemisimple/Basic.lean},
lines 20--26.  The skeleton witness consists of a finite index type, a family
of simple objects, pairwise nonisomorphism, and completeness among all simple
objects.  For every object $X$, the decomposition witness supplies finitely
many simple summands with inclusions and projections satisfying
\[
 p_i\iota_i=\operatorname{id},
 \qquad p_j\iota_i=0\quad(i\ne j),
 \qquad \sum_i\iota_i p_i=\operatorname{id}_X,
\]
with the composition order translated from Lean's diagrammatic convention.
These witness structures are declared in
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/CategoryTheory/FiniteSemisimple/Chosen.lean},
lines 17--52.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The displayed equations are the ordinary finite-biproduct coordinate laws.
They are proof fields because they certify that the supplied inclusions and
projections really form a simple decomposition; they do not assert any
Twisted Yetter calculation.  The public caller does not choose these
coordinates: Lean obtains them noncomputably from the existential class
fields.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{Ribbon structure.}

\emph{Human statement.}
A balancing is a natural automorphism
$\theta\colon\operatorname{Id}_{\cC}\Rightarrow
\operatorname{Id}_{\cC}$ satisfying
\[
 \theta_{X\otimes Y}
 =(\theta_X\otimes\theta_Y)\circ c_{Y,X}\circ c_{X,Y}.
\]
It is ribbon when it is compatible with right duality:
\[
 (\theta_X)^*=\theta_{X^*}.
\]

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The structure \texttt{Balancing} contains the natural isomorphism
\texttt{twist} and the displayed tensor equation.  The structure
\texttt{RibbonStructure} extends it by the displayed duality equation, and
\texttt{RibbonCategory} contains one such ribbon structure.  The declarations
are in \nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/CategoryTheory/Ribbon/Basic.lean}, lines
25--49.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These are precisely domain-defining ribbon laws.  Pivotal and spherical
structures are not supplied as independent fields: they are constructed from
the ribbon structure, with the spherical identity proved in
\texttt{Ribbon/CanonicalSpherical.lean}.  This prevents callers from supplying
an incompatible pivotal structure.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}; the cited
convention is \cite[Definition~8.10.1 and Proposition~8.10.12]{EGNO2015}.

\item \textbf{The character.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The twist parameter is a monoidal natural automorphism
\[
 \eta\colon\operatorname{Id}_{\cC}\Longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Id}_{\cC}.
\]
Thus every object $X$ has an invertible endomorphism $\eta_X$, natural in
$X$, with
\[
 \eta_{X\otimes Y}=\eta_X\otimes\eta_Y,
 \qquad \eta_{\mathbf 1}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbf 1}.
\]

\emph{Lean declaration.}
\begin{verbatim}
structure TensorIdentityCharacter where
  iso : identity functor is naturally isomorphic to itself
  monoidal : the forward natural transformation is monoidal
\end{verbatim}
This is a notation-normalized transcription of the complete two-field
structure in
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/CategoryTheory/Monoidal/IdentityCharacter.lean},
lines 19--22.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
Naturality and invertibility are carried by the natural isomorphism;
monoidality supplies the tensor and unit equations.  The action on duals and
the scalar action on simple objects are proved later as theorems, rather than
being extra character fields.  No universal-grading-group presentation is
assumed.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{Derived choices and dimensions.}

\emph{Human statement.}
After receiving $\cC$, Lean may noncomputably choose representatives of simple
objects and decompositions.  Quantum dimensions are canonical ribbon traces,
and the global dimension is
\[
 \operatorname{Dim}(\cC)
 =\sum_{i\in\operatorname{Irr}(\cC)} d_i^2.
\]

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The definitions \texttt{simpleSkeleton}, \texttt{decomposition},
\texttt{chosenSimpleFamily}, \texttt{quantumDimension}, and
\texttt{globalDimension} occur in
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/CategoryTheory/Premodular/Bundled.lean}.  They are
all definitions or noncomputable choices derived from A3--A5; none is a field
of \texttt{TwistedPremodularCategory}.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These choices affect coordinates used to write finite sums, but not the
public algebraic input.  The only additional literature axiom used by the
final route is the nonvanishing of global dimension; it will be listed in the
separate explicit-axiom subsection.  Status:
\texttt{MATCH\_WITH\_EXPLICIT\_NONVANISHING\_BOUNDARY}.

\end{enumerate}

The transitive project-defined algebraic input therefore contains no
evaluator equality, Kirby-move invariance, representative independence, or
four-manifold realization claim.  The proof fields exposed above define the
standard algebraic domain itself.  The next audit unit is the combinatorial
special Kirby diagram supplied to the evaluator.

\subsection{Combinatorial special Kirby diagrams}
\label{app:lean-kirby-diagram-input}

The Lean object in this subsection is deliberately lower-level than the
paper's \emph{special Kirby representative}: it is the finite combinatorial
diagram before a manifold realization, dotted co-core chart, degree-three
closing datum, or homology marking is attached.  Those later layers must be
audited separately.  This distinction is essential when comparing the two
interfaces.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{K\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]

\item \textbf{Component and strand records.}

\emph{Human statement.}
There are finitely many link components and finitely many strand segments.
Each component is declared either ordinary or dotted, is assigned an
auxiliary orientation, and stores an integer.  Each strand segment records
the component to which it belongs and its local orientation.

\emph{Lean declarations.}
\begin{verbatim}
inductive ComponentRole | ordinary | dotted

structure Component where
  role : ComponentRole
  orientation : StrandOrientation
  integerFraming : Int

structure Strand (componentCount : Nat) where
  component : Fin componentCount
  orientation : StrandOrientation
\end{verbatim}
These occur in
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/Kirby/Final/Semantics/Independent/KirbyDiagram.lean},
lines 24--48.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These are finite data, not proof fields.  Two details require later review.
First, the record assigns an integer framing even to a dotted component,
whereas the paper assigns framings only to ordinary $2$-handle components.
Second, this record does not itself say that every local strand orientation
agrees with the auxiliary orientation of its component.  Status:
\texttt{REVIEW\_REQUIRED\_REDUNDANT\_FRAMING\_AND\_ORIENTATION\_BINDING}.

\item \textbf{Finite link-diagram events.}

\emph{Human statement.}
A vertical sweep of the planar link diagram encounters only five kinds of
combinatorial events: a cup, a cap, a signed crossing, a signed framing twist,
or a planar-isotopy continuation.  A crossing records all four incident
strand segments, including which branch is over and which is under.  General
category-theoretic coupons do not occur in a Kirby link diagram.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is the inductive type \texttt{DiagramEvent} in the same file, lines
57--68.  The transparent definitions \texttt{beforeWord},
\texttt{afterWord}, and \texttt{continuationPairs}, lines 70--104, specify the
ordered boundary strands and the continuation of each branch through an
event.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is explicit finite syntax, not an axiom.  Its birth and death constructors
are planar cups and caps, not Kirby $1$--$2$ births and deaths.  Status:
\texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{The ordered sweep.}

\emph{Human statement.}
At every height of the sweep, Lean records the left-to-right ordered list of
active strand segments.  Passing one event replaces one exact contiguous
subword by that event's output word and leaves a common prefix and suffix
unchanged.  The bottom and top boundary words are empty.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
\texttt{CombinatorialLinkTrace} stores
\begin{verbatim}
activeOrder : Fin (eventCount + 1) -> List (Fin strandCount)
initialBoundaryEmpty
finalBoundaryEmpty
transitionValid
activeWordsNoDuplicates
\end{verbatim}
at lines 233--249.  The predicate \texttt{EventTransition} expanded at lines
110--115 is exactly the common-prefix/common-suffix equation.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The ordered words are actual data; all four displayed conditions are
transparent finite propositions.  They certify a closed planar sweep rather
than merely naming a parser certificate.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{Closed-circle incidence.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The local ports of an event are distinct; a continuation remains on the same
component; the two branches of a cup or cap have opposite local orientations,
while a crossing, twist, or isotopy preserves orientation along each branch;
every strand segment occurs exactly once immediately below some event and
exactly once immediately above some event; and all segments carrying one
component ID form one connected component.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
These are the four proof fields
\begin{verbatim}
eventPortsAreDistinct
eventContinuationsAreWellFormed
everyStrandHasUniqueInputAndOutput
everyComponentIsConnected
\end{verbatim}
of \texttt{CombinatorialLinkTrace}, lines 250--257.  Their full propositions
are transparent definitions at lines 144--202.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These are correctness proof fields, but their mathematical contents are
fully visible and decidable from the finite syntax in principle.  Together
they say that every declared component is one closed circle.  They do not,
however, repair the missing comparison between strand orientation and the
separately stored component orientation noted in K1.  Status:
\texttt{MATCH\_AS\_FINITE\_INCIDENCE}, subject to K1.

\item \textbf{Framing coherence.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For every declared component, its stored integer is the signed sum of all
explicit framing-twist events lying on that component.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The transparent predicate \texttt{FramingEventsMatchIntegerFramings} is
\[
 f(c)=\sum_e \operatorname{twistContribution}(c,e),
\]
and the field \texttt{framingEventsMatchIntegerFraming} supplies its proof;
see lines 204--231 and 258--259.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This visibly binds the stored integer to the diagram syntax, so it is not an
opaque framing certificate.  It currently applies to dotted components as
well as ordinary ones.  Unless downstream definitions prove that the dotted
integer is irrelevant, this is stronger and more data-dependent than the
paper's Kirby input.  Status:
\texttt{REVIEW\_REQUIRED\_DOTTED\_FRAMING}.

\item \textbf{Visible handle data.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The diagram records finite numbers $h_1,h_2,h_3$.  It identifies the $h_1$
primal $1$-handles bijectively with dotted components and the $h_2$ primal
$2$-handles bijectively with ordinary components.  It also stores a homomorphism
\[
 \partial_2\colon \mathbb Z^{h_2}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{h_1}.
\]

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is the complete structure \texttt{SpecialHandleData}, lines 289--300:
\begin{verbatim}
hOne : Nat
hTwo : Nat
hThree : Nat
oneHandleIndex : Fin hOne equiv dotted components
twoHandleIndex : Fin hTwo equiv non-dotted components
boundaryTwo : (Fin hTwo -> Int) ->+ (Fin hOne -> Int)
\end{verbatim}

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The counts, bijections, and boundary map are data, not propositions.  The
bijections force the displayed component roles to have the advertised handle
counts.  By contrast, \texttt{boundaryTwo} is not constrained inside
\texttt{SpecialKirbyDiagram} to equal the signed dotted-link incidence map.
That comparison is deferred to the cut-open layer and must be audited there.
Status: \texttt{INCONCLUSIVE\_UNTIL\_INCIDENCE\_BINDING\_IS\_AUDITED}.

\item \textbf{The diagram object and its deliberate omissions.}

\emph{Human statement.}
A \texttt{SpecialKirbyDiagram} is exactly one finite closed link trace together
with the visible handle data of K6.  ``Special'' means that the surrounding
handle decomposition has one $0$-handle and one $4$-handle, so those two counts
are not stored.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
\begin{verbatim}
structure SpecialKirbyDiagram where
  trace : CombinatorialLinkTrace
  handles : SpecialHandleData trace
\end{verbatim}
This is lines 302--308.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
No manifold, $H_2$ class, dotted co-core chart, $\partial_3$ map, closing
certificate, or evaluation appears in this two-field structure.  Therefore it
does not by itself claim to be the paper's complete special Kirby
representative.  Faithfulness of the full input depends on the subsequent
cut-open, homology, and realization layers.  Status:
\texttt{MATCH\_AS\_LOWER\_LEVEL\_COMBINATORIAL\_SYNTAX\_ONLY}.

\end{enumerate}

This audit unit therefore establishes that the planar trace and its
closed-circle conditions are transparent.  It does not yet certify the
topological meaning of the stored handle boundary, the omitted closing
$3$-handle data, or the relation between this syntax and a four-manifold.

\subsection{Dotted cut-open data}
\label{app:lean-dotted-cut-input}

This subsection audits the second combinatorial layer.  Its objects are
cut-open tangles associated with one fixed \texttt{SpecialKirbyDiagram}; they
are not themselves Kirby diagrams.  In particular, the categorical projector
inserted later by the evaluator must not be confused with the combinatorial
\texttt{projectorHole} marker described below.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]

\item \textbf{One dotted puncture.}

\emph{Human statement.}
A puncture records a dotted component, an ordinary $2$-handle component, the
strand segment entering the cut, the strand segment leaving it, the signed
direction of intersection with the cooriented dotted disk, and its position
in the ordered puncture word.  The two strand segments belong to the stated
ordinary component and have the same local ribbon orientation.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The structure \texttt{DottedPuncture}, in
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/Kirby/Final/Semantics/Independent/CutOpen.lean},
lines 38--55, contains six data fields and five displayed role, incidence, and
orientation proofs.  The two values of \texttt{PunctureDirection} are mapped
by \texttt{incidenceSide} to outbound and inbound occurrences, respectively,
at lines 13--36.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
All five proof fields are short transparent equalities or inequalities.  The
disk-intersection direction decides only whether the occurrence belongs to
the local domain or codomain.  It does \emph{not} choose whether a ribbon is
labelled by $X$ or $X^*$ and does not create a character exponent.  Component
labels are assigned before cutting.  Status:
\texttt{MATCH\_WITH\_DISK\_DIRECTION\_SEPARATED\_FROM\_DUALITY}.

\item \textbf{Cut-open event syntax.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The cut-open tangle uses the same cups, caps, crossings, framing twists, and
planar continuations as the closed link trace, together with one new marker
\texttt{projectorHole}.  Such a marker names one dotted component and carries
the complete ordered list of punctures at that hole.  Its input ports are the
recorded input strands and its output ports are the recorded output strands.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is \texttt{CutOpenEvent}, lines 119--169.  The two transparent predicates
\begin{verbatim}
StrandContinuationsAreWellFormed
CutOpenEventTransition
\end{verbatim}
at lines 179--223 check local component, orientation, and exact-word
continuity.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The hole is finite syntax only.  It contains no category object, projector
morphism, Kirby colour, character, scalar, or evaluation equality.  Status:
\texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{The complete cut-open exterior.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The exterior is a finite closed event trace with a left-to-right active strand
order.  Every transition is locally exact; continuations preserve component
and orientation; event ports and active words have no repetitions; every
strand has one input occurrence and one output occurrence; and every active
strand belongs to an ordinary $2$-handle component.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
These data and proofs are the complete fields of
\texttt{CutOpenExteriorTrace}, lines 273--298.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The correctness fields are transparent finite propositions analogous to K3
and K4.  They describe how the exterior tangle is wired, but do not themselves
assert that it is obtained by cutting the original embedded Kirby link.
That geometric realization is a separate external boundary.  Status:
\texttt{MATCH\_AS\_COMBINATORIAL\_CUT\_SYNTAX\_ONLY}.

\item \textbf{Exactly one projector marker per dotted component.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For every dotted component, the cut-open trace designates one projector-hole
event.  The designated event carries a puncture list belonging to that dotted
component and numbered in list order.  Conversely, every projector-hole event
is one of these designated events, so an ordinary component cannot create an
extra hole.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The structure \texttt{DottedCutData}, lines 312--340, stores the exterior,
the designated event index, and three transparent correctness proofs:
\begin{verbatim}
projectorEvent_is_for
everyProjectorEvent_is_indexed
projectorPunctures_are_indexed
\end{verbatim}

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These fields synchronize one event trace; they do not compare two separately
supplied puncture tables.  Their contents are purely finite and contain no
evaluation assertion.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{The puncture word and completeness theorems.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The ordered puncture word of a dotted component is defined by reading the list
inside its unique designated projector event.  Consequently every dotted
component has exactly one event carrying exactly that word, every projector
event uses such a word, and the word appears as one contiguous block in an
otherwise unchanged active-order context.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
\texttt{DottedCutData.punctureWord} is a noncomputable definition at lines
347--355.  Projector completeness, recorded order, exact ports, and unchanged
exterior context are theorems proved at lines 357--574, notably
\texttt{projectorEventsExactlyMatchDottedComponents}.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These consequences are kernel-checked theorems of C3--C4, not axioms or
additional proof fields.  Thus the evaluator cannot silently choose a second
puncture order.  Status: \texttt{DERIVED}.

\item \textbf{Signed incidence.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For a fixed dotted component and ordinary component, the signed incidence
coefficient is
\[
 N_{\mathrm{out}}-N_{\mathrm{in}},
\]
where $N_{\mathrm{out}}$ and $N_{\mathrm{in}}$ count, respectively, the
outbound and inbound punctures lying on that ordinary component.  Equivalently,
it is the fold over the ordered puncture word that adds $1$ at each outbound
occurrence, subtracts $1$ at each inbound occurrence, and ignores other
ordinary components.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is the definition \texttt{dottedCutBoundaryCoefficient}, lines 368--386.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This integer is computed solely from disk-intersection incidence.  It is not a
choice of $X$ versus $X^*$ and is not itself the character exponent attached
to the ordinary component by the evaluator.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{Binding cut incidence to the handle boundary.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For every primal $1$-handle $i$ and primal $2$-handle $j$, the $(i,j)$ matrix
entry of the stored cellular boundary
$\partial_2\colon C_2\to C_1$ equals the signed incidence from C6 between the
corresponding dotted and ordinary components.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The full second-layer package is
\begin{verbatim}
structure DottedCutPresentation (diagram : SpecialKirbyDiagram) where
  cut : DottedCutData diagram.trace
  incidence_agrees_with_handle_boundary :
    for every one-handle i and two-handle j,
    boundaryTwo(e_j)(i) = dottedCutBoundaryCoefficient(i,j)
\end{verbatim}
This is a notation-normalized transcription of lines 589--604.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The equality is a proof field, but its entire mathematical statement is the
single familiar handle-incidence equation above.  It closes the K6 gap by
preventing an arbitrary stored \texttt{boundaryTwo} from disagreeing with the
cut data.  It does not prove that the combinatorial cut is topologically
realizable; existence of realizable data remains an external topology input.
Status: \texttt{MATCH\_AS\_EXPLICIT\_INCIDENCE\_BINDING}.

\end{enumerate}

The cut-open data therefore fixes one ordered puncture word and one exterior
trace, and it explicitly binds their aggregate incidence to $\partial_2$.
What remains unaudited is the topology-to-graphical assertion that these data
arise from a genuine dotted-handle collar and that plugging the resulting
local morphism into its exterior yields the intended ribbon expression.  That
assertion is not hidden in \texttt{DottedCutPresentation}; it is an explicit
entrance axiom and will be reviewed in the axiom subsection.

\subsection{Homology marking and realization}
\label{app:lean-homology-marking}

The evaluator is written in the handle decomposition turned upside down, as
in Tham's Definition~5.11.  The public manifold class, however, lies in
ordinary singular homology.  The following list makes the intervening
coordinate layer explicit.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{H\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]

\item \textbf{The visible primal boundary and its transpose.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For a diagram $D$, let
\[
 C_2^{\rm pr}(D)=\mathbb Z^{h_2(D)},\qquad
 C_1^{\rm pr}(D)=\mathbb Z^{h_1(D)}.
\]
The diagram stores the cellular boundary
$\partial_2^{\rm pr}\colon C_2^{\rm pr}\to C_1^{\rm pr}$.
With respect to the displayed finite handle bases, Lean defines its transpose
\[
 (\partial_2^{\rm pr})^T\colon
 \mathbb Z^{h_1(D)}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{h_2(D)}
\]
and proves the usual adjointness identity for the integral coordinate pairing.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
These are \texttt{primalBoundaryTwo}, \texttt{transposeAddHom},
\texttt{transposeAddHom\_adjoint}, and \texttt{thamVisibleCoboundary} in
\nolinkurl{Homology.lean}, lines 70--193.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
All are transparent definitions or kernel-checked finite-sum theorems.  No
$3$-handle attaching map is inserted.  Status: \texttt{DERIVED}.

\item \textbf{The visible THAM coefficient space.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The evaluator assigns one integral exponent to every primal $2$-handle.  Thus
its raw coefficient vectors form
\[
 C^2_{\rm vis}(D)=\mathbb Z^{h_2(D)}.
\]
Changing a representative by a visible coboundary gives the quotient
\[
 Q_{\rm vis}(D)=
 \mathbb Z^{h_2(D)}/\operatorname{im}\bigl((\partial_2^{\rm pr})^T\bigr).
\]

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The types are \texttt{ThamTwoHandleCoefficients} and
\texttt{ThamMarkingClass}; the quotient map is \texttt{thamMarkingClass}, in
\nolinkurl{Homology.lean}, lines 195--214.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These are definitions.  Crucially, Lean does \emph{not} identify
$Q_{\rm vis}(D)$ with $H^2(X;\mathbb Z)$.  Because the closing $3$-handle
attaching data are intentionally absent, $Q_{\rm vis}(D)$ may be larger than
the genuine cocycle group.  Status:
\texttt{MATCH\_AS\_AMBIENT\_COORDINATE\_QUOTIENT}.

\item \textbf{A marked cut-open diagram.}

\emph{Human statement.}
A diagram-level marked input consists of a special Kirby diagram $D$, one
cut-open presentation $P$ of $D$, and one class
$\beta\in Q_{\rm vis}(D)$.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
\begin{verbatim}
structure HomologicallyMarkedDiagram where
  diagram : SpecialKirbyDiagram
  cut : DottedCutPresentation diagram
  thamAlpha : ThamMarkingClass diagram
\end{verbatim}
This is \nolinkurl{Homology.lean}, lines 216--220.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is data only.  It does not claim that $\beta$ is the Poincaré dual of a
manifold homology class.  Status: \texttt{MATCH\_AS\_DIAGRAM\_LEVEL\_DATA}.

\item \textbf{Selecting the genuine Poincaré-dual classes.}

\emph{Human statement.}
If $D$ presents $X$, the external relation
\[
 \operatorname{RepresentsPoincareDualClass}(D,\beta,\alpha)
\]
means that $\beta\in Q_{\rm vis}(D)$ is the turned-over handle-coordinate
class of $\operatorname{PD}(\alpha)$ for
$\alpha\in H_2(X;\mathbb Z)$.  It does not say that every element of
$Q_{\rm vis}(D)$ is genuine cohomology.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is the explicit axiom \texttt{RepresentsPoincareDualClass} in
\nolinkurl{EntranceAxioms.lean}, lines 388--404.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is a category-neutral topology boundary, necessitated by the deliberate
omission of $3$-handle attaching spheres.  It is an axiom and will reappear in
the complete axiom ledger.  Status: \texttt{EXTERNAL\_TOPOLOGY\_INPUT}.

\item \textbf{A representative of a marked manifold.}

\emph{Human statement.}
A special Kirby representative of $X$ consists of a diagram $D$, cut-open
data $P$, a proof that $D$ presents $X$, and an ambient THAM class
$\beta\in Q_{\rm vis}(D)$.  Relative to a chosen
$\alpha\in H_2(X;\mathbb Z)$, correctness is the separate proposition that
$\beta$ represents $\operatorname{PD}(\alpha)$.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is \texttt{SpecialKirbyRepresentativeOf X} and the predicate
\texttt{representsHomologyClass}, in \nolinkurl{EntranceAxioms.lean}, lines
406--426.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The structure contains no hidden correctness field beyond the explicitly
named presentation relation.  Correctness for $\alpha$ is not baked into the
structure and must be supplied separately.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{Transport under an elementary move.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For a move $D\to D'$, Lean stores homomorphisms
\[
 T_1\colon\mathbb Z^{h_1(D)}\to\mathbb Z^{h_1(D')},\qquad
 T_2\colon\mathbb Z^{h_2(D)}\to\mathbb Z^{h_2(D')}
\]
satisfying
\[
 T_2(\partial_2(D))^T=(\partial_2(D'))^T T_1.
\]
Therefore $T_2$ descends to a homomorphism
$Q_{\rm vis}(D)\to Q_{\rm vis}(D')$.  The target marking carried by the move
must equal the image of the source marking under this quotient map.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is \texttt{ThamHandleChainTransport}, its derived
\texttt{inducedMarking}, and the \texttt{markingTransport} field of every
\texttt{ElementaryMove}; see \nolinkurl{Moves.lean}, lines 239--290 and
570--656.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The commuting square and endpoint equality are proof fields.  Their complete
statements are visible and contain no evaluator equality.  They certify the
marked move rather than derive it from a raw unmarked move.  Status:
\texttt{MATCH\_AS\_MARKED\_MOVE\_DATA}.

\item \textbf{Birth and death coordinates.}

\emph{Human statement.}
When a move adds one primal $2$-handle, Lean identifies the target basis with
the old basis plus one new coordinate, preserves every old coefficient, and
assigns coefficient zero to the new handle.  When a move removes a
$2$-handle, it first chooses a homologous representative whose disappearing
coordinate is zero, then transports the surviving coordinates.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
These are \texttt{AddedTwoHandleCoordinates} and
\texttt{RemovedTwoHandleCoordinates}, in \nolinkurl{Moves.lean}, lines
291--387.  Their later \texttt{Matches...Component} predicates bind the
abstract coordinate equivalence to the literal newly added or removed
component.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These are proof-bearing coordinate normal forms carried by cancellation
moves.  The death structure's \texttt{safeCoefficients\_sameClass} is exactly
the representative replacement that must later invoke representative
independence of the evaluator; it is not itself an evaluation equality.
Status: \texttt{MATCH\_WITH\_VISIBLE\_COORDINATE\_CERTIFICATES}.

\end{enumerate}

The homology layer therefore does not replace manifold $H_2$ by a fixed copy
of $\mathbb Z$, nor does it mistake the visible quotient for all of $H^2$.
The two genuinely external statements are the realization of a diagram as a
closed four-manifold and the identification of the selected THAM class with
$\operatorname{PD}(\alpha)$; both will be listed explicitly below.
