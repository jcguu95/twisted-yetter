\section{Lean interface and trust boundary}
\label{app:lean-interface}

This appendix makes the Lean formalization auditable through a human
mathematical interface.  It records exact declarations and follows the entire
active route: public inputs, inherited category laws, Kirby and cut-open data,
homology markings, the graphical evaluator, all eight elementary moves,
representative independence, finite-movie descent, public outputs, explicit
axioms, structure proof fields, and the final kernel dependency closure.

The Lean source snapshot inspected here is commit \texttt{147a434}.  A
source-only red-team scan of
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/Kirby/Final/Semantics/Independent}
reported $19$ explicit axioms and $33$ possible proof fields.  ``Possible'' is
deliberately conservative: the scanner flags every proposition-valued
structure field, including elementary finite-list well-formedness certificates.
The fields are flattened below rather than silently treated as harmless.  A
separate kernel audit was run on the final theorem and its two principal proof
components; its exact closure is recorded at the end of this appendix.

\subsection{Public final-theorem inputs}
\label{app:lean-public-inputs}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{I\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]

\item \textbf{Coefficient field.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The Lean construction is parametrized by an algebraically closed field $K$ of
characteristic zero.  The mathematical results in this paper specialize to
$K=\mathbb C$.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
Every public declaration carries the ambient parameters
\begin{verbatim}
{K : Type uK} [Field K] [IsAlgClosed K] [CharZero K]
\end{verbatim}

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These are typeclass assumptions, not project-specific axioms.  The Lean
statement is more general than the paper statement.  Thus this is a faithful
specialization at $K=\mathbb C$, but not a literal statement match.

\item \textbf{Twisted premodular category.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The algebraic input is a premodular fusion category $\cC$ over $K$, together
with a monoidal natural automorphism
$\eta\colon\operatorname{Id}_{\cC}\Rightarrow
\operatorname{Id}_{\cC}$.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
\begin{verbatim}
structure TwistedPremodularCategory
    (K : Type uK) [Field K] [IsAlgClosed K] [CharZero K] where
  category : CategoryTheory.BundledPremodularCategory.{v, uK, uC, uI} K
  character : TensorIdentityCharacter (C := category.C)
\end{verbatim}
The declaration occurs in
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/CategoryTheory/Premodular/Bundled.lean},
lines 446--449.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is a two-field data structure.  Its immediate human meaning agrees with
the paper.  This does not yet certify the claim: the inherited laws inside
\texttt{BundledPremodularCategory} and \texttt{TensorIdentityCharacter} must be
flattened and reviewed in the category-law subsection.

\item \textbf{Four-manifold vocabulary.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The intended input $X$ is a closed, connected, oriented smooth
four-manifold, and it has an underlying topological space.

\emph{Lean declarations.}
\begin{verbatim}
axiom ClosedConnectedOrientedSmoothFourManifold : Type

axiom underlyingTopologicalSpace :
  ClosedConnectedOrientedSmoothFourManifold → TopCat
\end{verbatim}
They occur in \texttt{EntranceAxioms.lean}, lines 332--336, under
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/Kirby/Final/Semantics/Independent}.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These are explicit foundational vocabulary axioms; Lean does not construct
smooth four-manifolds from more primitive objects.  Connectedness is now part
of the primitive type's stated meaning and name, rather than being inferred
indirectly from a later presentation-existence axiom.  This exactly matches
the manifold scope of the paper.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{Oriented diffeomorphism.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For four-manifolds $X$ and $Y$, an input $f$ is an orientation-preserving
diffeomorphism $X\to Y$.  Forgetting smooth structure gives a homeomorphism of
their underlying topological spaces.

\emph{Lean declarations.}
\begin{verbatim}
axiom OrientedDiffeomorphism :
  ClosedConnectedOrientedSmoothFourManifold →
  ClosedConnectedOrientedSmoothFourManifold →
  Type

axiom underlyingHomeomorphism
    {X Y : ClosedConnectedOrientedSmoothFourManifold} :
    OrientedDiffeomorphism X Y →
    (underlyingTopologicalSpace X ≅ underlyingTopologicalSpace Y)
\end{verbatim}
The source is \nolinkurl{EntranceAxioms.lean}, lines 338--347.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
Both are explicit foundational axioms.  Their intended meaning matches the
paper at the level currently stated, but groupoid operations and orientation
compatibility are not visible in these declarations.  Whether downstream
proofs require more than the displayed forgetting map remains part of the
topology-boundary audit.

\item \textbf{Integral second homology and its transport.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For a four-manifold $X$,
\[
  \operatorname{ManifoldH}_2(X)=H_2(X;\mathbb Z).
\]
An oriented diffeomorphism $f\colon X\to Y$ induces the usual
additive-group isomorphism
\[
  f_*\colon H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow H_2(Y;\mathbb Z).
\]

\emph{Lean declarations.}
\begin{verbatim}
noncomputable def ManifoldH₂
    (X : ClosedConnectedOrientedSmoothFourManifold) : AddCommGrpCat :=
  integralSingularH₂Functor.obj (underlyingTopologicalSpace X)

noncomputable def inducedH₂
    {X Y : ClosedConnectedOrientedSmoothFourManifold}
    (f : OrientedDiffeomorphism X Y) :
    ManifoldH₂ X ≃+ ManifoldH₂ Y :=
  CategoryTheory.Iso.addCommGroupIsoToAddEquiv
    (integralSingularH₂Functor.mapIso (underlyingHomeomorphism f))
\end{verbatim}
The source is \nolinkurl{EntranceAxioms.lean}, lines 350--366.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These are derived noncomputable definitions, not new axioms.  They use
Mathlib's integral singular-homology functor and the explicitly supplied
underlying homeomorphism.  This matches the paper's $H_2(X;\mathbb Z)$ input.

\item \textbf{The marked public input.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The manifold-side argument of the invariant is exactly a pair
$(X,\alpha)$ with
$\alpha\in H_2(X;\mathbb Z)$; no Kirby diagram, evaluator, compiler, or proof
certificate is supplied by the caller.

\emph{Lean declaration as used by the public definition.}
\begin{verbatim}
(Xα : Σ X : ClosedConnectedOrientedSmoothFourManifold, ManifoldH₂ X)
\end{verbatim}
This is the dependent sum used in
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/Kirby/Final/TwistedYetter.lean}, lines 173--177.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is ordinary dependent-pair data, not an axiom or proof field.  It matches
the paper's marked input.

\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Transitive algebraic input}
\label{app:lean-category-input}

This subsection expands the category-theoretic content hidden behind I2.  The
purpose is not to reproduce Mathlib's definitions of category, abelian
category, monoidal category, braiding, or rigidity.  It is to expose every
project-defined layer between those standard interfaces and the public pair
$(\cC,\eta)$, so that no evaluator-correctness statement can hide inside the
word ``premodular.''

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{A\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]

\item \textbf{The first-class bundle.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The object $\cC$ packages one category together with compatible instances
making it abelian, $K$-linear, monoidal, rigid, braided, monoidally additive,
monoidally $K$-linear, and premodular.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The structure \texttt{BundledPremodularCategory} has precisely the fields
\begin{verbatim}
C, category, abelian, linear, monoidal,
rigid, braided, monoidalPreadditive,
monoidalLinear, premodular
\end{verbatim}
Its complete declaration is in
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/CategoryTheory/Premodular/Bundled.lean}, lines
19--37.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The first nine fields install standard Mathlib structures and their coherence
laws.  The last field is expanded in A2.  None mentions Kirby diagrams,
graphical evaluation, homology markings, or invariance.  Status:
\texttt{MATCH}, subject to A2--A6.

\item \textbf{Premodularity.}

\emph{Human statement.}
A premodular category over $K$ is a braided ribbon fusion category over an
algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
After listing the ambient Mathlib instances, the complete project-specific
class body is
\begin{verbatim}
class PremodularCategory ...
    extends FusionCategory K C, RibbonCategory C
\end{verbatim}
in \nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/CategoryTheory/Premodular/Basic.lean}, lines
25--31.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is a domain-defining class, not a theorem assumption about the proposed
invariant.  It agrees with the paper's use of a premodular fusion category and
with the convention of \cite[Definition~8.13.1]{EGNO2015}.  Its two parents are
expanded next.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{Fusion structure.}

\emph{Human statement.}
A fusion category is a finite semisimple $K$-linear rigid monoidal category
whose tensor unit is simple.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The class \texttt{FusionCategory} extends
\texttt{FiniteSemisimpleCategory K C} and has the single additional field
\begin{verbatim}
tensorUnit_simple : Simple (tensor unit)
\end{verbatim}
in \nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/CategoryTheory/Fusion/Basic.lean}, lines
25--32.  Here ``tensor unit'' is the source expression \texttt{(one\_ C)}
written without its Unicode glyph.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
Simplicity of the tensor unit is part of the definition of a fusion category,
not a project-specific proof shortcut.  The class assumes the standard
Mathlib linear, rigid, and monoidal interfaces rather than redefining their
coherence equations.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{Finite semisimplicity and its witnesses.}

\emph{Human statement.}
Every morphism space is finite-dimensional over $K$; there are finitely many
isomorphism classes of simple objects; and every object is a finite direct sum
of simple objects.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The class \texttt{FiniteSemisimpleCategory} has exactly three fields:
\begin{verbatim}
hom_finite
finite_simple_skeleton
finite_simple_decomposition
\end{verbatim}
in
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/CategoryTheory/FiniteSemisimple/Basic.lean},
lines 20--26.  The skeleton witness consists of a finite index type, a family
of simple objects, pairwise nonisomorphism, and completeness among all simple
objects.  For every object $X$, the decomposition witness supplies finitely
many simple summands with inclusions and projections satisfying
\[
 p_i\iota_i=\operatorname{id},
 \qquad p_j\iota_i=0\quad(i\ne j),
 \qquad \sum_i\iota_i p_i=\operatorname{id}_X,
\]
with the composition order translated from Lean's diagrammatic convention.
These witness structures are declared in
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/CategoryTheory/FiniteSemisimple/Chosen.lean},
lines 17--52.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The displayed equations are the ordinary finite-biproduct coordinate laws.
They are proof fields because they certify that the supplied inclusions and
projections really form a simple decomposition; they do not assert any
Twisted Yetter calculation.  The public caller does not choose these
coordinates: Lean obtains them noncomputably from the existential class
fields.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{Ribbon structure.}

\emph{Human statement.}
A balancing is a natural automorphism
$\theta\colon\operatorname{Id}_{\cC}\Rightarrow
\operatorname{Id}_{\cC}$ satisfying
\[
 \theta_{X\otimes Y}
 =(\theta_X\otimes\theta_Y)\circ c_{Y,X}\circ c_{X,Y}.
\]
It is ribbon when it is compatible with right duality:
\[
 (\theta_X)^*=\theta_{X^*}.
\]

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The structure \texttt{Balancing} contains the natural isomorphism
\texttt{twist} and the displayed tensor equation.  The structure
\texttt{RibbonStructure} extends it by the displayed duality equation, and
\texttt{RibbonCategory} contains one such ribbon structure.  The declarations
are in \nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/CategoryTheory/Ribbon/Basic.lean}, lines
25--49.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These are precisely domain-defining ribbon laws.  Pivotal and spherical
structures are not supplied as independent fields: they are constructed from
the ribbon structure, with the spherical identity proved in
\nolinkurl{Ribbon/CanonicalSpherical.lean}.  This prevents callers from supplying
an incompatible pivotal structure.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}; the cited
convention is \cite[Definition~8.10.1 and Proposition~8.10.12]{EGNO2015}.

\item \textbf{The character.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The twist parameter is a monoidal natural automorphism
\[
 \eta\colon\operatorname{Id}_{\cC}\Longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Id}_{\cC}.
\]
Thus every object $X$ has an invertible endomorphism $\eta_X$, natural in
$X$, with
\[
 \eta_{X\otimes Y}=\eta_X\otimes\eta_Y,
 \qquad \eta_{\mathbf 1}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbf 1}.
\]

\emph{Lean declaration.}
\begin{verbatim}
structure TensorIdentityCharacter where
  iso : identity functor is naturally isomorphic to itself
  monoidal : the forward natural transformation is monoidal
\end{verbatim}
This is a notation-normalized transcription of the complete two-field
structure in
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/CategoryTheory/Monoidal/IdentityCharacter.lean},
lines 19--22.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
Naturality and invertibility are carried by the natural isomorphism;
monoidality supplies the tensor and unit equations.  The action on duals and
the scalar action on simple objects are proved later as theorems, rather than
being extra character fields.  No universal-grading-group presentation is
assumed.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{Derived choices and dimensions.}

\emph{Human statement.}
After receiving $\cC$, Lean may noncomputably choose representatives of simple
objects and decompositions.  Quantum dimensions are canonical ribbon traces,
and the global dimension is
\[
 \operatorname{Dim}(\cC)
 =\sum_{i\in\operatorname{Irr}(\cC)} d_i^2.
\]

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The definitions \texttt{simpleSkeleton}, \texttt{decomposition},
\texttt{chosenSimpleFamily}, \texttt{quantumDimension}, and
\texttt{globalDimension} occur in
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/CategoryTheory/Premodular/Bundled.lean}.  They are
all definitions or noncomputable choices derived from A3--A5; none is a field
of \texttt{TwistedPremodularCategory}.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These choices affect coordinates used to write finite sums, but not the
public algebraic input.  The only additional literature axiom used by the
final route is the nonvanishing of global dimension; it will be listed in the
separate explicit-axiom subsection.  Status:
\nolinkurl{MATCH_WITH_EXPLICIT_NONVANISHING_BOUNDARY}.

\end{enumerate}

The transitive project-defined algebraic input therefore contains no
evaluator equality, Kirby-move invariance, representative independence, or
four-manifold realization claim.  The proof fields exposed above define the
standard algebraic domain itself.  The next audit unit is the combinatorial
special Kirby diagram supplied to the evaluator.

\subsection{Combinatorial special Kirby diagrams}
\label{app:lean-kirby-diagram-input}

The Lean object in this subsection is deliberately lower-level than the
paper's \emph{special Kirby representative}: it is the finite combinatorial
diagram before a manifold realization, dotted co-core chart, degree-three
closing datum, or homology marking is attached.  Those later layers must be
audited separately.  This distinction is essential when comparing the two
interfaces.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{K\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]

\item \textbf{Component and strand records.}

\emph{Human statement.}
There are finitely many link components and finitely many strand segments.
Each component is declared either ordinary or dotted, is assigned an
auxiliary orientation, and stores an integer.  Each strand segment records
the component to which it belongs and its local orientation.

\emph{Lean declarations.}
\begin{verbatim}
inductive ComponentRole | ordinary | dotted

structure Component where
  role : ComponentRole
  orientation : StrandOrientation
  integerFraming : Int

structure Strand (componentCount : Nat) where
  component : Fin componentCount
  orientation : StrandOrientation
\end{verbatim}
These occur in
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/Kirby/Final/Semantics/Independent/KirbyDiagram.lean},
lines 24--48.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These are finite data, not proof fields.  Two details require later review.
First, the record assigns an integer framing even to a dotted component,
whereas the paper assigns framings only to ordinary $2$-handle components.
Second, this record does not itself say that every local strand orientation
agrees with the auxiliary orientation of its component.  Status:
\nolinkurl{REVIEW_REQUIRED_REDUNDANT_FRAMING_AND_ORIENTATION_BINDING}.

\item \textbf{Finite link-diagram events.}

\emph{Human statement.}
A vertical sweep of the planar link diagram encounters only five kinds of
combinatorial events: a cup, a cap, a signed crossing, a signed framing twist,
or a planar-isotopy continuation.  A crossing records all four incident
strand segments, including which branch is over and which is under.  General
category-theoretic coupons do not occur in a Kirby link diagram.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is the inductive type \texttt{DiagramEvent} in the same file, lines
57--68.  The transparent definitions \texttt{beforeWord},
\texttt{afterWord}, and \texttt{continuationPairs}, lines 70--104, specify the
ordered boundary strands and the continuation of each branch through an
event.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is explicit finite syntax, not an axiom.  Its birth and death constructors
are planar cups and caps, not Kirby $1$--$2$ births and deaths.  Status:
\texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{The ordered sweep.}

\emph{Human statement.}
At every height of the sweep, Lean records the left-to-right ordered list of
active strand segments.  Passing one event replaces one exact contiguous
subword by that event's output word and leaves a common prefix and suffix
unchanged.  The bottom and top boundary words are empty.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
\texttt{CombinatorialLinkTrace} stores
\begin{verbatim}
activeOrder : Fin (eventCount + 1) -> List (Fin strandCount)
initialBoundaryEmpty
finalBoundaryEmpty
transitionValid
activeWordsNoDuplicates
\end{verbatim}
at lines 233--249.  The predicate \texttt{EventTransition} expanded at lines
110--115 is exactly the common-prefix/common-suffix equation.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The ordered words are actual data; all four displayed conditions are
transparent finite propositions.  They certify a closed planar sweep rather
than merely naming a parser certificate.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{Closed-circle incidence.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The local ports of an event are distinct; a continuation remains on the same
component; the two branches of a cup or cap have opposite local orientations,
while a crossing, twist, or isotopy preserves orientation along each branch;
every strand segment occurs exactly once immediately below some event and
exactly once immediately above some event; and all segments carrying one
component ID form one connected component.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
These are the four proof fields
\begin{verbatim}
eventPortsAreDistinct
eventContinuationsAreWellFormed
everyStrandHasUniqueInputAndOutput
everyComponentIsConnected
\end{verbatim}
of \texttt{CombinatorialLinkTrace}, lines 250--257.  Their full propositions
are transparent definitions at lines 144--202.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These are correctness proof fields, but their mathematical contents are
fully visible and decidable from the finite syntax in principle.  Together
they say that every declared component is one closed circle.  They do not,
however, repair the missing comparison between strand orientation and the
separately stored component orientation noted in K1.  Status:
\nolinkurl{MATCH_AS_FINITE_INCIDENCE}, subject to K1.

\item \textbf{Framing coherence.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For every declared component, its stored integer is the signed sum of all
explicit framing-twist events lying on that component.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The transparent predicate \nolinkurl{FramingEventsMatchIntegerFramings} is
\[
 f(c)=\sum_e \operatorname{twistContribution}(c,e),
\]
and the field \nolinkurl{framingEventsMatchIntegerFraming} supplies its proof;
see lines 204--231 and 258--259.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This visibly binds the stored integer to the diagram syntax, so it is not an
opaque framing certificate.  It currently applies to dotted components as
well as ordinary ones.  Unless downstream definitions prove that the dotted
integer is irrelevant, this is stronger and more data-dependent than the
paper's Kirby input.  Status:
\nolinkurl{REVIEW_REQUIRED_DOTTED_FRAMING}.

\item \textbf{Visible handle data.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The diagram records finite numbers $h_1,h_2,h_3$.  It identifies the $h_1$
primal $1$-handles bijectively with dotted components and the $h_2$ primal
$2$-handles bijectively with ordinary components.  It also stores a homomorphism
\[
 \partial_2\colon \mathbb Z^{h_2}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{h_1}.
\]

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is the complete structure \texttt{SpecialHandleData}, lines 289--300:
\begin{verbatim}
hOne : Nat
hTwo : Nat
hThree : Nat
oneHandleIndex : Fin hOne equiv dotted components
twoHandleIndex : Fin hTwo equiv non-dotted components
boundaryTwo : (Fin hTwo -> Int) ->+ (Fin hOne -> Int)
\end{verbatim}

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The counts, bijections, and boundary map are data, not propositions.  The
bijections force the displayed component roles to have the advertised handle
counts.  By contrast, \texttt{boundaryTwo} is not constrained inside
\texttt{SpecialKirbyDiagram} to equal the signed dotted-link incidence map.
That comparison is deferred to the cut-open layer and must be audited there.
Status: \nolinkurl{INCONCLUSIVE_UNTIL_INCIDENCE_BINDING_IS_AUDITED}.

\item \textbf{The diagram object and its deliberate omissions.}

\emph{Human statement.}
A \texttt{SpecialKirbyDiagram} is exactly one finite closed link trace together
with the visible handle data of K6.  ``Special'' means that the surrounding
handle decomposition has one $0$-handle and one $4$-handle, so those two counts
are not stored.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
\begin{verbatim}
structure SpecialKirbyDiagram where
  trace : CombinatorialLinkTrace
  handles : SpecialHandleData trace
\end{verbatim}
This is lines 302--308.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
No manifold, $H_2$ class, dotted co-core chart, $\partial_3$ map, closing
certificate, or evaluation appears in this two-field structure.  Therefore it
does not by itself claim to be the paper's complete special Kirby
representative.  Faithfulness of the full input depends on the subsequent
cut-open, homology, and realization layers.  Status:
\nolinkurl{MATCH_AS_LOWER_LEVEL_COMBINATORIAL_SYNTAX_ONLY}.

\end{enumerate}

This audit unit therefore establishes that the planar trace and its
closed-circle conditions are transparent.  It does not yet certify the
topological meaning of the stored handle boundary, the omitted closing
$3$-handle data, or the relation between this syntax and a four-manifold.

\subsection{Dotted cut-open data}
\label{app:lean-dotted-cut-input}

This subsection audits the second combinatorial layer.  Its objects are
cut-open tangles associated with one fixed \texttt{SpecialKirbyDiagram}; they
are not themselves Kirby diagrams.  In particular, the categorical projector
inserted later by the evaluator must not be confused with the combinatorial
\texttt{projectorHole} marker described below.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]

\item \textbf{One dotted puncture.}

\emph{Human statement.}
A puncture records a dotted component, an ordinary $2$-handle component, the
strand segment entering the cut, the strand segment leaving it, the signed
direction of intersection with the cooriented dotted disk, and its position
in the ordered puncture word.  The two strand segments belong to the stated
ordinary component and have the same local ribbon orientation.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The structure \texttt{DottedPuncture}, in
\nolinkurl{lean/TwistedYetter/Kirby/Final/Semantics/Independent/CutOpen.lean},
lines 38--55, contains six data fields and five displayed role, incidence, and
orientation proofs.  The two values of \texttt{PunctureDirection} are mapped
by \texttt{incidenceSide} to outbound and inbound occurrences, respectively,
at lines 13--36.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
All five proof fields are short transparent equalities or inequalities.  The
disk-intersection direction decides only whether the occurrence belongs to
the local domain or codomain.  It does \emph{not} choose whether a ribbon is
labelled by $X$ or $X^*$ and does not create a character exponent.  Component
labels are assigned before cutting.  Status:
\nolinkurl{MATCH_WITH_DISK_DIRECTION_SEPARATED_FROM_DUALITY}.

\item \textbf{Cut-open event syntax.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The cut-open tangle uses the same cups, caps, crossings, framing twists, and
planar continuations as the closed link trace, together with one new marker
\texttt{projectorHole}.  Such a marker names one dotted component and carries
the complete ordered list of punctures at that hole.  Its input ports are the
recorded input strands and its output ports are the recorded output strands.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is \texttt{CutOpenEvent}, lines 119--169.  The two transparent predicates
\begin{verbatim}
StrandContinuationsAreWellFormed
CutOpenEventTransition
\end{verbatim}
at lines 179--223 check local component, orientation, and exact-word
continuity.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The hole is finite syntax only.  It contains no category object, projector
morphism, Kirby colour, character, scalar, or evaluation equality.  Status:
\texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{The complete cut-open exterior.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The exterior is a finite closed event trace with a left-to-right active strand
order.  Every transition is locally exact; continuations preserve component
and orientation; event ports and active words have no repetitions; every
strand has one input occurrence and one output occurrence; and every active
strand belongs to an ordinary $2$-handle component.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
These data and proofs are the complete fields of
\texttt{CutOpenExteriorTrace}, lines 273--298.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The correctness fields are transparent finite propositions analogous to K3
and K4.  They describe how the exterior tangle is wired, but do not themselves
assert that it is obtained by cutting the original embedded Kirby link.
That geometric realization is a separate external boundary.  Status:
\nolinkurl{MATCH_AS_COMBINATORIAL_CUT_SYNTAX_ONLY}.

\item \textbf{Exactly one projector marker per dotted component.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For every dotted component, the cut-open trace designates one projector-hole
event.  The designated event carries a puncture list belonging to that dotted
component and numbered in list order.  Conversely, every projector-hole event
is one of these designated events, so an ordinary component cannot create an
extra hole.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The structure \texttt{DottedCutData}, lines 312--340, stores the exterior,
the designated event index, and three transparent correctness proofs:
\begin{verbatim}
projectorEvent_is_for
everyProjectorEvent_is_indexed
projectorPunctures_are_indexed
\end{verbatim}

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These fields synchronize one event trace; they do not compare two separately
supplied puncture tables.  Their contents are purely finite and contain no
evaluation assertion.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{The puncture word and completeness theorems.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The ordered puncture word of a dotted component is defined by reading the list
inside its unique designated projector event.  Consequently every dotted
component has exactly one event carrying exactly that word, every projector
event uses such a word, and the word appears as one contiguous block in an
otherwise unchanged active-order context.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
\texttt{DottedCutData.punctureWord} is a noncomputable definition at lines
347--355.  Projector completeness, recorded order, exact ports, and unchanged
exterior context are theorems proved at lines 357--574, notably
\nolinkurl{projectorEventsExactlyMatchDottedComponents}.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These consequences are kernel-checked theorems of C3--C4, not axioms or
additional proof fields.  Thus the evaluator cannot silently choose a second
puncture order.  Status: \texttt{DERIVED}.

\item \textbf{Signed incidence.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For a fixed dotted component and ordinary component, the signed incidence
coefficient is
\[
 N_{\mathrm{out}}-N_{\mathrm{in}},
\]
where $N_{\mathrm{out}}$ and $N_{\mathrm{in}}$ count, respectively, the
outbound and inbound punctures lying on that ordinary component.  Equivalently,
it is the fold over the ordered puncture word that adds $1$ at each outbound
occurrence, subtracts $1$ at each inbound occurrence, and ignores other
ordinary components.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is the definition \texttt{dottedCutBoundaryCoefficient}, lines 368--386.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This integer is computed solely from disk-intersection incidence.  It is not a
choice of $X$ versus $X^*$ and is not itself the character exponent attached
to the ordinary component by the evaluator.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{Binding cut incidence to the handle boundary.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For every primal $1$-handle $i$ and primal $2$-handle $j$, the $(i,j)$ matrix
entry of the stored cellular boundary
$\partial_2\colon C_2\to C_1$ equals the signed incidence from C6 between the
corresponding dotted and ordinary components.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The full second-layer package is
\begin{verbatim}
structure DottedCutPresentation (diagram : SpecialKirbyDiagram) where
  cut : DottedCutData diagram.trace
  incidence_agrees_with_handle_boundary :
    for every one-handle i and two-handle j,
    boundaryTwo(e_j)(i) = dottedCutBoundaryCoefficient(i,j)
\end{verbatim}
This is a notation-normalized transcription of lines 589--604.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The equality is a proof field, but its entire mathematical statement is the
single familiar handle-incidence equation above.  It closes the K6 gap by
preventing an arbitrary stored \texttt{boundaryTwo} from disagreeing with the
cut data.  It does not prove that the combinatorial cut is topologically
realizable; existence of realizable data remains an external topology input.
Status: \nolinkurl{MATCH_AS_EXPLICIT_INCIDENCE_BINDING}.

\end{enumerate}

The cut-open data therefore fixes one ordered puncture word and one exterior
trace, and it explicitly binds their aggregate incidence to $\partial_2$.
What remains unaudited is the topology-to-graphical assertion that these data
arise from a genuine dotted-handle collar and that plugging the resulting
local morphism into its exterior yields the intended ribbon expression.  That
assertion is not hidden in \texttt{DottedCutPresentation}; it is an explicit
entrance axiom and will be reviewed in the axiom subsection.

\subsection{Homology marking and realization}
\label{app:lean-homology-marking}

The evaluator is written in the handle decomposition turned upside down, as
in Tham's Definition~5.11.  The public manifold class, however, lies in
ordinary singular homology.  The following list makes the intervening
coordinate layer explicit.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{H\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]

\item \textbf{The visible primal boundary and its transpose.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For a diagram $D$, let
\[
 C_2^{\rm pr}(D)=\mathbb Z^{h_2(D)},\qquad
 C_1^{\rm pr}(D)=\mathbb Z^{h_1(D)}.
\]
The diagram stores the cellular boundary
$\partial_2^{\rm pr}\colon C_2^{\rm pr}\to C_1^{\rm pr}$.
With respect to the displayed finite handle bases, Lean defines its transpose
\[
 (\partial_2^{\rm pr})^T\colon
 \mathbb Z^{h_1(D)}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{h_2(D)}
\]
and proves the usual adjointness identity for the integral coordinate pairing.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
These are \nolinkurl{primalBoundaryTwo}, \nolinkurl{transposeAddHom},
\nolinkurl{transposeAddHom_adjoint}, and \nolinkurl{thamVisibleCoboundary} in
\nolinkurl{Homology.lean}, lines 70--193.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
All are transparent definitions or kernel-checked finite-sum theorems.  No
$3$-handle attaching map is inserted.  Status: \texttt{DERIVED}.

\item \textbf{The visible THAM coefficient space.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The evaluator assigns one integral exponent to every primal $2$-handle.  Thus
its raw coefficient vectors form
\[
 C^2_{\rm vis}(D)=\mathbb Z^{h_2(D)}.
\]
Changing a representative by a visible coboundary gives the quotient
\[
 Q_{\rm vis}(D)=
 \mathbb Z^{h_2(D)}/\operatorname{im}\bigl((\partial_2^{\rm pr})^T\bigr).
\]

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The types are \texttt{ThamTwoHandleCoefficients} and
\texttt{ThamMarkingClass}; the quotient map is \texttt{thamMarkingClass}, in
\nolinkurl{Homology.lean}, lines 195--214.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These are definitions.  Crucially, Lean does \emph{not} identify
$Q_{\rm vis}(D)$ with $H^2(X;\mathbb Z)$.  Because the closing $3$-handle
attaching data are intentionally absent, $Q_{\rm vis}(D)$ may be larger than
the genuine cocycle group.  Status:
\nolinkurl{MATCH_AS_AMBIENT_COORDINATE_QUOTIENT}.

\item \textbf{A marked cut-open diagram.}

\emph{Human statement.}
A diagram-level marked input consists of a special Kirby diagram $D$, one
cut-open presentation $P$ of $D$, and one class
$\beta\in Q_{\rm vis}(D)$.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
\begin{verbatim}
structure HomologicallyMarkedDiagram where
  diagram : SpecialKirbyDiagram
  cut : DottedCutPresentation diagram
  thamAlpha : ThamMarkingClass diagram
\end{verbatim}
This is \nolinkurl{Homology.lean}, lines 216--220.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is data only.  It does not claim that $\beta$ is the Poincaré dual of a
manifold homology class.  Status: \nolinkurl{MATCH_AS_DIAGRAM_LEVEL_DATA}.

\item \textbf{Selecting the genuine Poincaré-dual classes.}

\emph{Human statement.}
If $D$ presents $X$, the external relation
\[
 \operatorname{RepresentsPoincareDualClass}(D,\beta,\alpha)
\]
means that $\beta\in Q_{\rm vis}(D)$ is the turned-over handle-coordinate
class of $\operatorname{PD}(\alpha)$ for
$\alpha\in H_2(X;\mathbb Z)$.  It does not say that every element of
$Q_{\rm vis}(D)$ is genuine cohomology.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is the explicit axiom \texttt{RepresentsPoincareDualClass} in
\nolinkurl{EntranceAxioms.lean}, lines 388--404.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is a category-neutral topology boundary, necessitated by the deliberate
omission of $3$-handle attaching spheres.  It is an axiom and will reappear in
the complete axiom ledger.  Status: \nolinkurl{EXTERNAL_TOPOLOGY_INPUT}.

\item \textbf{A representative of a marked manifold.}

\emph{Human statement.}
A special Kirby representative of $X$ consists of a diagram $D$, cut-open
data $P$, a proof that $D$ presents $X$, and an ambient THAM class
$\beta\in Q_{\rm vis}(D)$.  Relative to a chosen
$\alpha\in H_2(X;\mathbb Z)$, correctness is the separate proposition that
$\beta$ represents $\operatorname{PD}(\alpha)$.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is \nolinkurl{SpecialKirbyRepresentativeOf} $X$ and the predicate
\nolinkurl{representsHomologyClass}, in \nolinkurl{EntranceAxioms.lean}, lines
406--426.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The structure contains no hidden correctness field beyond the explicitly
named presentation relation.  Correctness for $\alpha$ is not baked into the
structure and must be supplied separately.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{Transport under an elementary move.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For a move $D\to D'$, Lean stores homomorphisms
\[
 T_1\colon\mathbb Z^{h_1(D)}\to\mathbb Z^{h_1(D')},\qquad
 T_2\colon\mathbb Z^{h_2(D)}\to\mathbb Z^{h_2(D')}
\]
satisfying
\[
 T_2(\partial_2(D))^T=(\partial_2(D'))^T T_1.
\]
Therefore $T_2$ descends to a homomorphism
$Q_{\rm vis}(D)\to Q_{\rm vis}(D')$.  The target marking carried by the move
must equal the image of the source marking under this quotient map.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is \texttt{ThamHandleChainTransport}, its derived
\texttt{inducedMarking}, and the \texttt{markingTransport} field of every
\texttt{ElementaryMove}; see \nolinkurl{Moves.lean}, lines 239--290 and
570--656.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The commuting square and endpoint equality are proof fields.  Their complete
statements are visible and contain no evaluator equality.  They certify the
marked move rather than derive it from a raw unmarked move.  Status:
\nolinkurl{MATCH_AS_MARKED_MOVE_DATA}.

\item \textbf{Birth and death coordinates.}

\emph{Human statement.}
When a move adds one primal $2$-handle, Lean identifies the target basis with
the old basis plus one new coordinate, preserves every old coefficient, and
assigns coefficient zero to the new handle.  When a move removes a
$2$-handle, it first chooses a homologous representative whose disappearing
coordinate is zero, then transports the surviving coordinates.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
These are \nolinkurl{AddedTwoHandleCoordinates} and
\nolinkurl{RemovedTwoHandleCoordinates}, in \nolinkurl{Moves.lean}, lines
291--387.  Their later \nolinkurl{Matches...Component} predicates bind the
abstract coordinate equivalence to the literal newly added or removed
component.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
These are proof-bearing coordinate normal forms carried by cancellation
moves.  The death structure's \nolinkurl{safeCoefficients_sameClass} is exactly
the representative replacement that must later invoke representative
independence of the evaluator; it is not itself an evaluation equality.
Status: \nolinkurl{MATCH_WITH_VISIBLE_COORDINATE_CERTIFICATES}.

\end{enumerate}

The homology layer therefore does not replace manifold $H_2$ by a fixed copy
of $\mathbb Z$, nor does it mistake the visible quotient for all of $H^2$.
The two genuinely external statements are the realization of a diagram as a
closed four-manifold and the identification of the selected THAM class with
$\operatorname{PD}(\alpha)$; both will be listed explicitly below.

\subsection{Typed graphical language and evaluator}
\label{app:lean-graphical-evaluator}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{E\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]

\item \textbf{Typed ribbon expressions.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For objects $A,B\in\cC$, a ribbon expression from $A$ to $B$ is generated by
actual morphisms, composition, tensor product, zero, addition, and scalar
multiplication.  Composition is permitted only when the intermediate objects
agree.  Its interpretation is the corresponding morphism $A\to B$ in $\cC$.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is the indexed inductive type
\texttt{RibbonExpression C A B} and the recursive definition
\texttt{RibbonExpression.interpret}, in \nolinkurl{Graphical.lean}, lines
67--111.  Crossings, cups, caps, twists, and a general coupon are definitions
made from the braiding, rigidity, ribbon twist, and an actual supplied
categorical morphism; see lines 113--169.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is typed syntax and interpretation, not a correctness axiom.  A coupon is
not a string or parser token.  Ill-typed composition is unrepresentable.
Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{Ribbon orientation and duals.}

\emph{Human statement.}
A positively oriented strand labelled by a simple object $X$ denotes $X$; a
negatively oriented strand denotes the chosen right dual $X^*$.  This choice
comes from the strand orientation in the link trace, not from how the strand
intersects a dotted disk.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The definitions are \nolinkurl{RibbonDirection}, \nolinkurl{OrientedSimple},
\nolinkurl{orientedSimpleObject}, and
\nolinkurl{ribbonDirectionOfStrandOrientation}; see
\nolinkurl{Graphical.lean}, lines 30--65, and
\nolinkurl{Evaluator.lean}, lines 58--61.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is a definition.  The equalities comparing exterior input and output
ports to a cut port use only the puncture's component and orientation
preservation fields.  Disk-intersection direction never enters this choice.
Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{Character coupons and transport.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For an object $X$ and $n\in\mathbb Z$, the character coupon is the actual
endomorphism $\eta_X^n$.  Naturality gives
\[
 \eta_X^n\,f=f\,\eta_Y^n
 \qquad(f\colon X\to Y),
\]
with the order read in the category's diagrammatic composition convention.
Consequently the coupon can pass through a finite sequence of crossings,
cups, caps, twists, and morphism coupons.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
These are \nolinkurl{characterCoupon},
\nolinkurl{characterCoupon_naturality}, the inductive derivation
\nolinkurl{CharacterCouponTransport}, and its theorem
\nolinkurl{CharacterCouponTransport.interpret_eq}, in
\nolinkurl{Graphical.lean}, lines 171--568.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The equality is proved from the monoidal natural automorphism; no separate
``sliding evaluator'' axiom occurs.  Status: \texttt{DERIVED}.

\item \textbf{Fusion channels and the unit projector.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For simple $X,Y$, Lean chooses a finite simple decomposition of $X\otimes Y$.
Summing inclusion--projection pairs over all channels gives
$\operatorname{id}_{X\otimes Y}$.  For any object $V$, the tensor-unit
projector
\[
 P_{\mathbf1,V}=\sum_{i:\,V_i\cong\mathbf1}\iota_i p_i
\]
is derived from the same chosen decomposition.  On a simple $V$, it is the
identity when $V\cong\mathbf1$ and zero otherwise.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
These are \nolinkurl{fusionResolutionExpression}, its interpretation theorem,
\nolinkurl{tensorUnitProjector}, and
\nolinkurl{tensorUnitProjector_simple}, in \nolinkurl{Graphical.lean}, lines
816--978.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The morphisms are definitions and the identities are kernel-checked theorems
of finite semisimplicity.  The projector is not caller-supplied and is not the
transparent-sector projector.  Status: \texttt{DERIVED}.

\item \textbf{Dotted holes versus ordinary components.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For a fixed ordinary-component labelling, cups, caps, crossings, twists, and
planar continuations compile to their standard ribbon morphisms.  A
\texttt{projectorHole} compiles instead to the tensor-unit projector on the
ordered tensor product of all strands crossing that dotted disk.  The complete
closed morphism is obtained by composing these events in sweep order.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The local definition is \texttt{compileCutOpenEventMorphism}; its
\texttt{projectorHole} branch is at \nolinkurl{Evaluator.lean}, lines
587--598.  Exact common exterior contexts are added by
\nolinkurl{compileRoleAwareContextualCutOpenEventMorphism}, and the full closed
morphism is \nolinkurl{compileCutOpenExteriorMorphism}, lines 985--1083.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is the canonical compiler from the already audited finite cut-open
syntax.  Literal dotted components are never summed over ordinary Kirby
colours.  The compiler is a definition, not an input or soundness axiom.
Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{Canonical closed evaluation.}

\emph{Human statement.}
A closed ribbon expression denotes an endomorphism of the simple tensor unit.
Schur scalarization sends that endomorphism canonically to its scalar in $K$.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
\begin{verbatim}
noncomputable def canonicalRibbonGraphicalEvaluation
    (C : BundledPremodularCategory K)
    (expression : ClosedRibbonExpression C) : K :=
  C.closedScalar expression.interpret
\end{verbatim}
This is \nolinkurl{Graphical.lean}, lines 1264--1276.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is a derived noncomputable definition.  No graphical evaluator is
supplied by the theorem caller.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{Weights and the unique character exponent.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For a labelling $\lambda$ of the primal $2$-handles by simple objects and a
coefficient vector $a=(a_j)$, the two scalar weights are
\[
 w_{\rm K}(\lambda)=\prod_j d_{\lambda(j)},\qquad
 w_\eta(\lambda,a)=\prod_j\eta(\lambda(j))^{a_j}.
\]
The exponent $a_j$ comes only from the THAM marking coefficient of that
$2$-handle.  It does not come from puncture direction or strand orientation.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
These are \texttt{ordinaryKirbyWeight} and \texttt{characterWeight} in
\nolinkurl{Evaluator.lean}, lines 33--48.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
Both are definitions.  The character occurs exactly once as this scalar
weight; graphical character coupons are introduced only inside proofs that
rewrite the same scalar action.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{The role-aware coefficient evaluator.}

\emph{Human statement.}
Let $D=\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)$.  For a special Kirby diagram $K$, cut-open
presentation $P$, and THAM coefficient vector $a$, Lean defines
\[
 \operatorname{Eval}_{\cC,\eta}(K,P,a)
 =\frac{D}{D^{h_3^{\rm pr}(K)}}
  \sum_{\lambda}
  w_{\rm K}(\lambda)\,w_\eta(\lambda,a)\,
  \operatorname{RT}_{\cC}(G_{K,P,\lambda}).
\]
Here $h_3^{\rm pr}$ is the number of primal $3$-handles, hence the number of
THAM $1$-handles after turning the decomposition upside down.  The graph
$G_{K,P,\lambda}$ uses ordinary Kirby labels on primal $2$-handles and the
unit projector at every dotted hole.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The normalization, finite sum, and final product are respectively
\nolinkurl{thamDefinition511Normalization}, \texttt{rawCoefficientSum}, and
\texttt{evaluateCoefficients}, in \nolinkurl{Evaluator.lean}, lines
1104--1147.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
All three are definitions.  The normalization receives all three handle
counts for interface clarity but currently depends only on the THAM
$1$-handle count, passed as the primal $h_3$.  Global-dimension nonvanishing is
needed later to cancel normalization factors.  Status:
\nolinkurl{MATCH_WITH_EXPLICIT_NORMALIZATION}.

\item \textbf{Representative independence is an obligation.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For fixed $(K,P)$, if two coefficient vectors determine the same element of
$Q_{\rm vis}(K)$, then their evaluations are equal.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This proposition is \nolinkurl{RepresentativeIndependenceStatement}, in
\nolinkurl{Evaluator.lean}, lines 1149--1162.  It is proved in a separate
proof module and is not a field of the evaluator.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This declaration merely names the exact theorem obligation.  Whether its
proof uses only the admitted trust boundary is audited below.  Status:
\nolinkurl{OBLIGATION_NOT_ASSUMPTION}.

\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Eight elementary moves and local proofs}
\label{app:lean-eight-moves}

Every move below has exact source and target marked diagrams, an explicit
THAM-side chain transport, and a proof that the target marking is the induced
image of the source marking.  No move constructor contains an evaluation
equality.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{M\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]

\item \textbf{Framed isotopy.}

\emph{Human statement.}
A framed-isotopy move supplies reindexings of components and strands, an
external topology proof that these exact diagrams are related by framed
ambient isotopy preserving roles, framings, cut words, and exterior data, and
the marked chain transport.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is the \texttt{ElementaryMove.framedIsotopy} constructor, using
\nolinkurl{FramedIsotopyScriptRealizesFramedIsotopy}; see
\nolinkurl{Moves.lean}, lines 129--145 and 570--578.

\emph{Proof and judgment.}
The axiom \nolinkurl{framedIsotopy_hasTypedGraphicalInterpretation} supplies a
framed-ribbon isotopy between the two exact normalized expressions; Shum
coherence then proves equal morphisms and hence equal scalar evaluations.
The final equality is theorem
\nolinkurl{framedIsotopy_preserves_evaluation}.  Status:
\nolinkurl{PROVED_FROM_EXPLICIT_TOPOLOGY_BOUNDARIES}.

\item \textbf{The $1/1$ slide.}

\emph{Human statement.}
Both moving and stationary components are dotted.  The move is supplied as a
clean band slide with a clean attachment, a finite complete route along the
stationary component, and a clean detachment.  Its marked homology transport
is explicit.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is \texttt{ElementaryMove.oneOverOne}, lines 579--594.  The two role
proofs require both source components to be dotted.

\emph{Proof and judgment.}
It is an instance of \nolinkurl{cleanBandSlide_preserves_evaluation}: attach,
resolve the identity into all fusion channels, transport the character coupon
through finitely many naturality events, recombine the channels, and detach.
The dotted role enters through the role-aware endpoint interpretation rather
than an ordinary Kirby-colour replacement.  Status:
\nolinkurl{PROVED_CONDITIONALLY_ON_CLEAN_SLIDE_INPUTS}.

\item \textbf{The $2/1$ slide.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The moving component is ordinary and the stationary component is dotted; the
same clean attach--finite transport--detach data and exact marking transport
are supplied.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is \texttt{ElementaryMove.twoOverOne}, lines 595--610.

\emph{Proof and judgment.}
The same source-bound clean-band theorem proves the equality.  No conversion
of the dotted component into an ordinary Kirby-coloured component occurs.
Status: \nolinkurl{PROVED_CONDITIONALLY_ON_CLEAN_SLIDE_INPUTS}.

\item \textbf{The $2/2$ slide.}

\emph{Human statement.}
Both moving and stationary components are ordinary.  A clean band is attached,
the attaching coupon travels through a finite complete sequence of local
events on the stationary component, and the band is detached.  Fusion-channel
resolution and the character morphism are retained throughout.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is \texttt{ElementaryMove.twoOverTwo}, lines 611--626.

\emph{Proof and judgment.}
The theorem \nolinkurl{cleanBandSlide_preserves_evaluation} explicitly factors
the proof into attach, fusion resolution, finite coupon transport, inverse
fusion resolution, and detach.  The equality of scalar evaluations is derived
after morphism equality; it is not an entrance axiom.  Status:
\nolinkurl{PROVED_CONDITIONALLY_ON_CLEAN_SLIDE_INPUTS}.

\item \textbf{The $1$--$2$ birth.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The target appends one dotted component and one zero-framed ordinary meridian.
The cut-open local trace is exactly a cup, one tensor-unit-projector hole with
one puncture, and a cap.  The new $2$-handle coefficient is zero, while all old
coefficients and components retain their coordinates.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The transparent predicate is \nolinkurl{IsStandardOneTwoBirth} in
\nolinkurl{LocalReplacementSyntax.lean}, lines 105--183.  The corresponding
constructor of \nolinkurl{CancellationLocalNormalForm} is at
\nolinkurl{Moves.lean}, lines 533--541.

\emph{Proof and judgment.}
The local projector calculation makes the canceling meridian sum equal to
one; the new exponent is zero, and the handle-count normalization is unchanged
in the turned-over convention.  The resulting theorem is
\nolinkurl{oneTwoBirth_preserves_evaluation}.  Status: \texttt{PROVED}.

\item \textbf{The $1$--$2$ death.}

\emph{Human statement.}
This is the reverse of M5.  Before removing the canceling coordinate, one may
replace the source coefficient vector by a homologous representative on which
that coordinate is zero.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
\texttt{IsStandardOneTwoDeath} is definitionally the reversed birth normal
form.  The death constructor carries
\texttt{RemovedTwoHandleCoordinates} and its exact meridian binding.

\emph{Proof and judgment.}
Representative independence is used before and after the local death
calculation to justify the safe zero-coordinate representative.  The theorem
is \nolinkurl{oneTwoDeath_preserves_evaluation}.  Status: \texttt{PROVED}.

\item \textbf{The $2$--$3$ birth.}

\emph{Human statement.}
The target appends one isolated zero-framed ordinary $2$-handle and increases
the primal $3$-handle count by one.  Its finite local ribbon trace is a cup,
an identity continuation, and a cap.  The new $2$-handle coefficient is zero.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is \texttt{IsStandardTwoThreeBirth} in
\nolinkurl{LocalReplacementSyntax.lean}, lines 191--254.  The actual list
\texttt{twoThreeLocalEvents} is
\texttt{birth, planarIsotopy, death}.

\emph{Proof and judgment.}
Summing the isolated ordinary loop over simple labels contributes
$\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)$; this cancels the extra denominator caused by the
new primal $3$-handle.  The theorem is
\nolinkurl{twoThreeBirth_preserves_evaluation}.  The transparent definition
and its corrected source comment both use planar identity continuation, as
required by the zero framing; no nontrivial twist is identified with the
identity.  Status: \texttt{PROVED}.

\item \textbf{The $2$--$3$ death.}

\emph{Human statement.}
This is the reverse of M7.  A homologous representative with zero disappearing
coefficient is chosen, the isolated Kirby-coloured loop is removed, and the
normalization factor changes inversely.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
\texttt{IsStandardTwoThreeDeath} is the reversed birth normal form; the move
carries removed-handle coordinates bound to the isolated component.

\emph{Proof and judgment.}
The proof combines representative independence, the ordinary-loop global
dimension theorem, and field cancellation using
$\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)\ne0$.  The result is
\nolinkurl{twoThreeDeath_preserves_evaluation}.  Status: \texttt{PROVED}.

\item \textbf{Exhaustive local theorem.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For every constructor among M1--M8 and every source coefficient vector $a$,
\[
 \operatorname{Eval}(D,P,a)
 =\operatorname{Eval}(D',P',T_2a).
\]

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is \nolinkurl{frozenFrontendLocalMoveInvariance}, a theorem proving
\texttt{LocalMoveInvarianceStatement}, in
\nolinkurl{Proofs/FrozenFrontendLocalMoves.lean}, lines 96--134.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The proof performs an explicit case split over framed isotopy, the three slide
constructors, and the four cancellation normal forms.  There is no default
case and no local-invariance axiom.  Status: \texttt{DERIVED}.

\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Representative independence and four-manifold descent}
\label{app:lean-descent}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{D\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]

\item \textbf{One visible coboundary is invisible.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For $a\in\mathbb Z^{h_2}$ and
$b\in\mathbb Z^{h_1}$,
\[
 \operatorname{Eval}(D,P,a+(\partial_2^{\rm pr})^Tb)
 =\operatorname{Eval}(D,P,a).
\]
The proof expands the character factor contributed by the coboundary as a
product over dotted $1$-handles.  For each dotted handle, the standard collar
normal form identifies that factor with the monoidal character action on its
incidence morphism; naturality and the unit projector absorb it.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The theorem is
\nolinkurl{dotted_evaluateCoefficients_add_thamVisibleCoboundary}, in
\nolinkurl{Proofs/DottedRepresentativeIndependence.lean}, lines 310--348.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is a kernel-checked theorem.  Its topology-to-graphical input is exactly
\nolinkurl{standardDottedCollarIncidenceNormalForm}; puncture direction is used
only to form the aggregate incidence row and never to choose $X^*$ or a
character exponent.  Status: \nolinkurl{DERIVED_FROM_ONE_EXPLICIT_COLLAR_AXIOM}.

\item \textbf{Representative independence.}

\emph{Human statement.}
If $[a]=[a']$ in $Q_{\rm vis}(D)$, then
$\operatorname{Eval}(D,P,a)=\operatorname{Eval}(D,P,a')$.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is \nolinkurl{frozenFrontendRepresentativeIndependence}, exported from
\nolinkurl{Proofs/RepresentativeIndependence.lean}, lines 23--35.  Its proof
unpacks equality in the quotient into $a'=a+(\partial_2^{\rm pr})^Tb$ and
applies D1.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is a theorem, not a proof field or axiom.  Status: \texttt{DERIVED}.

\item \textbf{Evaluation of a marking.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For a marked diagram $(D,P,[a])$, choose any representative $a$ and evaluate
it.  D2 proves that this choice does not affect the result.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
\texttt{evaluateMarking} is defined using \texttt{Quotient.out} in
\nolinkurl{Invariant.lean}, lines 35--41.  The choice-independence theorem used
downstream is D2.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is a noncomputable definition plus a separate theorem; choice
independence is not hidden in the definition.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{One move preserves a marked evaluation.}

\emph{Human statement.}
Apply the exact chain map to a chosen source representative.  Local invariance
compares that transported representative with the source; representative
independence then compares it with the representative chosen by
\texttt{Quotient.out} for the target marking.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
This is \nolinkurl{elementaryMove_preserves_frontend_evaluation}, in
\nolinkurl{TwistedYetter.lean}, lines 43--79.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This theorem combines D2, M9, and the move's explicit marking-transport
equation.  Status: \texttt{DERIVED}.

\item \textbf{A finite Kirby movie preserves evaluation.}

\emph{Human statement.}
If
\[
 (D_0,P_0,\beta_0)\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow
 (D_n,P_n,\beta_n)
\]
is a finite sequence of elementary marked moves, then the evaluations of the
first and last stages agree.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
\texttt{KirbyMovie} stores a finite indexed sequence and one
\texttt{ElementaryMove} between each consecutive pair.  The theorem
\nolinkurl{kirbyMovie_preserves_frontend_evaluation}, in
\nolinkurl{TwistedYetter.lean}, lines 81--130, is proved by induction on the
finite stage index.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is a theorem.  No movie-invariance axiom is present.  Status:
\texttt{DERIVED}.

\item \textbf{Marked Kirby--Cerf descent.}

\emph{Human statement.}
Suppose two special representatives encode $(X,\alpha)$ and
$(Y,f_*\alpha)$ for an oriented diffeomorphism $f\colon X\to Y$.  Marked
Kirby--Cerf completeness supplies a finite marked movie between them; D5 then
proves equal evaluations.

\emph{Lean declaration.}
The external topology input is \nolinkurl{markedKirbyCerf_complete}; the
internal assembly theorem is
\nolinkurl{ConditionalDescent.diffeomorphism_invariant_of_descent_data}, in
\nolinkurl{TwistedYetter.lean}, lines 132--158.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The existence of the movie is an explicit topology axiom.  Equality along the
movie is derived.  Status: \nolinkurl{DERIVED_FROM_KIRBY--CERF_COMPLETENESS}.

\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Structure proof-field ledger}
\label{app:lean-proof-fields}

A structure field of type \texttt{Prop} does not appear as a separate item in
\texttt{\#print axioms}: it is evidence supplied whenever an instance of the
structure is constructed.  It can therefore hide a mathematical assumption if
the surrounding structure is itself postulated.  In the active route, however,
the following fields belong to finite combinatorial certificates or to data
returned by the explicit topology axioms already listed later.  For audit
purposes we nevertheless flatten all thirty-three fields here.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{F\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]

\item \textbf{One dotted puncture (four fields).}
\nolinkurl{DottedPuncture.dotted_role} says that the pierced component is
dotted.  The fields \nolinkurl{input_on_two_handle} and
\nolinkurl{output_on_two_handle} say that the incoming and outgoing strand
segments belong to the same declared ordinary $2$-handle.  The field
\nolinkurl{orientation_preserved} says that those two segments carry the same
intrinsic strand orientation.  None of these fields determines whether the
label is $X$ or $X^*$ from the disk-crossing direction; that choice is made
only from intrinsic strand orientation.

\item \textbf{A cut-open exterior trace (eight fields).}
\nolinkurl{initialBoundaryEmpty} and \nolinkurl{finalBoundaryEmpty} say that the
movie begins and ends closed.  \nolinkurl{transitionValid} checks the boundary
word change prescribed by each event.  The fields
\nolinkurl{strandContinuationsAreWellFormed},
\nolinkurl{eventPortsAreDistinct}, and
\nolinkurl{everyStrandHasUniqueInputAndOutput} rule out dangling, duplicated, or
multiply attached strand ends.  Finally,
\nolinkurl{activeWordsNoDuplicates} forbids a strand from appearing twice in one
boundary word, and \texttt{activeStrandsAreTwoHandles} restricts every active
strand to an ordinary $2$-handle.  These are transparent finite-trace
well-formedness conditions.

\item \textbf{Projector-hole indexing (four fields).}
\nolinkurl{DottedCutData.projectorEventIndex} assigns one exterior projector-hole
event to each dotted component.  \nolinkurl{projectorEvent_is_for} identifies
that event with the chosen dotted component and its ordered puncture word.
\nolinkurl{everyProjectorEvent_is_indexed} excludes extra projector holes, and
\nolinkurl{projectorPunctures_are_indexed} excludes omitted or invented
punctures.  These fields make the two finite indexings inverse on the recorded
data; they do not assert the topological existence of spanning disks.

\item \textbf{Cut incidence versus handle boundary (one field).}
\nolinkurl{DottedCutPresentation.incidence_agrees_with_handle_boundary} says
that the signed total incidence recorded by the cut-open trace equals the
corresponding entry of the visible $2$-to-$1$ handle boundary matrix.  The sign
is the algebraic incidence sign of the handle complex; it neither selects
$X^*$ nor supplies a character exponent puncture by puncture.

\item \textbf{A closed combinatorial link trace (five fields).}
\nolinkurl{CombinatorialLinkTrace.initialBoundaryEmpty},
\nolinkurl{finalBoundaryEmpty}, \nolinkurl{transitionValid},
\nolinkurl{activeWordsNoDuplicates}, and
\nolinkurl{everyStrandHasUniqueInputAndOutput} are the closed-trace analogues of
the corresponding cut-open conditions in F2.  They certify that the finite
event syntax describes a closed framed link rather than a tangle with dangling
or duplicated strands.

\item \textbf{Visible handle indexing (two fields).}
\nolinkurl{SpecialHandleData.oneHandleIndex} identifies each dotted component
with exactly one primal $1$-handle.  \texttt{boundaryTwo} records the integral
boundary homomorphism from ordinary primal $2$-handles to those $1$-handles.
The first is finite indexing data; the second is mathematical handle-chain
data, whose agreement with the cut incidence is separately required by F4.

\item \textbf{Chain transport (one field).}
\nolinkurl{ThamHandleChainTransport.commutesWithVisibleCoboundary} is the matrix
identity
\[
 T_2\,(\partial_2^{\rm pr})^T
 = (\partial_2^{\prime\,\rm pr})^T T_1.
\]
It is exactly the condition needed for the coefficient map $T_2$ to descend
to a homomorphism on the quotient $Q_{\rm vis}$; it asserts no evaluation
equality.

\item \textbf{Coordinates added by a birth (two fields).}
\nolinkurl{AddedTwoHandleCoordinates.oldCoefficient} says that every old
$2$-handle retains its former coefficient under the declared reindexing.
\nolinkurl{newCoefficientZero} says that the newly born canceling $2$-handle has
coefficient zero.  These are the coordinate form of the move's explicit chain
transport.

\item \textbf{Coordinates removed by a death (three fields).}
\nolinkurl{RemovedTwoHandleCoordinates.safeCoefficients_sameClass} says that the
chosen safe source vector represents the same quotient class as the original
one.  \nolinkurl{safe_newCoefficientZero} says its disappearing coordinate is
zero.  \nolinkurl{transportedOldCoefficient} says the surviving coordinates map
to the declared target coordinates.  Evaluation equality follows later from
representative independence and the local cancellation theorem; it is not one
of these fields.

\item \textbf{Finite movie endpoints and steps (three fields).}
\texttt{KirbyMovie.startsAtSource} and \texttt{endsAtTarget} identify the first
and last stages with the declared endpoints.  \texttt{elementary} supplies one
of the eight typed elementary moves between each consecutive pair of stages.
Finite-movie invariance is proved by induction from these data and is not a
field.

\end{enumerate}

The red-team scanner reports these fields because it cannot decide their
human meaning from syntax alone.  The review above classifies F1--F6 as
finite-diagram or handle data, F7--F9 as explicit homological transport
certificates, and F10 as finite-path bookkeeping.  None states graphical
evaluation equality, representative independence, local invariance, or the
final theorem.  Their remaining semantic risk is concentrated in whether the
objects that supply them really model the intended Kirby topology; that risk
is exposed by the explicit realization axioms X1--X3, X6, X8--X11, and X16--X19.

\subsection{Public output}
\label{app:lean-public-output}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{O\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]

\item \textbf{The manifold-level invariant.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For a twisted premodular category $(\cC,\eta)$ and a marked closed connected
oriented smooth four-manifold $(X,\alpha)$, choose a special Kirby

\[
 \operatorname{TwistedYetter}_{\cC,\eta}(X,\alpha)
 =\operatorname{Eval}_{\cC,\eta}(D,P,\beta).

noncomputable def TwistedYetter
    (Ceta : TwistedPremodularCategory K)

The source definition uses

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
The public inputs are only $(\cC,\eta)$ and $(X,\alpha)$.  No evaluator,
compiler, normalization, local-invariance proof, presentation, or topology
package is a public parameter.  Status: \texttt{MATCH}.

\item \textbf{The final diffeomorphism-invariance theorem.}

\emph{Human statement.}
For every twisted premodular category $(\cC,\eta)$ over $K$, every
orientation-preserving diffeomorphism $f\colon X\to Y$ of closed connected
oriented smooth four-manifolds, and every

\[
 \operatorname{TwistedYetter}_{\cC,\eta}(X,\alpha)
 =
 \operatorname{TwistedYetter}_{\cC,\eta}(Y,f_*\alpha).
\]

\emph{Lean declaration.}
\begin{verbatim}
theorem TwistedYetter_diffeomorphism_invariant
    (Ceta : TwistedPremodularCategory K)
    {X Y : ClosedConnectedOrientedSmoothFourManifold}
    (f : OrientedDiffeomorphism X Y)
    (alpha : ManifoldH2 X) :
    TwistedYetter Ceta <X, alpha> =
      TwistedYetter Ceta <Y, inducedH2 f alpha>
\end{verbatim}
This is an ASCII-normalized transcription of
\nolinkurl{TwistedYetter.lean}, lines 182--209.

\emph{Classification and judgment.}
This is the public kernel-checked theorem.  Its signature is clean and matches

\subsection{Complete explicit-axiom ledger}

The active independent mainline contains exactly the following nineteen
explicit Lean axioms.  This list counts primitive vocabulary declarations as
well as mathematical propositions, because all of them appear in the kernel
trust closure.  It does not count standard Lean axioms such as choice.

\subsubsection*{Finite Kirby-move topology}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{X\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]

\item \nolinkurl{FiniteCouponTransportRealizesCompletePath}.
A finite list of crossing, cup, cap, twist, and coupon events is declared to
be the complete path traversed by the attaching coupon along the stationary
component: no local event on that path is omitted.  This is a
category-neutral proposition on \texttt{FiniteCouponTransport}.  Judgment:
\nolinkurl{EXTERNAL_COMPACTNESS/GENERIC_POSITION_INPUT}.

\item \nolinkurl{CleanBandSlideRealizesStandardKirbySlide}.
The exact source and target diagrams, component roles, orientation, framing
push-off, clean attaching site, common exterior, transport window, and clean
detaching site stored in a \texttt{CleanBandSlide} realize one standard
oriented Kirby band slide.  It mentions no category or evaluation.  Judgment:
\nolinkurl{EXTERNAL_HANDLE_TOPOLOGY_INPUT}.

\item \nolinkurl{FramedIsotopyScriptRealizesFramedIsotopy}.
The exact source and target diagrams and displayed component/strand
reindexings are related by framed ambient isotopy preserving roles,
orientations, framings, dotted cut words, and common exterior.  Judgment:
\nolinkurl{EXTERNAL_HANDLE_TOPOLOGY_INPUT}.

\subsubsection*{Ribbon graphical coherence and dotted collars}

\item \texttt{FramedRibbonIsotopyWitness}.
For two typed ribbon expressions with the same source and target, this
primitive type is the vocabulary of a framed-ribbon isotopy witness between
them.  It asserts no equality by itself.  Judgment:
\nolinkurl{FOUNDATIONAL_GRAPHICAL_VOCABULARY}.

\item \nolinkurl{shum_framedRibbonCoherence}.
If two typed ribbon expressions are related by a witness from X4, then their
interpreted categorical morphisms are equal.  This is the formal interface to
Shum's coherence theorem for tortile/ribbon categories
\cite[Theorem~2.6]{Shum1994}.  Judgment:
\nolinkurl{LITERATURE_CATEGORY_INPUT}.

\item \nolinkurl{standardDottedCollarIncidenceNormalForm}.
Fix a labelled diagram and one dotted component.  Cutting a standard collar
around its disk exposes a morphism from the ordered tensor product of inbound
incidences to the ordered tensor product of outbound incidences, together with
one linear exterior context.  Plugging that morphism into the context is
framed-ribbon isotopic to the exact closed graph compiled from the cut-open
presentation.  The statement contains no character exponent, projector
formula, scalar equality, or invariance conclusion.  Judgment:
\nolinkurl{HIGH_RISK_TOPOLOGY--GRAPHICAL_BRIDGE}.

\item \nolinkurl{eno_globalDimension_ne_zero}.
For every bundled fusion category over an algebraically closed field of
characteristic zero,
$\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)\ne0$.  In the spherical setting, Corollary~2.10 of
\cite{ENO2005} identifies the squared norm of each simple object with the
square of its categorical dimension, while Theorem~2.3 proves that their sum
is nonzero over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.
Judgment: \nolinkurl{LITERATURE_CATEGORY_INPUT}.

\subsubsection*{Clean slide: topology to typed graphical expressions}

\item \nolinkurl{cleanAttach_hasStandardGraphicalInterpretation}.
For a realized clean band slide and a source coefficient vector, there exists
a standard endpoint normal form such that the exact normalized source
expression is framed-ribbon isotopic to the identity stage of that normal
form.  The normal form visibly contains the moving and stationary simple
labels and one exterior context.  Judgment:
\nolinkurl{HIGH_RISK_ATTACH_BRIDGE}.

\item \nolinkurl{cleanDetach_hasStandardGraphicalInterpretation}.
For the same slide, its exact chain transport, and the source coefficient
vector, there exists a standard endpoint normal form whose identity stage is
framed-ribbon isotopic to the exact normalized target expression evaluated at
the transported coefficient vector.  Judgment:
\nolinkurl{HIGH_RISK_DETACH_BRIDGE}.

\item \nolinkurl{finiteCouponTransport_hasTypedGraphicalDerivation}.

there is a typed \texttt{CharacterCouponTransport} from the fusion-resolved
attach stage to the fusion-resolved detach stage.  The resulting equality of
interpreted morphisms is proved from this derivation.  Judgment:
\nolinkurl{HIGH_RISK_FINITE_TRANSPORT_BRIDGE}.

\item \nolinkurl{framedIsotopy_hasTypedGraphicalInterpretation}.
A realized framed isotopy and its exact THAM chain transport give a
framed-ribbon isotopy between the normalized source expression and the
normalized target expression at the transported coefficient vector.
Judgment: \nolinkurl{HIGH_RISK_ISOTOPY--GRAPHICAL_BRIDGE}.

\subsubsection*{Four-manifold vocabulary and classical topology}

\item \nolinkurl{ClosedConnectedOrientedSmoothFourManifold}.
This primitive type is the domain of closed, connected, oriented smooth

\item \texttt{underlyingTopologicalSpace}.
Every object of X12 has an underlying topological space.  Judgment:
