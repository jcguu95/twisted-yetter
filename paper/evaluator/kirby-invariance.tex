\subsection{Kirby-move invariance}
\label{subsec:kirby-invariance}

The character coupons disappear on the tensor-unit channel for a formal
reason that will be used repeatedly.

\begin{lemma}[Absorption by the tensor-unit projector]
\label{lem:unit-projector-absorbs-character}
For every object $W\in\cC$ and every $n\in\mathbb Z$,
\[
  P_{\mathbf1,W}\eta_W^n=P_{\mathbf1,W}
  =\eta_W^nP_{\mathbf1,W}.
\]
The same identities hold after multiplying the projector by
$\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $P_{\mathbf1,W}=\sum_\mu u_\mu\widehat u_\mu$ as in
\cref{def:tensor-unit-projector}.  Monoidality gives
$\eta_{\mathbf1}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbf1}$.  Naturality, first for
$u_\mu\colon\mathbf1\to W$ and then for
$\widehat u_\mu\colon W\to\mathbf1$, gives
\[
  \eta_W^n u_\mu=u_\mu,
  \qquad
  \widehat u_\mu\eta_W^n=\widehat u_\mu.
\]
Summing these identities proves both equalities.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Independence of graphical auxiliaries]
\label{prop:graphical-auxiliary-independence}
For a fixed marked diagram $(D,\alpha_D)$, the evaluator is independent of
the trace-dual bases used to expand the tensor-unit projectors and of the
dotted cut system used to display those projectors inside a cut-open tangle.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The sum $\sum_\mu u_\mu\widehat u_\mu$ is the canonical projector onto the
full tensor-unit isotypic summand, so a change of trace-dual bases does not
change its morphism.  A dotted cut system is only a factorization of the
same closed dotted-component operation into a cut-open exterior tangle and
this canonical projector.  Moving the cut through a crossing, extremum,
twist, or coupon changes that factorization by the corresponding ribbon or
naturality identity; regluing returns the same closed morphism.  Two cut
systems are related by a finite sequence of these local changes and ambient
isotopies, as in the cutting calculus of
\cite[Section~4.3]{BarenzBarrett2018}.  Induction over that sequence proves
the claim.  No character exponent or $X$ versus $X^*$ choice is read from
the direction of a dotted cut; those data remain fixed throughout, in
accordance with \cref{conv:three-sign-sources}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Representative independence]
\label{prop:representative-independence}
If two integral handle cycles $a$ and $a'$ represent the same class in
$H_2(D)$, then
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(D,a)=\CY_{\cC,\eta}(D,a').
\]
Consequently the value depends only on the marked class
$\alpha_D\in H_2(D)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
It is enough to treat $a'=a+\partial_3c$ for one degree-three generator and
then induct on the finite integral combination $c$.  After turning the
handle decomposition over as in \cref{conv:primal-turned-over}, the added
boundary inserts powers of $\eta$ on precisely the strands passing through
the associated dotted cut.  The cut operator factors through the
tensor-unit summand, so every added power is absorbed by
\cref{lem:unit-projector-absorbs-character}.  The ordinary labels,
projector normalization, and exterior ribbon graph are otherwise unchanged.
The same argument with the inverse powers treats negative coefficients.
\end{proof}

We therefore write $\CY_{\cC,\eta}(D,\alpha_D)$ for the value of any cycle
representing $\alpha_D$.

\begin{theorem}[Elementary Kirby-move invariance]
\label{thm:elementary-kirby-invariance}
Let
\[
  m\colon(D,\alpha_D)\longrightarrow(D',\alpha_{D'})
\]
be a marked elementary Kirby event in the sense of
\cref{def:marked-kirby-event}.  Then
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(D,\alpha_D)
  =\CY_{\cC,\eta}(D',\alpha_{D'}).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We use the local forms of \cref{prop:local-movie-normal-forms}; the exterior
graph and all labels not mentioned below remain fixed.

\emph{Framed isotopy.}
The two graphs represent the same colored framed ribbon graph.  Their
evaluations agree by ribbon graphical coherence
\cite[Theorem~2.6]{Shum1994}.

\emph{$1/1$ and $2/1$ slides.}
Cut the stationary dotted circle before the clean attachment.  Its
normalized cut operator projects all incident strands to the tensor-unit
channel.  Character coupons on that channel are removed by
\cref{lem:unit-projector-absorbs-character}.  The moving strand may then be
transported through the attachment, the finite list of crossings, extrema,
twists, and coupons, and the detachment: the tensor unit is transparent and
each individual passage is an instance of braiding, evaluation,
coevaluation, twist, or coupon naturality.  Induction on the finite event
list proves equality between the attached and detachable graphs.  Regluing
the same normalized projector gives the target evaluator.

\emph{$2/2$ slide.}
At the clean attachment, resolve the moving and stationary simple labels by
the complete set of fusion channels, including every multiplicity index.
If the moving and stationary labels and exponents are $(x,a)$ and $(y,b)$,
then our positive convention resolves channels
$z\subset x\otimes y^*$, changes the moving exponent to $a+b$, and places
$\eta_z^{-b}$ on the internal channel.  The negative convention resolves
$z\subset x\otimes y$, changes the moving exponent to $a-b$, and places
$\eta_z^b$ on that channel.  These formulas are exactly monoidal naturality
of $\eta$ applied to the oriented fusion inclusion.  Move the internal
coupon around the stationary
component.  Each of the finitely many local events preserves the morphism by
the same naturality identities as above, so induction gives invariance of
the transport stage.  At the clean detachment, the trace-dual fusion bases
recombine to the identity.  This recovers exactly the transported target
labels and exponents.  Summing over the stationary label, fusion channel,
multiplicity, and all exterior labels proves the stated equality for either
band orientation.

\emph{$1$--$2$ birth and death.}
The new $2$-handle coordinate is zero under the induced handle-homology map,
so its character coupon is $\eta^0=\operatorname{id}$.  Cutting the new
dotted circle evaluates the cancelling local pair to the identity in the
tensor-unit channel.  Both the raw graphical sum and the primal $3$-handle
count are therefore unchanged.  Death is the reverse calculation.

\emph{$2$--$3$ birth and death.}
The standard cancelling pair contributes one factor
$\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)$ to the unnormalized graphical sum.  In the primal
handle convention, birth also increases $h_3$ by one, so the prefactor
$\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)^{1-h_3}$ contributes its inverse.  The new
homology coordinate is zero and hence contributes no character factor.
Again, death is the reverse calculation.

These are all eight directed events of
\cref{def:marked-kirby-event}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Manifold-level evaluator]
\label{def:manifold-level-evaluator}
For a marked closed oriented smooth four-manifold $(X,\alpha)$, choose a
special Kirby representative $(D,\alpha_D)$ as in
\cref{prop:special-representative-existence} and set
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(X,\alpha)
  :=\CY_{\cC,\eta}(D,\alpha_D).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Marked diffeomorphism invariance]
\label{prop:diffeomorphism-invariance}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be closed oriented smooth four-manifolds, let
$f\colon X\to Y$ be an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism, and let
$\alpha\in H_2(X;\mathbb Z)$.  Then
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(X,\alpha)
  =\CY_{\cC,\eta}(Y,f_*\alpha).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose special representatives of the two marked manifolds.  By marked
Kirby--Cerf completeness, \cref{prop:marked-kirby-cerf}, they are connected
by a finite sequence of the eight marked events.  Apply
\cref{thm:elementary-kirby-invariance} at each step and induct on the length
of the sequence.  Representative independence identifies the transported
handle classes with $\alpha$ and $f_*\alpha$.  Thus
\cref{def:manifold-level-evaluator} is independent of every choice and has
the asserted diffeomorphism invariance.
\end{proof}
