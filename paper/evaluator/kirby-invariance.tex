\subsection{Kirby-move invariance}
\label{subsec:kirby-invariance}

The character coupons disappear on the tensor-unit channel for a formal
reason that will be used repeatedly.

\begin{lemma}[Absorption by the tensor-unit projector]
\label{lem:unit-projector-absorbs-character}
For every object $W\in\cC$ and every $n\in\mathbb Z$,
\[
  P_{\mathbf1,W}\eta_W^n=P_{\mathbf1,W}
  =\eta_W^nP_{\mathbf1,W}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $P_{\mathbf1,W}=\sum_\mu u_\mu\widehat u_\mu$ as in
\cref{def:tensor-unit-projector}.  Monoidality gives
$\eta_{\mathbf1}=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbf1}$.  Naturality, first for
$u_\mu\colon\mathbf1\to W$ and then for
$\widehat u_\mu\colon W\to\mathbf1$, gives
\[
  \eta_W^n u_\mu=u_\mu,
  \qquad
  \widehat u_\mu\eta_W^n=\widehat u_\mu.
\]
Summing these identities proves both equalities.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Naturality of the tensor-unit projector]
\label{lem:unit-projector-naturality}
For every morphism $f\colon U\to V$ in $\cC$,
\[
  fP_{\mathbf1,U}=P_{\mathbf1,V}f.
\]
Consequently a tensor-unit projector may be moved through any finite
composite of crossings, cups, caps, twists, and morphism coupons, with the
source and target projectors changed according to the boundary objects.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Finite semisimplicity decomposes every object as a direct sum of its simple
isotypic summands.  By Schur's lemma, a morphism has no matrix entries
between the tensor-unit isotypic summand and a nonisomorphic simple
isotypic summand.  It therefore preserves the tensor-unit summand, which is
exactly the displayed identity.  Applying the identity successively to the
elementary morphisms in a ribbon expression gives the second assertion.
Graphically, this is the tunnelling property of the completeness idempotent
in \cite[Lemmas~2.27--2.28]{Tham2021}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Oriented dotted-section factorization]
\label{lem:dotted-collar-factorization}
Fix an ordinary-label state $\lambda$, an oriented dotted component $\delta$,
and a dotted co-core chart.  Its positive transverse section determines
the signed word $w_\delta$ and the boundary-state object
$B_\delta=|w_\delta|$ of \cref{def:dotted-circle-translation}.  After all
other labels and dotted operations are fixed, the exterior determines the
closure context
\[
  \mathcal E_\delta\colon
  \operatorname{End}_{\cC}(B_\delta)\longrightarrow\mathbb C.
\]
The dotted operation in that term is exactly
\[
  \mathcal E_\delta(P_{\mathbf1,B_\delta}).
  \tag{3.2}\label{eq:typed-dotted-section}
\]
For every puncture $p$ on the $k$th ordinary component, the following three
pieces of data remain distinct.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),ref=\roman*]
  \item The orientation of component $k$ fixes the ribbon label
  $\lambda_k$ before the disk is chosen; this is the datum that distinguishes
  $X$ from $X^*$.

  \item The oriented arc and the disk coorientation determine the intersection
  sign $\epsilon(p)$.  This is both its contribution to $\partial_2$ and the
  variance of the corresponding boundary point in the signed cut word.

  \item The marking representative supplies $a_k$ and hence the actual coupon
  $(\eta^{a_k})_{\lambda_k}$.  The disk supplies neither this exponent nor its
  sign.
\end{enumerate}
Thus disk intersection never chooses the oriented label or $a_k$.
In particular, a negative boundary occurrence is represented by the dual of
the already fixed oriented label; it does not replace that component's colour
by a disk-dependent choice.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Read the punctures on the positive collar face.  The explicit realization
$B_\delta=|w_\delta|$ uses the rigid dual for a negative boundary occurrence
but does not change the label of the underlying oriented component.  The
opposite face is the reversed rigid dual, exactly as in
\cref{def:dotted-circle-translation}.  Regluing everything
outside the section defines the linear context $\mathcal E_\delta$.

Inside the product neighborhood, bunch the transverse ribbons and insert the
dimension-weighted simple-channel resolution of
\cite[Lemma~2.27]{Tham2021}.  Definition~5.11, $k=3$, sends a resulting simple
channel $i$ to zero unless $i\cong\mathbf1$, in which case it removes that
channel.  Summing the surviving multiplicity terms is precisely the canonical
projector $P_{\mathbf1,B_\delta}$, so regluing gives
\eqref{eq:typed-dotted-section}.  Notice that the projector is inserted on
the through-channel before closure; no currying of the whole exterior tangle
is involved.  The second item is the standard signed-intersection formula for
the coefficient of a $1$-handle in the cellular boundary of a $2$-handle; the
first and third items were fixed before the disk was chosen.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Independence of projector bases and graphical representatives]
\label{prop:graphical-auxiliary-independence}
For a fixed marked diagram $(D,\xi_D)$, the evaluator is independent of the
bases used to expand the tensor-unit projectors.  A dotted co-core chart is
not an input to the evaluator: whenever one is used, it is a graphical
representative of the intrinsic index-$3$ maps already fixed by the handle
decomposition.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The sum $\sum_\mu u_\mu\widehat u_\mu$ is the canonical projector onto the
full tensor-unit isotypic summand, so a change of composition-dual bases does
not change its morphism.  The evaluator itself was defined by composing the
intrinsic index-handle maps, before choosing any graphical chart.
By \cref{prop:dotted-chart-realization}, a dotted co-core chart is a diagram of
those fixed maps.  Two drawings of the same intrinsic composite have the same
morphism by ribbon graphical coherence \cite[Theorem~6.1]{Shum1994}; no
comparison theorem for arbitrary spanning-disk systems is required.  For
several dotted handles, their actual handle neighborhoods are disjoint, so
their maps tensor independently.  Exchanging their written order is the
interchange law for tensor products of morphisms.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Independence of auxiliary orientations]
\label{prop:orientation-choice-independence}
The evaluator is unchanged when any auxiliary oriented handle generator and
its tied Kirby component orientation are reversed as in
\cref{conv:orientation-reversal}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For an ordinary component, reindex its simple-label sum by
$i\leftrightarrow i^*$.  Ribbon orientation reversal is precisely the
standard dual-label convention, $d_{i^*}=d_i$, and
\[
  (\eta^{-a})_{i^*}
  =\eta_{i^*}^{-a}\operatorname{id}_{i^*}
  =\eta_i^a\operatorname{id}_{i^*}.
\]
Thus every reindexed summand gives the same RT morphism under the canonical
orientation--duality identification and has the same coefficient.  For a
dotted component, reversing the $1$-handle generator also reverses the disk
coorientation.  The signed boundary word is replaced by its rigid dual, with
reversed order; pivotal duality carries the canonical projector onto the
tensor-unit summand to the canonical projector on the dual boundary object,
and the exterior closure is carried to its pivotal transpose.  Spherical
graphical evaluation is unchanged.  The incidence
coefficient and the dual $1$-cochain generator both reverse, so the
representative calculation below is covariant.  A $C_3$ orientation change
is covered by \cref{lem:c3-basis-independence}.
\end{proof}

For an ordered signed boundary word
$w=((X_1,\sigma_1),\ldots,(X_r,\sigma_r))$, with
$\sigma_j\in\{+1,-1\}$, set
\[
  \chi_\eta(w):=\prod_{j=1}^r\eta_{X_j}^{\sigma_j}
  \in\mathbb C^\times.
\]
Here $X_j$ is the label of the already oriented ribbon.  The sign records the
variance of its boundary occurrence; categorically a negative occurrence is
interpreted by rigidity, and
$\eta_{X_j^*}=\eta_{X_j}^{-1}$.  It does not recolour the component.

\begin{lemma}[Dotted-incidence character identity]
\label{lem:dotted-incidence-character}
Let $e_\delta^*$ be the cellular $1$-cochain dual to a dotted primal
$1$-handle, and let $w_\delta$ be the signed boundary word of
\cref{lem:dotted-collar-factorization}.  For every $m\in\mathbb Z$,
\[
  \prod_k\eta_{\lambda_k}^{m(\delta^1e_\delta^*)_k}
  =
  \chi_\eta(w_\delta)^m.
  \tag{3.3}\label{eq:dotted-incidence-character}
\]
Moreover the scalar on either side acts trivially on the closed term
$\mathcal E_\delta(P_{\mathbf1,B_\delta})$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient $(\delta^1e_\delta^*)_k$ is the coefficient of the dotted
$1$-handle in the cellular boundary of the $k$th $2$-handle.  In the collar
normal form, \eqref{eq:dotted-cellular-boundary} gives the cochain identity
\[
  \delta^1e_\delta^*=\sum_k I_\delta(k)e_k^*,
  \qquad
  I_\delta(k)=\sum_{p:\,k(p)=k}\epsilon(p).
\]
The puncture $p$ contributes
$\eta_{\lambda_{k(p)}}^{m\epsilon(p)}$.  Multiplying these contributions and
grouping equal component labels proves
\eqref{eq:dotted-incidence-character}.  This bookkeeping uses the disk only
to record the signed boundary occurrence; the ordinary component was already
coloured by $\lambda_{k(p)}$, and the disk supplies neither that colour nor
the exponent $a_{k(p)}$.

By rigidity and \cref{lem:character-identities}, the component of $\eta^m$
on the boundary-state object $B_\delta=|w_\delta|$ is exactly the scalar
$\chi_\eta(w_\delta)^m$: a contravariant occurrence is interpreted on the
dual object and hence inverts its character value.  Therefore the scalar
introduced by the coboundary is $(\eta^m)_{B_\delta}$.  It is absorbed on
either side of $P_{\mathbf1,B_\delta}$ by
\cref{lem:unit-projector-absorbs-character}.  Applying the exterior context
$\mathcal E_\delta$ proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Representative independence]
\label{prop:representative-independence}
If two integral cellular $2$-cocycles $a$ and $a'$ represent the same class
in $H^2(D)$, then
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(D,a)=\CY_{\cC,\eta}(D,a').
\]
Consequently the value depends only on the marked class
$\xi_D\in H^2(D)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $a$ and $a'$ represent the same cellular cohomology class, there is a
$1$-cochain $b\in C^1(D)$ such that
\[
  a'-a=\delta^1b.
\]
The group $C^1(D)$ is freely generated by the primal $1$-handles, so it is
enough to prove the assertion when $b=m e^*_{\delta}$ for one dotted
component $\delta$ and one integer $m$, and then induct over the finite
support of $b$.

Fix an ordinary-label state $\lambda$.  Replacing $a$ by
$a+m\delta^1e_\delta^*$ multiplies its character weight by
\[
  \prod_k\eta_{\lambda_k}^{m(\delta^1e_\delta^*)_k}.
\]
By \cref{lem:dotted-incidence-character}, this is the component of $\eta^m$
on the exact through-channel $B_\delta$ carrying the dotted projector, and
hence is absorbed by that projector before the exterior is closed.  The
Kirby-colour weights and all other local morphisms are
unchanged.  Therefore the two fixed-label summands are equal.  Summing over
$\lambda$, and then applying the argument successively to the finitely many
generators in the support of $b$, proves the claim.
\end{proof}

We therefore write $\CY_{\cC,\eta}(D,\xi_D)$ for the value of any cellular
$2$-cocycle representing $\xi_D$.

\begin{lemma}[Typed finite character transport]
\label{lem:finite-coupon-transport}
Let $n\in\mathbb Z$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),ref=\roman*]
  \item For every morphism $f\colon U\to V$,
  \[
    (\eta^n)_Vf=f(\eta^n)_U.
  \]
  This moves the automorphism of the \emph{whole} input word through a
  multivalent coupon; it does not route one coupon through an arbitrary leg.

  \item The same naturality identity applies to braidings and twists.

  \item At a dual bend the transported component is its mate:
  \[
    \operatorname{ev}_X
      (\operatorname{id}_{X^*}\otimes(\eta^n)_X)
    =
    \operatorname{ev}_X
      ((\eta^{-n})_{X^*}\otimes\operatorname{id}_X),
  \]
  with the analogous coevaluation identity.  Thus the local exponent changes
  sign when the oriented route is expressed on the dual strand.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently a character decoration may be transported through a finite
typed event sequence provided every event uses the matching rule above and
the final boundary words are identified by the framed isotopy.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first statement is naturality of the monoidal natural automorphism
$\eta^n$; braiding and twist are particular morphisms.  Naturality for
$\operatorname{ev}_X$ gives
\[
 \operatorname{ev}_X
 ((\eta^n)_{X^*}\otimes(\eta^n)_X)=\operatorname{ev}_X.
\]
Precompose with $(\eta^{-n})_{X^*}\otimes\operatorname{id}_X$ to obtain the
displayed bend identity; the coevaluation formula is dual.  These prove one
typed event.  Induction on the finite event list proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Index-$3$ handle-slide identity]
\label{lem:one-one-exact-interface}
Let $D\to D'$ be a $1/1$ slide in the clean local form of
\cref{prop:local-movie-normal-forms}.  Turn the handle decompositions over,
and denote the two involved dual index-$3$ handles before the slide by
$H_m^\vee,H_s^\vee$ and after the slide by
$H_m^{\vee\prime},H_s^{\vee\prime}$.  After identifying the incoming and
outgoing level boundaries outside the supporting ball, let
\(\mathsf H^{(3)}_{\mathrm{src}}\) and
\(\mathsf H^{(3)}_{\mathrm{tgt}}\) be the composites of the two intrinsic
index-$3$ handle maps on the source and target sides.  Then, for every
decorated incoming skein $\Gamma$,
\[
  \mathsf H^{(3)}_{\mathrm{src}}([\Gamma])
  =
  \mathsf H^{(3)}_{\mathrm{tgt}}([\Gamma]).
  \tag{3.4}\label{eq:one-one-index-three-interface}
\]
In dotted co-core charts, each side is expanded using the full
composition-dual resolution of the tensor-unit isotypic summand for each of
the two handles.  Equation~\eqref{eq:one-one-index-three-interface} retains
every multiplicity index separately: it does \emph{not} replace two
projectors by a single projector on the tensor product of their
through-objects.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:dotted-chart-realization}, the two chart projectors are the
coordinate expressions of the actual dual index-$3$ handle maps.  Expand the
incoming skein simultaneously across the two co-cores using complete simple
resolutions.  Write its summands as
\[
  [\Gamma]
  =\sum_{i,j,\mu,\nu}[\Gamma_{i,j;\mu,\nu}],
\]
where $i,j$ are the two transverse simple channels and $\mu,\nu$ are their
full multiplicity indices; the trace-dual resolution coefficients are
absorbed into the notation $[\Gamma_{i,j;\mu,\nu}]$.  By
\cite[Definition~5.11, $k=3$]{Tham2021}, the
two source handle maps act termwise as
\[
  \mathsf H^{(3)}_{\mathrm{src}}
    ([\Gamma_{i,j;\mu,\nu}])
  =\delta_{i,\mathbf1}\delta_{j,\mathbf1}
    [\Gamma_{i,j;\mu,\nu}].
  \tag{3.4a}\label{eq:one-one-simple-channel-action}
\]

The standard $1/1$ source and target are the local models fixed by
\cref{conv:referenced-kirby-dictionary}.  Moving from the source pair of
co-cores to the target pair changes the two complete resolutions by the
tunnelling isomorphism of
\cite[Lemma~2.28, Equation~(2.17)]{Tham2021}.  This isomorphism is obtained
from the same composition-dual bases, is invertible on every multiplicity
space, and carries the $(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ summand identically to the
$(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ summand.  Applying the target index-$3$ maps therefore
again gives the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:one-one-simple-channel-action}, with the same surviving
$\mu,\nu$.  Summing the resolved terms proves
\eqref{eq:one-one-index-three-interface}.  This is the algebraic expansion
of the final $1$-handle-slide relation in the proof of
\cite[Proposition~5.13, pp.~53--54]{Tham2021}.

The input is an arbitrary decorated skein, so all character coupons and
uninvolved morphisms remain present.  The supporting-ball model and
\cref{item:fixed-exterior} identify the relation's endpoints with the actual
Kirby source and target, while the common exterior is applied only after the
local morphism equality has been established.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Projector naturality for a $2/1$ slide]
\label{lem:two-one-exact-interface}
Put a $2/1$ slide into the clean local form of
\cref{prop:local-movie-normal-forms}, cut the stationary dotted handle, and
write $B=|w_\delta|$ for its through-object, $x$ for the moving oriented
ribbon label, and $a\in\mathbb Z$ for the moving character exponent.  There
is one fixed exterior closure context on
$\operatorname{Hom}_{\cC}(B\otimes x,x\otimes B)$ in which the positive
source and target local tangles are respectively the two sides of
\[
  c_{B,x}\bigl(P_{\mathbf1,B}\otimes(\eta^a)_x\bigr)
  =
  \bigl((\eta^a)_x\otimes P_{\mathbf1,B}\bigr)c_{B,x}.
  \tag{3.5}\label{eq:positive-two-one-interface}
\]
For the opposite crossing orientation they are the two sides of
\[
  c_{x,B}^{-1}\bigl(P_{\mathbf1,B}\otimes(\eta^a)_x\bigr)
  =
  \bigl((\eta^a)_x\otimes P_{\mathbf1,B}\bigr)c_{x,B}^{-1}.
  \tag{3.6}\label{eq:negative-two-one-interface}
\]
The clean attaching collar is the left factorization, the band transport is
the displayed braiding followed by the finite typed route along the
stationary component, and the clean detaching collar is the right
factorization.  Positive orientation gives $c_{B,x}$, negative orientation
gives $c_{x,B}^{-1}$, the blackboard push-off fixes the band framing, and a
bend transports the coupon by its rigid mate.  The source and target
regluing maps agree outside the supporting ball.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In the pair-of-balls model for the stationary $1$-handle, a $2/1$ slide
pulls the moving attaching arc through one ball and returns it through the
other.  Cutting at the positive face of
\cref{prop:dotted-chart-realization} turns the standard before picture into
the left composite of \eqref{eq:positive-two-one-interface} and the standard
after picture into the right composite.  Reversing the local crossing gives
\eqref{eq:negative-two-one-interface}.  The blackboard push-off of the band
fixes the ribbon framing, and a change of local orientation is expressed by
the rigid mate at the corresponding cup or cap.  Thus the equations name the
actual source and target tangles, not merely abstract morphisms with the same
type.

Both equalities are the naturality square for the braiding, applied to
$P_{\mathbf1,B}\colon B\to B$ and
$(\eta^a)_x\colon x\to x$.  Equivalently, expand $P_{\mathbf1,B}$ as the full
composition-dual resolution and specialize the tunnelling identity
\cite[Lemma~2.28, Equation~(2.17), p.~8]{Tham2021} to the morphism
$f=(\eta^a)_x$; every unit multiplicity index remains in the sum.  Every
event between the two clean collars is handled by
\cref{lem:finite-coupon-transport}; cups and caps use the displayed mate
identity there.  The exterior is fixed by \cref{item:fixed-exterior}.
Applying that common exterior context proves the local evaluation equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Dotted-handle slides]
\label{lem:dotted-handle-slides}
The marked evaluator is unchanged under a $1/1$ slide and under a $2/1$
slide.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put the slide into the clean local form of
\cref{prop:local-movie-normal-forms} and keep the exterior fixed.

For a $1/1$ slide, \cref{lem:one-one-exact-interface} identifies the
source and target composites of the two dual index-$3$ maps.  Because the
identity is an equality on every decorated input skein, it retains all
character coupons already present on ordinary strands.  It also retains the
two full unit-projector resolutions separately, including all multiplicity
indices.  No ordinary $2$-handle generator changes.

For a $2/1$ slide, \cref{lem:two-one-exact-interface} identifies the
source and target local tangles with the two sides of projector and character
naturality, for either crossing orientation.  During the finite route, the
moving character decoration is transported only by the typed rules of
\cref{lem:finite-coupon-transport}; any bend uses the dual mate and any
multivalent coupon uses whole-word naturality.  Since the route returns to
the same oriented moving component, the final coupon is again
$(\eta^{a})_x$.  Applying the common exterior context therefore gives the
target marked local morphism with no appeal to modular killing.

For both moves the ordinary $2$-handle generators are canonically identified
before and after the move.  Choose the target cocycle to be the image of the
source cocycle under the induced cochain map; corresponding ordinary
components then carry the same exponents and the same character coupons.
The preceding graphical equality is therefore already an equality of marked
summands.  Any other cocycle representing the transported target class gives
the same answer by \cref{prop:representative-independence}.  Summing over all
ordinary labels proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Weighted Kirby-colour slide]
\label{lem:weighted-kirby-colour-slide}
Let $A$ be a fixed exterior ribbon tangle and let an $x$-coloured ribbon be
band-summed with a component coloured by
$\Omega_{\cC}=\sum_y d_y y$.  Before inserting character coupons, the
weighted sum of the resulting local morphisms is unchanged by transporting
the band once along the stationary component and detaching it.  The statement
holds for either orientation of the band and inside every fixed exterior
context.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expand the stationary Kirby colour.  For each simple $y$, insert the
dimension-weighted trace-dual resolution through all simple channels
$z\subset x\otimes y^*$ (or $z\subset x\otimes y$ for the opposite
orientation), including every multiplicity index.  The expanded coefficient
is the product $d_y d_z$.  Move the inclusion around the stationary
component by braiding naturality.  Taking its pivotal mate gives a bijection
between the attaching and detaching fusion bases; sphericality says that
this bijection preserves the trace-dual pairing.  The inverse weighted
resolution then recombines the $y$-sum into the target Kirby colour.  Thus
the source and target sums, including all quantum-dimension coefficients,
are equal.  This is the insertion and sliding calculation of
\cite[Lemmas~3.2--3.4]{BarenzBarrett2018}.  The factors $d_y d_z$ and the
pivotal mate are displayed in Equations~(3.1.3)--(3.1.8), while preservation
of the trace-dual pairing is proved in Equations~(3.1.9)--(3.1.11), with the
pivotal functor specialized to the identity.  The displayed calculation is
the positive band orientation.  Reversing the stationary orientation,
replacing every label by its dual, and using
$\Omega_{\cC}^*=\Omega_{\cC}$ gives the negative orientation; spherical
duality preserves $d_y d_z$ and every multiplicity index.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Character-aware fusion attachment]
\label{lem:character-aware-fusion-attachment}
Let $x$ and $y$ be simple labels and let $a,b\in\mathbb Z$.  Choose, for
every simple summand $z$ and multiplicity index $\mu$, trace-dual maps
\[
  \iota_{z,\mu}\colon z\longrightarrow x\otimes y^{\varepsilon},
  \qquad
  \pi_{z,\mu}\colon x\otimes y^{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow z,
  \qquad
  \pi_{z,\mu}\iota_{z,\nu}
    =\frac{\delta_{\mu\nu}}{d_z}\operatorname{id}_z,
  \qquad
  \sum_{z,\mu}d_z\,\iota_{z,\mu}\pi_{z,\mu}
    =\operatorname{id}_{x\otimes y^{\varepsilon}}.
\]
Here $\varepsilon=-1$ means $y^*$ and $\varepsilon=+1$ means $y$.
The exact source/channel/target data are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|c|c|c|c}
band & attaching channel & target stationary label & target coordinates
  & target character \\
\hline
positive & $z\subset x\otimes y^*$ & $z^*$ & $(a+b,b)$
  & $\eta_x^{a+b}\eta_{z^*}^{b}$ \\
negative & $z\subset x\otimes y$ & $z$ & $(a-b,b)$
  & $\eta_x^{a-b}\eta_z^{b}$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For the positive band, the pivotal mate
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}(z,x\otimes y^*)
  \cong\operatorname{Hom}(y,z^*\otimes x)
\]
is the detaching basis.  Suppressing associators, it sends
$\iota\colon z\to x\otimes y^*$ to
\[
  \iota^\sharp
  :=(\operatorname{id}_{z^*}\otimes\operatorname{id}_x
       \otimes\operatorname{ev}_y)
    (\operatorname{id}_{z^*}\otimes\iota\otimes\operatorname{id}_y)
    (\operatorname{coev}_z\otimes\operatorname{id}_y)
  \colon y\longrightarrow z^*\otimes x.
\]
Thus the first output ribbon at detachment is literally labelled $z^*$; this
is the stationary target strand in the positive convention, while the second
output is the moving $x$-strand.  The negative row is obtained by conjugating the positive slide by
reversal of the auxiliary orientation of the stationary handle, both before
and after the slide.  In the reversed coordinates
\[
  \bar e_y=-e_y,\qquad \bar b=-b,\qquad \bar y=y^*,
\]
the geometric negative slide is the positive slide
$e_x'=e_x+\bar e_y$.  Its attaching channels are
$z\subset x\otimes\bar y^*=x\otimes y$, and the positive calculation gives
the target label $z^*$ with respect to the reversed target orientation.
Reversing that target orientation back changes $z^*$ to $z$ and
$\bar b$ to $b$.  The multiplicity index is transported by the same pivotal
mate.  Thus both rows name the actual oriented target label, not merely an
abstract scalar identity.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The displayed normalization is the spherical trace-dual resolution
\cite[Lemma~2.37, Equation~(2.2.5)]{BarenzBarrett2018}; the factor $d_z$
occurs exactly once.  Naturality and monoidality of $\eta$ give
\[
  (\eta_x^n\otimes\eta_{y^{\varepsilon}}^n)\iota_{z,\mu}
  =\iota_{z,\mu}\eta_z^n
\]
for every $n\in\mathbb Z$.  Since
$\eta_{y^*}=\eta_y^{-1}$, the positive channels satisfy
$\eta_z=\eta_x\eta_y^{-1}$ and therefore
\[
  \eta_x^{a+b}\eta_{z^*}^{b}
  =\eta_x^{a+b}\eta_z^{-b}
  =\eta_x^{a+b}(\eta_x\eta_y^{-1})^{-b}
  =\eta_x^a\eta_y^b.
\]
The negative channels satisfy $\eta_z=\eta_x\eta_y$, so
\[
  \eta_x^{a-b}\eta_z^b
  =\eta_x^{a-b}(\eta_x\eta_y)^b
  =\eta_x^a\eta_y^b.
\]
For the negative row, apply the already proved positive identity after replacing
$(e_y,b,y)$ by $(\bar e_y,\bar b,\bar y)=(-e_y,-b,y^*)$.  Its target character
factor is
\[
  \eta_x^{a+\bar b}\eta_{z^*}^{\bar b}
  =\eta_x^{a-b}\eta_{z^*}^{-b}
  =\eta_x^{a-b}\eta_z^b.
\]
Now reverse the auxiliary target orientation back.  By
\cref{prop:orientation-choice-independence}, this changes neither the RT term
nor its coefficient, and it converts the target label and coordinate to the
negative row of the table.  Spherical duality preserves the trace-dual pairing,
$d_{y^*}=d_y$, and $d_{z^*}=d_z$, so every multiplicity term and the single
$d_z$ factor are carried unchanged.  Multiplying each term of the weighted
identity resolution by the corresponding character equality proves both rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Character-aware $2/2$ slide]
\label{lem:character-aware-two-two}
The marked evaluator is unchanged under either orientation of a $2/2$
slide.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix all labels outside the two participating components.  Write $a$ and $b$
for the moving and stationary character exponents, and expand their Kirby
colours into simple labels $x$ and $y$.  Apply the complete
weighted sliding calculation of
\cref{lem:weighted-kirby-colour-slide}.  It includes every stationary label
$y$, every internal channel $z$, every multiplicity index, and the required
quantum-dimension coefficients.  On each of these summands apply
\cref{lem:character-aware-fusion-attachment}.  The source character factor
then becomes exactly the positive or negative transported factor fixed in
\cref{conv:two-two-coordinates}, without changing any categorical weight.

Move the internal coupon from the attaching collar to the detaching collar.
The route is finite by \cref{prop:local-movie-normal-forms}, and its value is
unchanged by the event-by-event typed rules of
\cref{lem:finite-coupon-transport}; at every dual bend the coupon is replaced
by its mate and its local exponent changes sign.  At a fusion coupon we use
whole-word naturality, not a choice of one outgoing leg.  At detachment,
compose the fusion inclusions with their trace-dual projections.
Orthogonality removes unequal channels and basis indices, and the weighted
completeness relation
$\sum_{z,\mu}d_z\iota_{z,\mu}\pi_{z,\mu}=\operatorname{id}$ recombines the
remaining sum.  The factor $d_z$ here is the same factor already present in
the $d_y d_z$ expansion of \cref{lem:weighted-kirby-colour-slide}; it is not
inserted a second time.  The oriented target identifications and identities in
\cref{lem:character-aware-fusion-attachment} identify the recombined
character factor with the target marking.  Finally sum over $z$, its
multiplicity basis, $x$, $y$, and the fixed exterior labels.  This gives the
source and target state sums, with their full Kirby-colour coefficients, for
the positive and negative slides, respectively.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cochain normal forms for cancelling pairs]
\label{lem:cancellation-cochain-normal-forms}
For an isolated standard $1$--$2$ birth, extension by zero on the new primal
$2$-handle sends a source $2$-cocycle to a target $2$-cocycle representing
the transported class.  Conversely, every target $2$-cocycle is cohomologous
to one whose new $2$-handle coordinate is zero.  The same statement applies
to an isolated $1$--$2$ death after the slide reduction in
\cref{item:cancellation-normal-form}.  For a standard $2$--$3$ birth or
death, every cocycle on the side containing the pair has zero coordinate on
the added $2$-handle.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
After the other incidence entries have been cleared by the $2/2$ slides in
\cref{item:cancellation-normal-form}, the handle cochain complex of the
isolated cancelling $1$--$2$ pair contains a direct
summand
\[
  \mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\,\pm1\,}\mathbb Z
\]
in degrees $1$ and $2$.  Extension by zero is the inclusion of the
complementary cochain complex.  Subtracting the coboundary of the appropriate
multiple of the new degree-$1$ basis cochain makes the new degree-$2$
coordinate zero, without changing its cohomology class.

For a cancelling $2$--$3$ pair the corresponding direct summand lies in
degrees $2$ and $3$, again with differential $\pm1$.  The cocycle equation
in degree $3$ therefore forces the new degree-$2$ coordinate itself to be
zero.  These are precisely the two isolated local cancellation complexes.
We do not claim that an arbitrary, non-isolated presentation already splits
before the slide reduction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Local cancellation sums]
\label{lem:local-cancellation-sums}
For the simple-label set of $\cC$,
\[
  \sum_i d_i\,\operatorname{tr}_i(P_{\mathbf1,i})=1,
  \qquad
  \sum_i d_i\,\operatorname{tr}_i(\operatorname{id}_i)
  =\operatorname{Dim}(\cC).                  \tag{3.7}\label{eq:local-cancellation-sums}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $i$ is simple, then $P_{\mathbf1,i}$ is the identity for
$i\cong\mathbf1$ and zero otherwise.  Since $d_{\mathbf1}=1$, the first sum
is $1$.  Spherical trace gives
$\operatorname{tr}_i(\operatorname{id}_i)=d_i$, so the second sum is
$\sum_i d_i^2=\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Elementary Kirby-move invariance]
\label{thm:elementary-kirby-invariance}
Let
\[
  m\colon(D,\xi_D)\longrightarrow(D',\xi_{D'})
\]
be a marked elementary Kirby event in the sense of
\cref{def:marked-kirby-event}.  Then
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(D,\xi_D)
  =\CY_{\cC,\eta}(D',\xi_{D'}).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We use the local forms of \cref{prop:local-movie-normal-forms}; the exterior
graph and all labels not mentioned below remain fixed.

\emph{Framed isotopy.}
The two graphs represent the same colored framed ribbon graph.  Their
evaluations agree by ribbon graphical coherence
\cite[Theorem~6.1]{Shum1994}.

\emph{$1/1$ and $2/1$ slides.}
These are \cref{lem:dotted-handle-slides}.

\emph{$2/2$ slide.}
This is \cref{lem:character-aware-two-two}.

\emph{$1$--$2$ birth and death.}
For birth, and for death after the explicit $2/2$ slide reduction of
\cref{item:cancellation-normal-form}, the pair is isolated from the exterior.
By \cref{lem:cancellation-cochain-normal-forms}, the standard coordinate
inclusion extends a source cocycle by zero on the new $2$-handle.  Hence its
character coupon is
$\eta^0=\operatorname{id}$.  For each fixed exterior labeling, the new
dotted circle and its ordinary meridian contribute the first sum in
\eqref{eq:local-cancellation-sums}, hence the raw state sum is multiplied by
$1$.  A $1$--$2$ birth does not change $h_3$, so the normalization is also
unchanged.  For death, the preceding $2/2$ slides preserve the marked
evaluator by \cref{lem:character-aware-two-two}; representative independence
then replaces the cocycle by the zero-coordinate representative supplied by
\cref{lem:cancellation-cochain-normal-forms}, and the isolated calculation
is read backwards.

\emph{$2$--$3$ birth and death.}
The cocycle equation in \cref{lem:cancellation-cochain-normal-forms} forces
the exponent on the new ordinary $0$-framed component to be zero, so it
contributes no character factor.  For each fixed exterior labeling its
Kirby-colour sum is the second sum in
\eqref{eq:local-cancellation-sums}; hence the raw state sum is multiplied by
$\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)$.  Birth increases the primal $3$-handle count by
one, and therefore multiplies
$\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)^{1-h_3}$ by
$\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)^{-1}$.  The factors cancel.  For death, first use
the same cocycle equation, rather than a change of representative, and read
the calculation backwards.

These are all eight directed events of
\cref{def:marked-kirby-event}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Manifold-level evaluator]
\label{def:manifold-level-evaluator}
For a marked closed connected oriented smooth four-manifold $(X,\alpha)$, choose a
special Kirby representative $(D,\xi_D)$ as in
\cref{prop:special-representative-existence} and set
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(X,\alpha)
  :=\CY_{\cC,\eta}(D,\xi_D).
\]
The next theorem proves that this scalar is independent of the chosen
special Kirby representative.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Marked diffeomorphism invariance]
\label{prop:diffeomorphism-invariance}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be closed connected oriented smooth four-manifolds, let
$f\colon X\to Y$ be an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism, and let
$\alpha\in H_2(X;\mathbb Z)$.  Then
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\eta}(X,\alpha)
  =\CY_{\cC,\eta}(Y,f_*\alpha).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose special representatives of the two marked manifolds.  By marked
Kirby--Cerf completeness, \cref{prop:marked-kirby-cerf}, they are connected
by a finite sequence of the eight marked events.  Apply
\cref{thm:elementary-kirby-invariance} at each step and induct on the length
of the sequence.  Handle-cohomology naturality and Poincar\'e-duality
naturality identify the two endpoint markings, while representative
independence says that the evaluation on each fixed diagram does not depend
on the cocycle chosen to display its handle-cohomology class.  Thus
\cref{def:manifold-level-evaluator} is independent of every choice and has
the asserted diffeomorphism invariance.
\end{proof}
