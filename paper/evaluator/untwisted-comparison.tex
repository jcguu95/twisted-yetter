\subsection{Comparison with the untwisted Crane--Yetter theory}
\label{subsec:untwisted-comparison}

Here ``untwisted'' means that the character is the identity monoidal natural
automorphism.  It does not mean that the ribbon twist of $\cC$ is trivial.

\begin{theorem}[Untwisted comparison]
\label{thm:untwisted-comparison}
Let $\operatorname{id}$ denote the identity character of $\cC$.  For every
closed oriented smooth four-manifold $X$ and every
$\alpha\in H_2(X;\mathbb Z)$,
\[
  \CY_{\cC,\operatorname{id}}(X,\alpha)
  =Z^{\mathrm{sk}}_{\mathrm{CY},\cC}(X)
  =Z_{\mathrm{CY},\cC}(X),
\]
where $Z^{\mathrm{sk}}_{\mathrm{CY},\cC}$ is Tham's closed skein
Crane--Yetter theory and $Z_{\mathrm{CY},\cC}$ is the original state sum.
In particular, the left-hand side is independent of $\alpha$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the identity character every coupon
$\eta_{\lambda_k}^{a_k}$ is an identity, for every marking and every simple
label.  The remaining local operations agree term by term with Tham's
handle operators: ordinary $2$-handles are expanded with the Kirby colour,
while the turned-over operation corresponding to a primal dotted
$1$-handle is the normalized projection to the tensor-unit channel.  The
normalizing scalar is the same global dimension
$\operatorname{Dim}(\cC)=\sum_i d_i^2$; compare
\cite[Equation~(2.3), Definition~5.11, and the handle calculation on
pp.~99--101]{Tham2021}.  Hence our diagrammatic sum equals Tham's closed
skein evaluation.  Tham's comparison theorem identifies that skein theory
with the original Crane--Yetter state sum
\cite[Theorem~3.61]{Tham2021}.
\end{proof}
